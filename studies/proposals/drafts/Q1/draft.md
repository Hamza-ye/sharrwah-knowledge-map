# Q1: Places a store sells in or operates from, a draft proposal

## 1. How the platforms handle it

### [1]

- Shopify sets up per place in a market, "a set of customers that you define in your store", made of countries or regions, company locations, retail locations or sales channels. A sub-region market "targets a specific state or province", and on it "you can customize only the shipping options". Q1-SH-014, Q1-SH-021, Q1-SH-022
- Salla's market matches Shopify's on the term (basis 1, Salla's own translation counting): its help centre's الأسواق المتعددة copy a store "لكل دولة تستهدفها أو منطقة أو مدينة", and its dashboard text, as one session recorded it on 8 October 2026 (observed), calls them "Markets". Q1-SA-K1976, Q1-SA-037
- Zid's الأسواق manage prices and availability "حسب كل سوق جغرافي بشكل مستقل", matching Salla's on the term (basis 1) and Shopify's on role (basis 4). Q1-ZD-001, Q1-SA-K1976
- BigCommerce runs several storefronts from one store, which "may work for some merchants as a multi-region or cross-border solution": a match with the market on role (basis 4). Q1-BC-004
- WooCommerce's reading showed no part of a store holding its own settings for some of its places: its "Selling location(s)" is one setting for the store. Q1-WC-N01, Q1-WC-K0536
- What a market or storefront can set for itself, matching on role (basis 4): Shopify lists "currency, product availability, theme content, domains, languages, and taxes"; Salla's market page holds currencies, branches and warehouses, shipping, domain, login options and design; a Zid market has a name, countries and "عملة السوق"; BigCommerce's storefronts set domains, themes, currencies, checkout settings and price lists. Q1-SH-K1931, Q1-SA-004, Q1-ZD-004, Q1-BC-005
  - Prices: Salla's are entered per market or follow "سعرها الأساسي وسعر الصرف للعملة"; Zid's are copied from the main market, converted at the current rate, or entered by hand; BigCommerce's price lists set "different prices in the same currency per different storefronts". Q1-SA-019, Q1-ZD-011, Q1-BC-034
  - Tax: on Salla "ستُفعَّل الضريبة حسب الدولة المحددة في السوق، ويشترط أن يكون لكل سوق دولة واحدة فقط"; BigCommerce and WooCommerce set rates by geography, in zones "based on country, state, or postal code" and, in WooCommerce's shipped code, by a rate's "2 digit country code". Q1-SA-023, Q1-BC-129, Q1-WC-024
  - Payment methods: Shopify shows local payment methods on a market's payments card when it "contains a country that is eligible"; Salla lets the merchant "تخصيص طرق الدفع حسب السوق الذي يختاره العميل"; BigCommerce sets them for each transactional currency. Q1-SH-051, Q1-SA-033, Q1-BC-053
  - A legal entity, on Shopify Plus: "A business entity is the legal entity that processes payments for orders in a market", and "Each entity has its own Shopify Payments account, payout currency, and bank account". Q1-SH-041, Q1-SH-K1268
- What stays one for the whole store: on Shopify, "Staff permissions can't be assigned per market", while Salla can restrict a role "بحسب السوق المطلوب"; on BigCommerce, orders arrive in one area with "storefront attribution", and the store's address type and country are "inherently global" (not checked on Zid). WooCommerce's store address, "the location where you are based as a seller", matches BigCommerce's store country on role (basis 4). Q1-SH-035, Q1-SA-028, Q1-BC-003, Q1-BC-018, Q1-WC-001
- Placing a buyer, matching on role (basis 4): Shopify uses the IP address or "a market-specific domain, subdomain, or subfolder"; Salla's buyer picks the market; WooCommerce offers "Geolocate". Q1-SH-060, Q1-SH-061, Q1-SA-032, Q1-WC-005
- Buyers outside the store's places, matching on role (basis 4): on Shopify they "can browse your store, but can't complete a purchase"; Salla's merchant can confine buying "على السوق المطابق لدولة العميل"; WooCommerce's shipped code has an optional zone for "any regions that have not been listed above". Q1-SH-018, Q1-SA-022, Q1-WC-027

### [2]

- Shopify: a market without its own customization "inherits the value from its parent market", and with no parent "the store default is used". Staff reach Markets through permissions such as `CREATE_AND_EDIT_MARKETS` (shipped admin code), and apps through the `read_markets` and `write_markets` scopes. Q1-SH-008, Q1-SH-K0176, Q1-SH-K0862
- BigCommerce: "Each new channel will use the store's global default values unless the merchant chooses to override them", matching Shopify's inheritance on role (basis 4). Q1-BC-017
- Salla: the merchant sets markets in the dashboard under المزيد then الأسواق المتعددة, and their settings under الإعدادات. The store's language setting "ينطبق على السوق الافتراضي فقط", so the default market holds the store's own settings, a partial overlap with Shopify's and BigCommerce's defaults. Its dashboard text (observed, 8 October 2026) says enabling Markets goes through the account manager. Q1-SA-018, Q1-SA-016, Q1-SA-045
- Zid's merchant reaches markets at "الاعدادات > الأسواق". Q1-ZD-K0234
- Lists of places, matching on role (basis 4): in WooCommerce's shipped code, country codes and names "should follow the Unicode CLDR recommendation"; BigCommerce's Geography API gives "countries, states, and provinces". Q1-WC-K1382, Q1-BC-K0405

### [3]

- Who contracts with the seller follows the seller's place in three platforms' terms, matching on role (basis 4): Shopify contracts "based on the billing address of your Store"; BigCommerce's table runs by the merchant's country of residence down to "Any other jurisdiction"; WooCommerce names "WooCommerce Ireland Ltd." for residents of its "Designated Countries". Q1-SH-K1243, Q1-BC-K0425, Q1-WC-K0499
- Salla and Zid keep one set of terms across their countries. Salla's usage agreement, the same at salla.com/terms and salla.com/ar-ae/terms, names شركة تطبيق سلّة لتقنية المعلومات, follows Saudi law and states "كافة الرسوم تحتسب بالريال السعودي". Zid's terms, Zid Pay's for Kuwait included, follow Saudi law, while Zid Egypt "operates officially under Commercial Registration No.:201574". Q1-SA-094, Q1-SA-095, Q1-SA-096, Q1-ZD-K0212, Q1-ZD-K0221
- The platform's own payment product depends on where the seller is based, matching on role (basis 4), Shopify's in its payments terms and the others in help pages: Shopify Payments needs "a business located in your Designated Country"; Zid Pay is "متاح لتجار المملكة العربية السعودية و دولة الكويت"; WooPayments counts the country "where your business is registered — not the one in which you personally live"; BigCommerce Payments needs a store "located in the United Kingdom or the United States"; Salla pays UAE stores "كل يوم خميس" once their balance passes 250 dirhams. Q1-SH-K1536, Q1-ZD-K0005, Q1-WC-K0402, Q1-BC-P0068, Q1-SA-K1547
- What a seller must provide depends on the country, matching on role (basis 4): Salla asks at sign-up for the store's country, "لأن ذلك يحدد الوثائق الحكومية لتهيئة متجرك وفق أنظمتها"; Zid's requirements differ "حسب نوع النشاط التجاري والدولة". Q1-SA-K1989, Q1-ZD-K0240
- The first currency comes from the seller's location, matching on role (basis 4): BigCommerce assigns the default currency "based on your location", and Zid's العملة الأساسية is picked "بناء على عملة دولتك". Q1-BC-060, Q1-ZD-015
- What is offered depends on the seller's place, matching on role (basis 4): where Shopify Payments "isn't available in your country", the seller chooses another provider; WooCommerce's code (shipped) keeps "The payment extension list for each country"; Salla's multiple markets feature "is currently available only in Saudi Arabia". Shopify's admin code (shipped) gates Shopify Balance on `"regions":["US"]`, and one Salla session's feature list (observed, 9 October 2026) gives `kyc_new_workflow` the list `"countries": ["AE", "SA", "KW", "QA", "OM", "BH"]`. Q1-SH-065, Q1-WC-K0054, Q1-SA-036, Q1-SH-K0041, Q1-SA-K1988
- Fees by place, matching on role (basis 4), Shopify's in a help page and the others in their terms: Shopify's currency conversion fee "is either 1.5% or 2%, depending on your store's location"; BigCommerce's "Fees are payable in U.S. Dollars, unless otherwise specified"; Zid's fees are "بالريال السعودي أو بعملة التاجر المحلية". Q1-SH-057, Q1-BC-K0177, Q1-ZD-K0156
- Data: new Shopify merchants in Europe have their data "stored at rest in Europe by default"; which region holds a BigCommerce store's data is a copied gap. Q1-SH-K2321, Q1-BC-G0154
- Yemen, as observed on Shopify: for capture-gb-plus on 5 October 2026, whose business entity address was `"countryCode":"YE"`, Shopify Payments was `"eligible":false` and Stripe `"available":false,"eligible":false`; for fake-demo-sharrwah, with a Sanaa address, the admin listed third-party providers that day, Tap Payments and Cybersource among them. WooCommerce's code (shipped) suggests only `VISA` for store country YE. Q1-SH-094, Q1-SH-098, Q1-SH-095, Q1-SH-103, Q1-WC-K0333

## 2. Proposed: names, structure and behaviour

- **Place register**, proposed: Sharrwah's one list of places: every country and, under it, the areas a setting needs, such as a region, a governorate or a city, each with its parent and a code. Markets, delivery zones, tax rates, addresses and operating places name their places from it. Q1-WC-K1382, Q1-BC-K0405
  - It draws on WooCommerce's country list, which follows a published standard, and BigCommerce's countries, states and provinces, and takes levels below country from Shopify's sub-region markets, Salla's markets for an "منطقة أو مدينة" and BigCommerce's tax zones. Why: places are set up as data (rule 1), behind their own part (rule 2), with levels below country in the data model from the start, since adding them later changes every record that names a place (rule 3). Not taken: countries only, as in WooCommerce's selling locations, since Shopify and BigCommerce set delivery and tax below a country. Q1-WC-K1382, Q1-SH-022, Q1-SA-K1976, Q1-BC-129, Q1-WC-K0536
- **Operating place**, proposed: a place, or a group of places, whose sellers Sharrwah serves, with their set-up: the contracting entity, terms and law; the currency of Sharrwah's fees and their tax; verification and documents; the payment providers offered, in order; the services offered; the default store currency and language; and where store data is kept. One set-up can serve every place not named. Q1-SH-K1243, Q1-BC-K0425
  - It takes the seller's place as the key, with a set-up for all other places, from Shopify's, BigCommerce's and WooCommerce's contracting parties; a per-country set of verification, payments, payouts and carriers from Salla's country articles; and an ordered provider list from WooCommerce's suggestions. Why: a new place is a new set-up, not new code (rule 1); terms per place change most, so they sit behind this part (rule 2); legal structure and partner contracts are expensive to change, so the slots are fixed now and their values left to company decisions (rule 3, section 7). Salla's and Zid's one set of terms is one value it can hold. Q1-WC-K0499, Q1-SA-076, Q1-WC-K0054, Q1-SA-094
  - Its payout slot, for rules such as Salla's per country, serves only an arrangement that holds sellers' money through Sharrwah, which goes live only by the founder's decision. Q1-SA-K1547
- **Seller entity**, proposed: the person or company that sells: its kind, its country of registration (for a person, of residence), its address, documents and bank account. Its country is the store's **home place**, which picks the operating place; the store's own address serves tax and delivery. Q1-WC-K0402, Q1-WC-001
  - It takes the country of registration from WooPayments, the entity held apart from the store from the General settings of the Shopify store capture-gb-plus on 5 October 2026 ("Business entity used for financial products, markets, apps, and taxes in this shop"), and the sign-up country from Salla. Sharrwah verifies it, and it changes only through a controlled change, as Shopify asks a seller to "promptly update your location" and Zid wants an empty wallet before a store changes country, acting on new orders only, as Shopify's entity change "affects only new orders". Why: apart from the store, it lets one seller run several stores, and a market later use another entity, without changing stored records (rule 3). Q1-WC-K0402, Q1-SH-100, Q1-SA-K1989, Q1-SH-K1271, Q1-ZD-K0230, Q1-SH-043
  - Not taken: the seller's country as a store setting alone, like Salla's store country, since BigCommerce's contract follows the merchant's country of residence and WooPayments the business's registration. Q1-SA-K1989, Q1-BC-K0425, Q1-WC-K0402
- **Store currency**, proposed: the one currency a store prices and reports in, defaulting from the operating place; the seller changes it freely until the first order, then only through a change that leaves past orders as they were (rule 3). Q1-SH-K1935, Q1-BC-060
  - It takes the default from BigCommerce and Zid, the lock after orders from Shopify and Salla, and past orders kept from WooCommerce; converting prices at the day's rate, as Zid does, or keeping the numbers, as WooCommerce does, is the seller's choice. Q1-ZD-015, Q1-SH-K1936, Q1-SA-093, Q1-WC-016, Q1-ZD-K0302
- **Market**, proposed: a part of a store that sells to chosen places from the register, with its own values for some settings and the store's defaults for the rest: currencies, prices and products, languages and web address, theme content, payment methods and delivery options, and later tax display and a legal entity. Every store has a **home market** from creation; the seller adds others, each a draft, hidden or active; each order records its market. Q1-SH-K1931, Q1-BC-017, Q1-SH-008
  - It takes store defaults overridden per market from Shopify and BigCommerce; markets made with the store from Shopify and Zid; the hidden state from Zid; and orders that record their market from BigCommerce's storefront attribution. Why: a seller entering a new place adds a market, not a store (rule 1); and with a home market from the start, a second market changes no stored data (rule 3), where Salla's switch-on changes how inventory is deducted and is undone only through the account manager. Q1-SH-027, Q1-ZD-003, Q1-ZD-012, Q1-BC-003, Q1-SA-107
  - Not taken: WooCommerce's store-wide places, since the other four set prices, currencies and content per place; and Salla's default market holding the store's settings, since explicit store defaults let a setting be set once and differ only where a market overrides it. Q1-WC-N01, Q1-SA-016
- **Market selection**, proposed: a buyer is placed by the market's web address, else by choice, else by location, and the seller can confine checkout to the market's places. A buyer in no market gets the store's **fallback**, browsing only or buying in the home market, as the seller chooses, since Shopify blocks such buying while Salla's restriction and WooCommerce's zone are options. The rest is left to the build (rule 3). Q1-SH-061, Q1-SA-032, Q1-SH-018, Q1-SA-022, Q1-WC-027
- **Market currency**, proposed: each of a market's currencies is **charged**, so the buyer pays in it, or **shown**, so prices display in it and the buyer pays in the store currency. A currency is charged only where a connected provider supports it, with its own payment methods. Prices are the seller's own or converted at a rate from the **rate source**, rounded as the seller sets, and each order records its rate. Q1-BC-049, Q1-BC-053, Q1-SH-K1937
  - It takes the two kinds from BigCommerce's display and transactional currencies and Shopify's gateway-bound local currency, and the price modes from Salla, whose prices are set per market or "تلقائياً بناء على سعرها الأساسي وسعر الصرف للعملة", and from Zid's copied, converted or manual prices. Why: what the buyer is charged in decides what the provider must support and what reaches the seller (rule 3); rates come from sources that change, so the rate source is its own part (rule 2). Q1-SA-019, Q1-ZD-011, Q1-SH-057
- **Payment provider**, proposed: a company that takes buyers' payments for a seller, in Sharrwah's **provider catalogue** with its places, currencies and methods. For each operating place Sharrwah sets which providers are offered, in order. The seller makes a **provider connection** with credentials from their own account, after the provider's onboarding, and turns **payment methods** on per market and per charged currency. Q1-SH-065, Q1-SA-K1656, Q1-BC-P0048
  - It takes the offer by the seller's place from Shopify and WooCommerce, the seller's own credentials from Salla and Zid, the provider's underwriting from BigCommerce, methods per market from Shopify and Salla, and per currency from BigCommerce. Why: providers change, so they sit behind one interface (rule 2); and the seller contracts with the provider, which settles under that contract, as Salla's page on Tabby says, so no seller money is held through Sharrwah (rule 3, the guard). Q1-WC-K0054, Q1-ZD-K0094, Q1-SH-051, Q1-SA-033, Q1-SA-089
  - Not taken now: a Sharrwah payment product that holds sellers' money and shuts out other providers, as Salla Payments does Tap, Zid Pay most gateways, and Shopify Payments standalone Stripe. The design keeps room for it (section 5); it, and any payment account opened through Sharrwah, goes live only by the founder's decision. Q1-SA-K1653, Q1-ZD-K0089, Q1-SH-K1528
- **Availability rule**, proposed: for each feature, service and provider, the operating places and plans where Sharrwah offers it, set by Sharrwah and read wherever something is offered (rule 1). It takes the place key from Shopify's shipped `"regions":["US"]` and Salla's observed `kyc_new_workflow` list, and the plan key from Zid's markets per plan. Q1-SH-K0041, Q1-SA-K1988, Q1-ZD-K0235
- **Tax by place** and **delivery zone**, proposed and left to the build (rule 3): tax rates per place, as in BigCommerce's zones and WooCommerce's country codes; delivery zones as sets of places with methods, a market going live only where its places have one, as Shopify requires shipping rates. Q1-BC-129, Q1-WC-024, Q1-SH-034
- **Market access**, proposed and left to the build (rule 3): staff can be limited to a market, as on Salla and unlike Shopify, and apps reach markets through scopes, as Shopify's `read_markets`. Q1-SA-028, Q1-SH-035, Q1-SH-K0862

## 3. What can plug in, what is set per what, and who sets it

- Plugs in: payment providers, through Sharrwah's payments interface after Sharrwah's approval and an agreement, as Shopify approves payments apps, Zid asks for a file per country and WooCommerce for a separate agreement. Q1-SH-K1697, Q1-ZD-K0082, Q1-WC-K0353
- Plugs in: the rate source, as WooPayments' rates are "regularly retrieved from the WooPayments server" and Shopify's can be set by hand; and a location lookup, as WooCommerce's "Geolocate". Q1-WC-K0755, Q1-SH-058, Q1-WC-005
- Set per operating place: all of its set-up (section 2), places sharing one and one serving every place not named. Q1-SH-K1243, Q1-BC-K0425
- Set per seller entity: its country, documents, bank account and payment accounts, as each Shopify entity has its own payments account, payout currency and bank account. Q1-SH-K1268
- Set per store: the store currency and defaults, the plan, and the store's own country, which BigCommerce holds "inherently global" (not checked on Zid). Q1-SH-K1935, Q1-BC-018
- Set per market: its places and the settings it holds for itself (section 2). Q1-SH-K1931, Q1-SA-004
- Set per buyer's place within a market: the tax rate, the delivery zone, and which local payment methods show, as on WooPayments only if "the customer billing country and the customer currency meet the requirements". Q1-WC-032
- Set per charged currency: its payment methods, rate and rounding. Q1-BC-053
- Set per plan: how many markets, and which market settings, as Zid's counts and Shopify's per-market theme on "the Advanced plan or higher". Q1-ZD-K0235, Q1-SH-037
- Set by Sharrwah: the place register, operating places, the provider catalogue and availability rules, as Shopify keeps its Designated Countries in its terms and WooCommerce its suggestions in code. Q1-SH-K1604, Q1-WC-K0054
- Set by the seller: the home place at sign-up, markets, currencies and prices, provider connections, tax rates and delivery zones; on Shopify "Only the store owner can add a payment provider". Q1-SA-K1989, Q1-SH-064
- Set by the seller's staff, by permission, as Shopify's `CREATE_AND_EDIT_MARKETS` and BigCommerce's "the Manage Settings permission". Q1-SH-K0176, Q1-BC-061
- Set by the provider: its places and currencies, its onboarding and its settlement terms. Q1-BC-P0048, Q1-SA-089

## 4. Parts, in the order they can be built

1. **Place register** (A-16): needs nothing.
2. **Operating place**, with Yemen's as the first, and the **availability rule** (A-16, A-19, A-03): need the **place register**.
3. **Seller entity** and **home place**, with sign-up and verification (A-01, A-18): need the **operating place**.
4. **Store currency** and the store's defaults (A-31): need the **seller entity**.
5. **Market**, with the **home market** made with the store and each order recording its market (A-15, A-30, A-33): needs the **place register** and the **store currency**.
6. **Market selection** and the **fallback**: need the **market**.
7. **Market currency** and the **rate source** (A-31): need the **market** and the **store currency**.
8. **Provider catalogue**, **provider connection** and **payment methods** per market and per charged currency (A-11): need the **operating place**, the **seller entity** and the **market currency**.
9. **Tax by place** and **delivery zone** (A-14, A-10): need the **place register** and the **market**.
10. **Market access** for staff and apps (A-01): needs the **market**.
11. Later, and only on the decisions in section 5: a market's own legal entity, a merchant-of-record service, and any arrangement that holds sellers' money through Sharrwah (A-12). Q1-SH-041, Q1-SH-K1958

## 5. What the design must keep open

- Levels below country. The records don't settle which settings vary below a country: Shopify's sub-region markets customize "only the shipping options", while Salla's markets reach an "منطقة أو مدينة" but tax by market needs "دولة واحدة فقط"; in Yemen, facts hold at cities and at areas of control (section 6). Changing the place model after launch would touch every record naming a place. Keep the register's levels open, areas of control among them, and each setting's levels; the design settles them, with a source on what each area covers. Q1-SH-022, Q1-SA-K1976, Q1-SA-023, YK-12 (split @ Sana'a and Aden, as of 2026)
- A zone within one currency. The rial's market price is YER 533 at Sana'a and YER 1,562 buying at Aden on near dates, and a Yemeni provider's API takes the buyer's currency zone. A currency named by its code alone can't tell these apart, and changing its shape after launch would touch every price and order. Keep a zone beside each currency code, and the rate source keyed by place as well as by pair; the design settles it with section 6's areas. YA-01 (YER 533 @ Sana'a, as of 2026-10-08), YA-01 (YER 1,562 buying, 1,567 selling @ Aden, as of 2026-10-10), YK-452 (by currency zone, old, new or all @ Kuraimi Islamic Microfinance Bank's E-Pay service, as of 2026-10-10)
- Who contracts with sellers in each place. Three platforms change the contracting party with the seller's place, and Salla and Zid keep one. Keep the entity, terms and law as values of each operating place; a company decision settles them (section 7). Q1-SH-K1243, Q1-SA-094
- Arrangements that hold sellers' money. Salla pays its UAE stores out on its own schedule, Zid Pay serves merchants in Saudi Arabia and Kuwait, and WooPayments' signup "creates a Stripe Express account that is connected to the WooPayments service". Keep the provider interface able to carry a Sharrwah payment product or an account opened through Sharrwah, and each operating place's payout slot empty; the founder's decision under the guard settles it, with a licence decision (section 7). Q1-SA-K1547, Q1-ZD-K0005, Q1-WC-K0371
- Provider connections by area. In Yemen a provider from one area operates in the other only with separate infrastructure, and each Shopify entity holds its own payments account. Keep a provider connection able to belong to a market or an area, not only to a store; the design settles it with the providers' terms. YK-124 (only with separate infrastructure in the other area @ Aden, as of 2024-04-05), Q1-SH-K1268
- A market's own legal entity. Shopify Plus lets a market use another entity, whose change "affects only new orders". Keep a market able to name a seller entity, and each order recording the entity that sold; sellers' demand and the founder settle when it is built. Q1-SH-041, Q1-SH-043
- A merchant of record. Shopify's Managed Markets lets a seller sell abroad "without registering a business, registering for tax, or arranging customs clearance in each destination", with Global-e as merchant of record. Keep a slot where a market's sales pass to such a partner, under the guard where it holds sellers' money through Sharrwah; a partner decision settles it (section 7). Q1-SH-K1966, Q1-SH-K1958
- The rate source. Where the rate behind Salla's automatic pricing comes from was not in what was read (not checked on Zid); WooPayments takes its rates from its server; Shopify's automatic rates carry a fee that depends on the store's location. Keep the rate source as its own part and each order's rate on the order; a company decision on a rate provider settles it, with section 6. Q1-SA-N02, Q1-WC-K0755, Q1-SH-057
- Where store data is kept. New Shopify merchants in Europe have their data at rest in Europe, which region holds a BigCommerce store's data is a copied gap, and in Yemen a sales-tax registered seller's books and computer systems are to be kept in Yemen. Keep a data location for each store, set at creation from its operating place; a company decision with a lawyer settles it. Q1-SH-K2321, Q1-BC-G0154, YK-290 (regular books, computer systems kept in Yemen @ the unified Republic's area, as of 2005)
- Markets within markets. A Shopify market inherits from a parent, and "Different customizations follow different inheritance rules". Keep a market able to have a parent; the design settles it. Q1-SH-008, Q1-SH-009

## 6. Yemen: what its evidence allows, forbids or doesn't show

- **Place register**: Yemen's evidence gives facts at cities and at areas of control, which allows a register holding both within Yemen, at these places and dates: YK-12 (split @ Sana'a and Aden, as of 2026), YA-15 (old notes only, Aden's new notes barred @ Houthis' area, as of 2026-10-10).
- Yemen's evidence doesn't show which governorates or districts each area of control covers, which the register needs to place a seller's or a buyer's address in an area. A dated source listing each area's governorates and districts would show it.
- **Market** and **rate source**: the rial's market price differs between two cities on near dates, and an international data provider's quote matches neither. This allows markets and rates keyed to places within Yemen, and shows that a rate taken from such a quote differs from both market prices, as of these dates: YA-01 (YER 533 @ Sana'a, as of 2026-10-08), YA-01 (YER 1,562 buying, 1,567 selling @ Aden, as of 2026-10-10), YA-07 (YER 236.197 @ place not stated, as of 2026-10-10).
- **Market currency**, its zone: one provider's payment API takes the buyer's currency zone, and the notes allowed in payment differ by area, so a currency named by its code alone can't carry what this provider asks for (section 5): YK-452 (by currency zone, old, new or all @ Kuraimi Islamic Microfinance Bank's E-Pay service, as of 2026-10-10), YA-15 (old notes only, Aden's new notes barred @ Houthis' area, as of 2026-10-10).
- **Market currency**, charged: the evidence forbids charging domestic sales in a currency other than the rial at the internationally recognized government's area as of 2026-04-24, and allows the rial there: YA-21 (rial only @ the internationally recognized government's area, as of 2026-04-24).
- **Payment provider** and **provider connection**: Stripe was unavailable for Yemen, as it was for the Yemen-address Shopify store capture-gb-plus on 5 October 2026, while a merchant uses Cash Pay's payment API with credentials of its own. This allows an offer for Yemen unlike other places', and a connection by the seller's own credentials, at these services and dates: Q1-SH-095, YA-62 (Unavailable @ Stripe's service, as of 2026-10-10), YA-115 (a username, a service-provider ID and an encrypted password, and a certificate for release @ the Cash e-wallet's Cash Pay service, as of 2024-03-17).
- **Availability rule**: a provider from one area may operate in the other only with separate infrastructure there, and parties in the Houthis' area are barred from dealing with Kuraimi, so for Yemen the rule is set per area. The second fact forbids a connection to Kuraimi for sellers in the Houthis' area as of 2025-06-26: YK-124 (only with separate infrastructure in the other area @ Aden, as of 2024-04-05), YK-369 (Kuraimi Islamic Microfinance Bank barred @ Houthis' area, as of 2025-06-26).
- **Payment provider**, the alternative not taken: e-money and e-payment services are for banks and licensed non-bank institutions at the internationally recognized government's area as of 2026-02, which bears on a Sharrwah payment product, live only by the founder's decision, and on the licence decision in section 7: YA-102 (banks and licensed non-bank institutions @ the internationally recognized government's area, as of 2026-02).
- **Operating place**, where data is kept: a sales-tax registered seller's books and computer systems are to be kept in Yemen at the unified Republic's area as of 2005, which bears on where a Yemeni store's records are kept, open in section 5: YK-290 (regular books, computer systems kept in Yemen @ the unified Republic's area, as of 2005).
