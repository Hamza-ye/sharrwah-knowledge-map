# Review of first decisions 11 to 15, and of the list

- **Wording:** the questions of 11, 12, 14 and 15 assume part of Shopify's answer (the plan decides; one bill; outside developers add screens, logic and data everywhere; fields are marked). The question of 13 is neutral.
- **Citations:** all twelve are real. ISO/IEC 27701:2019 has been replaced by its 2025 edition. Two are not confirmed for what the file says they contain: the governor limits in Weissman & Bobrowski 2009, and the file's paraphrase of Baldwin & Woodard 2009.
- **Claims:** all 151 of the file's quotes are on their lines, and its counts hold. Four claims go further than their evidence: one erasure request reaching everywhere (15); a warehouse post applied to every area (15); limits set per pod (13); apps billing through the platform with no exceptions (12).
- **Timing:** 11, 13 and 15 are needed before areas are designed. 12 is needed only before the first area that charges. 14 could wait: Shopify added extension points to areas that already existed.
- **The list:** Shopify's evidence shows three concerns missing (consistency within a shop, the boundary around card data, retention) and four rules that belong inside existing decisions (search syntax in 6, a request identifier in every log in 1, each area's resiliency matrix in 3, the sale record kept in 9). Betas belong inside 11's check.

**How to read.** A sentence with a pinned link is a fact; the words in straight quotation marks are on the linked line. The file's own words are in curly quotation marks. **Reading:** marks a reading. A count without a quote was made by this review in the linked file. Published sources are listed at the end. 1. **Wording.** Nothing found in the question. Its reading, “every area's operations must carry a cost under one model”, turns Shopify's model into a rule. Shopify's reason for that model, against request counts: "Clients use the same amount of credits regardless, even if they don't need all the data in an API response." ([Rate Limiting GraphQL APIs by Calculating Query Complexity, 2021, L228](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L228)).
2. **Citations.**
   - Homer et al. 2014: real. Its Throttling pattern controls the resources used by an application instance, a tenant or a whole service, as the file says.
   - Krebs, Momm & Kounev 2012: real. *Performance Isolation* is among its keywords. Its body was not read.
   - Weissman & Bobrowski 2009: real, but governor limits were not confirmed in the paper, whose copy answered HTTP 503. Salesforce's Apex documentation and Weissman's QCon 2008 slides do describe them.
3. **Needed before any area?** The model, yes; the numbers can wait.
   - Every request has its own cost, and writes cost more: "A higher cost is necessary to account for this increased server load" ([same post, L228](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L228)).

4. **Claims the evidence doesn't bear out.** The order table says limits are “set per plan and per pod”. Per plan is borne out (L971 above); per pod is not.
   - The cited evidence sets limits per client, "clients receive 50 points per second up to a limit of 1,000 points" ([L228](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L228)), and per store: "the resource-based throttles that apply to large stores" ([shopify.dev front matter, L6780](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/docs-front-matter/shopify.dev@md/items.jsonl?plain=1#L6780)).
   - None of it names a limit per pod.

## 15. Personal data

1. **Wording.** The question, “How does every area mark the personal data it holds”, assumes marking. Shopify describes marking for its warehouse's events and pairs it with a token vault: "there will be nothing left in the data warehouse to delete after removing the mapping from the tokenization vault" ([Schematizing Deletion at Scale, 2021, L231](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L231)).
   - “Erased everywhere at once” goes further than Shopify's erasure does (see point 4).
   - A neutral question: how is a person's request to see, export or erase their data met across every area, apps included?
2. **Citations.**
   - Cavoukian 2009: real. Its principles include privacy as the default setting and privacy embedded into design, as the file says.
   - ISO/IEC 27701:2019: real, but replaced by ISO/IEC 27701:2025 (October 2025), now a standalone standard. The citation should name the 2025 edition.
   - NIST Privacy Framework 1.0 (2020): real. Its *Inventory and Mapping* category covers identifying the data a system processes, as the file says. Version 1.1 was only a public draft (April 2025) in what this review found.
3. **Needed before any area?** Yes, before any area that holds data about people.
   - Shopify's account of its own work is that missing context was the cost: "Lack of guaranteed schemas for events was the root cause of a lot of our challenges." and "Having a solution versus adopting it are two different problems." ([L231](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L231)).
   - **Reading:** the marks are cheapest to add when each area is designed.
4. **Claims the evidence doesn't bear out.**
   - “One request reaches every place the data went” is not borne out by its own source line. The request reaches the store, its apps and its channels, but it is "your responsibility to contact any other companies that you've shared the customer's personal data with", and "Certain information, like what was sold and the date and time of the sale, is still visible in your admin." ([Help Center: Customer data requests, L25](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/help-center/help.shopify.com@en-ar/md/en/manual/privacy-and-security/privacy/processing-customer-data-requests.md?plain=1#L25)). The request also "doesn't erase data Shopify receives as a result of the customer's relationship with Shopify" ([L30](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/help-center/help.shopify.com@en-ar/md/en/manual/privacy-and-security/privacy/processing-customer-data-requests.md?plain=1#L30)).
   - The deletion post is cited for the rule that each area marks its fields as it is designed. The post is about events in the data warehouse: "These events are collected via our streaming platform (Kafka) and stored in our data warehouse" ([L231](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L231)). It bears out marking for those events, not for each area's own records.
