# Q2. The Yemen stress test

**Status:** waits for Q1, the concern map, and for the founder's go after reviewing it.

This question was agreed with the founder on 10 October 2026, together with Q1. It runs against the map that Q1 produces.

## The question, as agreed

> Using only Yemen evidence:
> 1. Identify the axes the market varies along, and build cases on them, with today as the anchor case.
> 2. For each case and each concern on the map, mark one of:
>    - carried unchanged by the product class's placement;
>    - carried with configuration;
>    - carried only with a partner;
>    - no placement.
> 3. Write every "no placement" so it still makes sense with Yemen's name removed. Only those are product gaps; everything else is market setup.
> 4. Record what moves Sharrwah from one case to another: a consultation, a partnership, a licence, a regulator's action.

## Settled when the question was agreed

- **Yemen is a market, not the product.** Product gaps are written without market names. Yemen facts and settings belong to the market layer.
- **The engineering question.** The founder's first wording asked whether Shopify could serve the market "if they wanted to". The agreed question asks instead whether the product class's placements carry each case. That keeps it about architecture, not a company's intentions.
- **Cases follow axes, not one line.** A single worst-to-best line can't describe the market, because several things vary independently. When the question was agreed, these were named as examples:
  - what the law allows;
  - which partners will work with a platform;
  - what the payment rails can do;
  - the currency situation;
  - what can be cut off overnight.

  The axes come from the evidence, not from this list.
- **Moves are not cases.** Consultations and partnerships are recorded as moves between cases.
- **Some things have no product-class precedent.** Examples include a currency circulating in two editions. Concerns the product class never faced are named as design decisions for the founder, not dressed up as classifications.
