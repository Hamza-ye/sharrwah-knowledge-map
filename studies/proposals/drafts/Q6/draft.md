## 2. Proposed: names, structure and behaviour

- **Seller**: in Sharrwah's proposed design, the party Sharrwah contracts with, an individual or an organization, following Shopify's contracting "Store Owner" and Salla's merchant, "سواءً كان شخص طبيعي أو معنوي". It accepts Sharrwah's seller terms on registering and may own several stores, as one Salla owner may. Rule 3: the data model is expensive to change after launch. Q6-SH-K1237; Q6-SA-014; Q6-SA-038
  - **Account user**: the person who signs up and acts in Sharrwah. One who registers for an employer makes the employer the Seller, as on Shopify, and Sharrwah checks the user's phone at sign-up, as Salla does. Rule 3, for the same reason. Q6-SH-003; Q6-SA-002
- **Eligibility rules**: what Sharrwah requires to register, held per place in the **Place set-up**: a minimum age of 18 or the place's age of majority, whichever is older, following Shopify and Zid, which take it from the seller's own place, not Salla's tie to one country's law, since Sharrwah is built once for several places (rule 1); a business purpose, as on Shopify; and Sharrwah's right to refuse, as Shopify keeps. Whether minors may register through a guardian is a company decision (section 7). Q6-SH-001; Q6-ZD-009; Q6-SA-011; Q6-SH-002; Q6-SH-K1238
- **Seller entity**: the registered business, or the individual, that a Seller trades as: its type, its place of registration, its documents and its bank details. The types offered are set per place, since Salla limits its individual type to Saudi citizens and WooCommerce gives UAE businesses none; Salla's and Zid's four shared types are the starting list; for Yemen, the saved markup's text of store ibx5bt-xy's organization step held a company and an individual, on 30 September 2026. A Seller may hold several entities, as Shopify's billing rule speaks of "one of your active business entities" and that step held two; the first build gives each store one, and the data model keeps more (rule 3). Q6-SH-K1260; Q6-SA-026; Q6-ZD-K0239; Q6-WC-006; Q6-SH-045; Q6-SH-K1259
- **Business place**: the place a Seller's business is based, which sets its requirements, its contracting company and the parts open to it. For a registered business it is where the business is registered, following Salla's question, observed for store store-sherawya on 8 October 2026, and WooCommerce's rule; for an individual, where they reside and trade, as Shopify's age rule reads. Sharrwah asks it at sign-up and requires it, as Salla does, offering the places Sharrwah has set up and an option for any other. Rules 1 and 3. Q6-SA-059; Q6-WC-K0402; Q6-SH-001
  - Sharrwah also records the address the seller sells or ships from, which Zid accepts as the location, and a billing address. Neither is the key: a place's documents follow where the business is registered, as Salla's step gives its reason, "لأن ذلك يحدد الوثائق الحكومية", and Shopify itself ties its billing address to an entity's country. Q6-ZD-K0231; Q6-SA-K0083; Q6-SH-K1259
- **Place set-up**: for each place Sharrwah serves, one record of what differs there: whether registration is open; the entity types and the documents each needs; the age of majority; the contracting company and any supplement to the terms; the parts available, with their providers in order; and the wording of notices. This is rule 1, following Salla's country choice that sets legal requirements, in its dashboard strings, Zid's requirements by type and country, Shopify's billing address and WooCommerce's list per country. Q6-SA-008; Q6-ZD-K0240; Q6-SH-013; Q6-WC-K0054
  - A place is a country, or a region within one, since WooCommerce records a state or province and Shopify's restrictions name regions of one country. Section 6 adds what Yemen's evidence shows. Q6-WC-011; Q6-SH-K2275
- **Restricted places**: lists of places from which Sharrwah takes no registration, or serves no part, each naming the regime or provider it comes from and what it bars, as Shopify's bar on creating an account does. The lists sit behind their own part (rule 2), since the platforms' lists differ and Shopify's grounds include commercial partnerships as well as regulation. A provider may bring its own list for its part, as Zid Pay's Kuwait terms name "اليمن"; such a list bars that part, not registering. Which regimes Sharrwah follows is a company decision (section 7). Q6-SH-K1270; Q6-SH-K2270; Q6-WC-K0526; Q6-ZD-035
- **Contracting company map**: which Sharrwah company contracts with a Seller, chosen by its **Business place**, with a row for any other place, and each place's supplement to the seller terms. It follows Shopify, BigCommerce and WooCommerce, which choose the company by the seller's place, and BigCommerce's supplements. It sits behind its own part (rule 2), and its structure is decided now (rule 3: legal structure and partner contracts); with one company, it has one row. Not taken: one company written into the terms, as Salla's and Zid's terms read, since adding a company later would then touch every seller's contract. Q6-SH-K1243; Q6-BC-008; Q6-BC-K0425; Q6-WC-K0498; Q6-BC-010; Q6-SA-023; Q6-ZD-K0214
- **Verification**: checks in stages, each opening particular capabilities, with requirements set per place and entity type in the **Place set-up**. First, the Account user's phone at sign-up. Second, the Seller entity's identity and documents, before Sharrwah activates any part that takes payments or pays out, as Salla's payment data showed `"blocks_payment_activation": true` for store store-sherawya on 8 October 2026, and WooCommerce has sellers verify with Stripe when they sign up for its payments. Third, a provider's own checks when it plugs in. A store can be built and opened before the second stage, as on Zid, where a commercial registration is needed only to connect payment gateways; whether the second stage comes before any selling, as on Salla's "باقة بيسك", is set per place in the **Place set-up** (rule 1). Q6-SA-061; Q6-WC-K0393; Q6-ZD-K0241; Q6-SA-036; Q6-BC-P0048
  - Gating activation, not money: Sharrwah takes no payment for a seller before the second stage, rather than suspending payments and settlement afterwards, as Salla's FAQ describes, or holding funds, as BigCommerce's payment service may. Either would hold sellers' money, and under the guard could go live only by the founder's decision. Rule 3: money flows are decided now. Q6-SA-037; Q6-BC-P0084
  - Who reviews, Sharrwah's own team as on Salla, or the provider as on Zid, stays open (section 5). Q6-SA-K0087; Q6-ZD-K0003
- **Part availability**: for each part Sharrwah offers, its own or a provider's, where it is available: the places, and, where the evidence shows a second key, the currencies, plans and product types; with a reason when a seller can't use it. Zid limits a part by place, BigCommerce adds currency, Shopify plan and product type, and Salla's payment data, observed for store real-merchant on 12 October 2026, carry per method `"available_countries": []` and `"unavailable_reason": null`. Rule 2: availability is data per part, so opening a part in a new place changes no code. Q6-ZD-K0177; Q6-BC-022; Q6-SH-030; Q6-SA-062
- **Provider choice**: for each place, the providers Sharrwah offers there for each kind of part, payments first, in order; the seller picks and connects one, and each provider approves the seller itself. It follows WooCommerce's list per country, Shopify's third-party providers where its own service isn't available, and BigCommerce's application process per provider. Where Sharrwah has no payment service of its own, this list is how a seller takes online payments, as Shopify's admin showed `"thirdPartyDirectEligible":true` for store ts15uk-ug, with its entity in Yemen, on 7 October 2026; two Yemen stores' records name the same six third-party providers, Tap Payments among them. A payment service of Sharrwah's own, as each platform has, is not decided here: section 5 keeps it open under the guard. Q6-WC-K0054; Q6-SH-022; Q6-BC-P0048; Q6-SH-037; Q6-SH-041; Q6-SH-048
  - Two arrangements are possible: the seller contracts with the provider directly, as Salla tells stores outside Saudi Arabia to do with Tabby, or an account is opened through the platform, as WooPayments signup creates a Stripe Express account. Under the guard, an arrangement in which a provider holds sellers' money in an account opened through Sharrwah goes live only by the founder's decision. Q6-SA-056; Q6-WC-K0393
- **Availability notice**: how Sharrwah says something isn't available, built from the **Place set-up** and **Part availability** and never written screen by screen (rule 2): at sign-up, when the Business place isn't set up, a message naming where Sharrwah serves now, as Salla's dashboard strings, captured 8 October 2026, do; in the admin, where the part is used, naming the seller's place, as Salla's bank-transfer string does; and through the API, as Zid's message does. Whether a place not set up gets this notice, or a store with fewer parts as Shopify gave a sign-up from Yemen, stays open (section 5). Q6-SA-007; Q6-SA-058; Q6-ZD-025; Q6-SH-011
  - **Place interest list**: a seller from a place not set up can leave its details and be told when Sharrwah opens there, as Salla's strings promise; the requests count toward which places come next, as WooCommerce counts them. Q6-SA-007; Q6-WC-K0403
- **Change of place**: how a Seller moves its Business place: parts tied to the old place, and above all any money, are closed or settled first, as Shopify and Zid require; then the new place's set-up, contracting company and verification apply. WooCommerce's answer for its payments, no change at all, is not taken, since Sharrwah's places are to grow. Rule 3: money flows and legal structure. Q6-SH-006; Q6-ZD-K0230; Q6-WC-020

## 4. Parts, in the order they can be built

- Place register: the **Place set-up**, with places as countries and the regions within them. Needs nothing before it.
- Restricted lists: the **Restricted places**. Needs the place register.
- Seller records: **Seller**, **Account user**, **Seller entity** and **Business place**. Needs the place register.
- Contracts: the **Contracting company map**, with the terms each **Seller** accepted. Needs the place register and the seller records.
- Sign-up: the flow that applies the **Eligibility rules**, asks the **Business place**, checks the **Restricted places**, and for a place not set up shows the **Availability notice** and offers the **Place interest list**. Needs the four parts above.
- Availability: **Part availability**, and the admin side of the **Availability notice**. Needs the place register.
- **Verification**: its stages, document capture per place and entity type, a review queue, and a hook for outside verifiers. Needs the seller records, sign-up and availability.
- **Provider choice**: the provider interface and the lists per place. Needs availability and verification. A provider arrangement holding sellers' money goes live only by the founder's decision.
- **Change of place**. Needs all the parts above.

## 5. What the design must keep open

- What a place is below the country: WooCommerce records a state or province, and Shopify's restrictions name regions of a country. In Yemen, payment providers from one area may operate in the other only with separate infrastructure there. The design keeps places as a hierarchy, a country and the regions within it, so that a rule can attach at either level; Yemen's areas of control are its first use (section 6), and each place's entry settles the levels it needs. Q6-WC-011; Q6-SH-K2275; YK-124 (only with separate infrastructure in the other area @ Aden, as of 2024-04-05)
- Which address decides what: the place of registration on Salla, observed for store store-sherawya on 8 October 2026, and on WooCommerce, residence on BigCommerce, the billing address on Shopify, the seller's or inventory's location on Zid. The design records each address apart, and each rule names the address it reads; the company decision on contracting, with a lawyer's advice to the founder, settles which address keys the contract. Q6-SA-059; Q6-WC-K0402; Q6-BC-008; Q6-SH-K1243; Q6-ZD-K0231
