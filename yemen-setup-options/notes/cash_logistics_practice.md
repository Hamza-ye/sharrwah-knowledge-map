# Ground truth of practice: how Yemeni online and social commerce collects money (COD, couriers, agents, transfer-plus-screenshot)

Research date: 9 October 2026. Read-only web research. Confidence grades: **H** = primary or official source, or a published study with stated method; **M** = a credible secondary source, or the operator's own listing; **L** = an SEO or directory blog, a search-result snippet only, or anecdote. Staleness is flagged inline. Several key pages (the Jeeey Play listing, the MDPI paper, the Kuraimi Hasib service page) could not be fetched in full, so their findings rest on search snippets and are graded L or M.

Source-quality warning for the writer: much of the Arabic web on "Yemeni delivery companies" and "Yemeni e-commerce apps" is SEO filler. Examples are nano2soft.com (Sept 2025) and aitecnews.com (Sept 2025). They list apps and couriers with no prices, no payment terms and no verifiable details, and some listed names may not exist. I use them only as L-grade evidence that a name exists.

## Q1. Which e-commerce platforms, marketplaces and delivery/ordering apps operate in Yemen, and which payment methods does each offer at checkout?

### Takeaway
Every platform I could verify offers cash on delivery (COD) as the default. The larger ones have added wallet or bank payment at checkout since 2025: Bazzarry (Jaib, Kuraimi "Hasib", Al-Qutaibi bank, its own stored-value wallet, a bank-approved BNPL) and Tawseel One (Mastercard, stored balance, e-wallets for pickup orders). Smaller web stores and social sellers publish a list of wallet numbers and Kuraimi or exchange-company accounts, and ask buyers to send a screenshot on WhatsApp. No regional platform (Noon, Amazon, Talabat, Shein) delivers into Yemen directly. Buyers use intermediaries in Saudi Arabia.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Checkout in Yemen is now a three-tier menu:
  1. COD, which everyone offers.
  2. "Pay to our number" via wallet, Kuraimi or an exchange company, verified by a screenshot. Every store that has no integration uses this.
  3. True in-app integration (Bazzarry with Jaib, Hasib and Qutaibi; Tawseel One with Mastercard and wallets). Only a few large apps have this, and only since 2025 or 2026.
- Integration at Bazzarry was added rail by rail (Hasib, Jaib, then Qutaibi). Each wallet or bank appears to be a separate bilateral integration, with no single gateway. This matches the Sana'a Center's call for a "unified payment gateway".
- The da5m page shows that sellers keep parallel receiving instruments per currency and rail: a YER account, a SAR account, three wallets on one phone, and four exchange houses. This is the de facto "payment setup" a Sharrwah merchant would want to configure.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Jeeey listing or a payment FAQ, so its exact rails are unknown.
- No checkout data was found for Wssy, Etlobni, Talqh or Mostaid.
- Bazzarry's split of payments between COD and electronic is not public.
- No source states fees charged to buyers for COD versus prepaid.

## Q2. How is cash on delivery organised (in-house drivers vs courier companies vs bus/transport between cities), how and when are collected funds handed over to sellers, and what are typical fees and failure/refusal rates?

### Takeaway
COD in Yemen is overwhelmingly **intra-city** and run by the seller's own driver, the platform's own fleet (Bazzarry), or a city delivery app. Stores restrict COD to their own city: "COD inside Sana'a only". Inter-city orders shift to prepayment by transfer, with goods sent by bus or transport offices. **I found no public evidence of any Yemeni courier offering COD remittance with published settlement timing, fees or refusal statistics.** Remittance timing, fees and refusal rates are a documented gap.

### Cited Findings
- **The platform's own fleet.** In Aden and Sana'a, a fleet managed by Bazzarry delivers orders directly, or customers pick up. Bazzarry does not use external logistics companies, "to ensure quality and better oversight". (H) — [Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)
- **Seller COD limited to the home city.**
  - da5m: "COD inside Sana'a" only; other buyers pay by Kuraimi, a wallet or an exchange company (H, one store) — [da5m](https://da5m.com/pages/pay)
  - Aosan: COD and free shipping in Sana'a, and selling only in Sana'a (L) — [aosan.co](https://aosan.co/about-us)
- **Third-party courier with collection.** The Torod Express (طرود) FAQ describes:
  - payment at pickup or on delivery, or by Jeeb, Jawali, Floosak or Kuraimi wallet;
  - "collection fees" listed among possible extra charges, with no amounts;
  - tracking numbers (TRK-XXXXX) with statuses pending, picked up, in transit, at distribution centre (inter-city), delivered and cancelled;
  - a business dashboard with reports, and volume discounts;
  - merchant e-commerce integration "coming soon", with no API described;
  - failed delivery: the driver phones the recipient, the parcel goes back to the nearest distribution centre and the sender arranges a new attempt; returns can incur an extra fee.
  - The FAQ does not state the company's country; its wallet list implies Yemen. It does not describe remitting COD cash to merchants. (M/L) — [torodexpress.com/faq](https://torodexpress.com/faq)
- **Inter-city transport.**
  - A Sana'a transport operator (النقيب بن راجح) advertises carrying "passengers, goods, letters" on Sana'a–Dhamar–Yarim–Ibb–Hawban–Aden, with prices in YER and SAR. Nothing in the advert says whether it collects goods value. (L, snippet of a classifieds listing) — [OpenSooq Yemen transport services](https://ye.opensooq.com/ar/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA/%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%86%D9%82%D9%84-%D9%88-%D8%B4%D8%AD%D9%86)
  - Al-Hajer Al-Aswad (Sana'a, licence 77) advertises inter-governorate delivery and acts as a DHL services agent (L) — [alhajer-alaswad.com](https://alhajer-alaswad.com/blog/%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%83%D8%A9-%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%8A%D9%84-%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AD%D8%A7%D9%81%D8%B8%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%86%D9%8A%D8%A9)
- **Courier lists.** Lists name Tawseel One (Sana'a, Aden, Taiz, Hodeidah, with inter-governorate shipping), Alan Delivery (online-store product shipping, monthly subscriptions), Delivery Yemen (small parcels, Sana'a and Aden), Taiz Express (Taiz to Sana'a), Aden Fast, EasyShip Yemen (tracking app) and others. **No prices, COD terms or remittance terms are given.** (L, SEO) — [aitecnews, Sept 8 2025](https://aitecnews.com/ar/blog/post/afdl-10-shrkat-tosyl-altlbat-fy-alymn)
- **Prices.** A Sana'a listing offers delivery for shop owners and e-commerce stores at "500 riyals" (old-note YER; date unknown) (L, snippet) — [yemenss.com](https://www.yemenss.com/logistics-and-transportation-services/delivery-service/)
- **Infrastructure.** Roads are damaged and fuel short, with no rail network and steep transport costs. Displacement means "customers' whereabouts lack consistency". Bazzarry calls storage, transport and delivery "a daily challenge". Some governorates are "cut off from efficient transportation networks". (H) — [Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)
- **Double customs.** Imports are cleared twice (once in the north, once in the south), so importers pay two prices (M, 2023) — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2023/02/yemens-pursuit-e-commerce-sector)
- **Delivery failures (anecdote).** An Aden seller cites delivery points, sudden store closures, and customers unavailable to receive goods on time (M) — [South24, Apr 2025](https://south24.net/news/newse.php?nid=4590)
- **Refusal at the door (anecdote).** Women sellers report customers rejecting local products that don't match the photo of the foreign product. Goods ordered from foreign sites cannot be returned. Some deliveries were delayed by months, with a Taiz rep waiting three months. (M) — [Al-Mahriah, ~Sept 12 2025](https://almahriah.net/reports/52752)
- **Regional benchmark only, not Yemen data.** Bain notes that across MENA, COD raises return and failed-delivery rates and adds working-capital pressure (M) — [Bain, E-commerce in MENA](https://www.bain.com/contentassets/2b078686303045ffa1d1207130ab5d79/bain_report__ecommerce_in_mena.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The currency split blocks cross-line COD.** Physical cash collected in Aden is new-print rial, which is banned and confiscated in Houthi areas (see Q5). So a courier collecting COD in Aden for a Sana'a seller cannot carry the cash north. It must settle through an exchange company and absorb a large conversion spread. This structurally explains why COD stays within one city or one zone. (Inference, high plausibility.)
- **Who holds the cash.**
  - With own-fleet or own-driver COD, the cash sits with the driver until the end of the shift or a handover. The seller learns the outcome by phone or WhatsApp from the driver.
  - With delivery apps, the rider collects. The platform likely settles with the merchant periodically. No source documents the cycle.
  - (Inference; timing not sourced.)
- **The inter-city pattern.** The probable pattern is: buyer prepays by wallet, Kuraimi or exchange transfer, then the seller hands the parcel to a bus or transport office, then the buyer collects at the destination office. COD collected by bus drivers may exist informally, but I found no source for it. (Inference, medium.)
- **The market gap.** No visible Yemeni courier publishes "COD remittance + statement + API". This looks like an open gap that Sharrwah could fill, either by partnering with couriers such as Torod or Tawseel One, or by building a COD ledger. (Inference, medium.)

### Gaps
- I found no published figures for COD remittance timing (same day, weekly), COD fees (flat or %), cash-handling limits, or refusal and failed-delivery rates in Yemen.
- No source confirms whether bus companies such as Al-Baraq, Rajeh or Al-Ahmadi collect goods value.
- Torod Express's home country and its actual COD-settlement terms are unverified.
- The Sana'a-to-Aden parcel transit time is not documented.

## Q3. How do social sellers collect payment, and how do they confirm a transfer or wallet payment before shipping?

### Takeaway
Social commerce on WhatsApp, Facebook and Instagram is the dominant channel. Sellers use three patterns:
- **COD inside their own city**, to known customers.
- **Full prepayment by transfer**, which is standard for pre-ordered imported goods such as Shein-type items via women "mandoubat" networks. The buyer pays to a Kuraimi account or Hasib point, a wallet number (Jaib, Jawali, Cash, Floosak, OneCash) or an exchange company in the owner's name.
- **Confirmation by screenshot sent on WhatsApp.** The seller then checks the wallet or account balance, or collects at the exchange office.

There is no automated confirmation for social sellers, and fake stores taking prepayment are a documented fraud pattern.

### Cited Findings
- Most adoption of e-commerce happens "through informal channels, such as social media platforms like Facebook and WhatsApp". Many Yemenis "rely on word-of-mouth recommendations and personal networks for online purchases". (H) — [Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)
- An official of the Aden Ministry of Trade (Undersecretary Ali Al-Sharafi) says the sector relies on social media and cash on delivery. Online payment remains limited. There are no official statistics on the number of e-commerce businesses. (M) — [South24, Apr 15 2025](https://south24.net/news/newse.php?nid=4590)
- **Trust through a known clientele.** Rose Marine (a Taiz bakery selling via a Facebook page) reports no problems with delivery or payment, because it sells mainly to a known group of reliable customers (M) — [South24](https://south24.net/news/newse.php?nid=4590)
- **Transfer plus screenshot.** The da5m procedure: pay by Kuraimi (point number or account), by wallet (Jaib, Jawali or Cash) to one phone number, or by exchange company to a named person, then "send a screenshot of the payment receipt via WhatsApp" (H, one store) — [da5m](https://da5m.com/pages/pay)
- **Women's WhatsApp networks.** WhatsApp is the main platform. E-commerce "requires paying the amount in advance", by transfer to the trader's account. The report also describes fraud among traders, customers and mandoubat, and traders absorbing losses from other traders' scams. (M) — [Al-Mahriah, ~Sept 2025](https://almahriah.net/reports/52752)
- **Fraud by fake stores.** Activists warn of fake online stores that take the price of goods plus delivery fees, then disappear and move on to new victims (dated 29 Mar 2026; the article body did not load) (M/L) — [Yemen Eco](https://yemeneco.org/archives/127400)
- **Fraud by fake supplier offers.** A shoe-stall owner sent a large transfer to a scammer behind a fake import offer advertised on a Facebook page (M) — [South24](https://south24.net/news/newse.php?nid=4590)
- **Lack of regulation.** No sector-specific regulation exists, which "exposes customers and sellers to fraud". Neither central bank regulates digital commercial activity. (H) — [Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)
- **Verification tools on the rails.**
  - Floosak requires a one-time password for each transfer and sends an instant notification (L, blog) — [infoalltec](https://infoalltec.com/mahfazat-floosak-alraqmiya/)
  - Jaib offers a transfer-status enquiry (L/M, snippet) — [e-jaib.com](https://e-jaib.com/pages/personal.html)
  - Wallet scams that use phone calls impersonating mobile companies are reported (L, snippet) — same search; [Jaib news](https://e-jaib.com/pages/personal.html)
- **Advice on transfers to unknown sellers.** Forum advice is to transfer to a business, not a personal name, state the purpose, keep screenshots and chats, and test with a small amount first (L; old, non-Yemen-specific forum) — [adslgate](https://www.adslgate.com/thread/2208401)

### Inferences
- **Proof and outcome.** The buyer produces the proof (a screenshot or exchange receipt). The seller establishes the outcome by:
  - opening their own wallet or bank app to see the credit, for wallets and Kuraimi;
  - physically collecting at the exchange office with ID and the transfer number, for hawala to a named person.
- A screenshot proves nothing by itself; sellers who skip the balance check are exposed to edited screenshots. I found no Yemen-specific report of fake-screenshot fraud, so this is an inference from the mechanics and common in the region.
- **No reversal path.** Wallet-to-wallet and exchange transfers are final. Disputes are settled bilaterally by the seller refunding, or not at all. No source documents a wallet chargeback or dispute mechanism.
- **Prepay versus COD depends on who bears the risk.** Prepayment is demanded where the seller must buy stock abroad first (Shein-type pre-orders) or ship inter-city. COD is offered where the seller has local stock and a driver.

### Gaps
- No source documented a partial deposit (عربون) as standard practice.
- No source gave the share of social sellers using each rail.
- No report specifically on fake transfer screenshots in Yemen was found.
- No Telegram-specific evidence was found.
- It is undocumented whether wallets (Jaib, Floosak, OneCash, Jawali) offer merchants an instant-notification or webhook product usable by social sellers.

## Q4. Do any courier or logistics firms offer COD remittance as a service with tracking and statements? Do any provide APIs?

### Takeaway
**No verified Yemeni courier publicly offers COD remittance with statements and an API.** The closest is Torod Express:
- shipment tracking;
- a business dashboard with "reports and statistics";
- "collection fees" mentioned;
- wallet payment (Jeeb, Jawali, Floosak, Kuraimi);
- merchant e-commerce integration "coming soon", with no API.

The largest platform, Bazzarry, deliberately runs its own fleet. On the payment side, integration exists bilaterally through Kuraimi Hasib, Jaib and Al-Qutaibi bank, as Bazzarry shows. No courier-side API was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Torod Express.** Tracking IDs and statuses, a business dashboard, volume discounts, "collection fees" among extra charges, return fees, and merchant integration "coming soon" via info@torodexpress.com. No API and no remittance terms are given. The country is not stated. (M/L) — [torodexpress.com/faq](https://torodexpress.com/faq)
- **Bazzarry** does not use external logistics companies (H) — [Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)
- **Wssy (Aden)** advertises live order and captain tracking for consumers, not a merchant API (L) — [Wssy App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%8A-%D8%AA%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%8A%D9%84-%D8%B7%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%85-%D9%88%D8%A3%D9%83%D8%AB%D8%B1-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%86/id6751149035)
- **EasyShip Yemen** is listed with phone-app tracking. No API is mentioned and the source is low quality. (L) — [aitecnews](https://aitecnews.com/ar/blog/post/afdl-10-shrkat-tosyl-altlbat-fy-alymn)
- **Kuraimi Hasib** e-commerce acceptance requires an app or site, a physical address, an account in the store's name and signed agreements. This is the closest thing to a merchant acquiring onboarding, but it is a payment rail, not logistics. (L/M) — [Kuraimi Bank](https://kuraimibank.com/ar)
- **Unified gateway.** A mobile network operator's digital-transformation manager says a unified payment gateway would make e-commerce transactions "significantly easier". This implies none exists. (H) — [Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)
- **Regional comparator.** Gulf couriers advertise COD collection with remittance within 24 hours (e.g. Alreem in the UAE). This is a template that does not exist in Yemen as far as I found. (L) — [alreem.delivery](https://alreem.delivery/)

### Inferences
- A Sharrwah "COD setup option" would initially have to rely on:
  - the merchant's own driver plus a manual "mark as paid", or
  - a partner courier that reports outcomes by phone or WhatsApp and settles by wallet or Kuraimi transfer.
- Any statement or reconciliation would have to be built by Sharrwah: a COD ledger per courier, with expected versus remitted amounts.
- Tawseel One (seven cities, stored balance, wallets, Mastercard) and Torod (tracking, business dashboard, wallet payments) are the most plausible integration partners from public evidence. Both would need direct confirmation.

### Gaps
- No courier publishes COD settlement frequency, holdback or float, insurance or caps.
- No public developer documentation was found for any Yemeni courier.
- I could not verify the existence or scale of Alan Delivery, Delivery Yemen, EasyShip Yemen and others beyond SEO lists.

## Q5. What do buyers prefer and distrust, and does it differ between Sana'a-controlled and government-controlled areas or between cities?

### Takeaway
Buyers strongly prefer COD. The only platform survey found, in Aden, gives 74% COD preference. COD is driven by distrust of online payment and of unknown sellers, and by low account ownership. Digital payment use is tiny in the latest Findex data. The north–south split matters in practice:
- **Sana'a-area sellers price and accept only old-print rial** (or SAR and USD).
- New notes are banned in Houthi areas.
- The two e-money models differ: non-bank wallets in Houthi areas, bank-led in government areas.
- Cross-line transfers lose heavily to the exchange-rate gap.

COD and seller networks are therefore effectively zone-local.

### Cited Findings
- **Survey in Aden.** In a survey of Bazzarry users in Aden, 74.44% preferred COD and 25.56% electronic payment. The sample was 180, a convenience sample that was 77% female and mostly aged 18–28 and students; 54% buy "a few times per year". The study was published 31 Aug 2026. (M; a small non-random sample and a low-tier journal) — [Taleb, Sattar et al., UKR Journal of Economics, Business and Management 2(8), 2026](https://ukrpublisher.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/The-Impact-of-Determinants-of-Customer-Buying-Behavior-on-the-Market-Performance-of-E-Commerce-Platform.-A-case-stud-on-BAZARRY-e-commerce-company-Aden-Yemen.pdf)
- **Share paid in cash (fragile figure).** "61.9 percent" of online purchases were paid in cash. The brief's own footnote says the Findex item fin14c rests on "only 979 cases with only 21 valid responses". Treat it as near-anecdotal. (H source, statistically weak) — [Sana'a Center 2025, fn 16](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)
- **Findex 2021 (fieldwork 2022–23).** 96.2% of respondents had not used a phone to pay in-store in the past 12 months, and 98.9% had made no digital payment to a merchant. 12% held accounts in 2022, against 48% in MENA. (H; stale data) — [Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)
- **Business payments (World Bank PID, Jan 2025).** 97.2% of firms pay salaries in cash. 68.1% of suppliers are paid in cash, 20.6% through cash transfers via money exchange companies, and 7.6% through formal banking. (H) — [Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)
- **E-wallet footprint.**
  - 807,919 e-wallet accounts in Dec 2019. 93.8% of wallets are in cities. Many were opened only to obtain old notes after the 2019 Houthi ban on new notes.
  - One Cash (HSA Group, licensed by CBY-Sana'a in 2022): 200,000 active customers, more than 14,000 retail outlets, 800 corporations accepting, and 3.2M transactions worth US$1.6B by Dec 2023 (Forbes ME, Mar 2025).
  - (H/M) — [Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)
- **Distrust of online payment.** It ranked as the third most important factor (weight 0.115) affecting Yemeni e-commerce startups in a grey-AHP expert study, which recommends COD alternatives (M; 2023; snippet only, the page returned 403) — [MDPI Sustainability 15(18):13712](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/18/13712)
- **Two e-money models.** The non-bank model operates in Houthi areas, under CBY-Sana'a Decision No. 1/2020, and the bank-led model in government areas, under CBY-Aden. After the US FTO redesignation of the Houthis (4 Mar 2025), banks moved their headquarters to government areas. (H) — [Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)
- **Currency split.**
  - The Houthis banned post-2016 notes in 2020. New notes are reportedly confiscated or torn if travellers carry them north.
  - The USD reached about 2,830 YER in Aden in July 2025.
  - Example of the loss: a 10,000 new-rial wage in Aden is worth about 1,000 rial when sent to Sana'a.
  - People convert savings to USD or SAR to avoid transfer losses.
  - The Aden central bank called Sana'a's new 50-rial coin and 200-rial note "counterfeit".
  - (H/M) — [Al Jazeera Net, Jul 19 2025 (updated Sept 21 2025)](https://www.aljazeera.net/ebusiness/2025/7/19/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%86-%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%86%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%86%D9%82%D8%B3%D9%85-%D9%8A%D9%86%D9%87%D9%83)
- **Transfer commissions.** Commissions on transfers between the zones have exceeded 29% of the amount, because exchangers recover the old/new note spread. (L/M; publication date unclear, possibly before 2025) — [Al-Mawqea Post](https://almawqeapost.net/reports/53255)
- **Exchange-rate trackers.** Trackers later show about 1,558 YER/USD in Aden against about 530 in Sana'a, which implies the Aden rate partly recovered after mid-2025. (L; aggregator snippet, date unclear) — [exrye.com](https://exrye.com/currency-converter); [Radar, Dec 26 2025](https://radar2.net/806330/)
- **Seller practice by zone.** A Sana'a store accepts "old currency only" (H) — [da5m](https://da5m.com/pages/pay). An importer-seller deals only in SAR (M) — [South24](https://south24.net/news/newse.php?nid=4590).
- **Where the customers are.** E-commerce customers are concentrated in Sana'a, Taiz, Ibb and Aden. Urban women are among the most engaged sellers and buyers. Rural uptake is limited by digital literacy and access. (H) — [Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf). Sana'a has the most e-commerce businesses, with Aden and Taiz close behind (M, 2023) — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2023/02/yemens-pursuit-e-commerce-sector)
- **Rate volatility suppresses buying.** Rate swings make customers delay purchases. A fall in the exchange rate led to buyers waiting for further falls and to stagnation. (M) — [South24](https://south24.net/news/newse.php?nid=4590); [Al-Mahriah](https://almahriah.net/reports/52752)
- **Internet access** was 17.7% in 2024 (H) — [Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)

### Inferences
- Any Sharrwah setup option must carry **currency and zone attributes**: YER-old (north), YER-new (south), SAR and USD. COD and wallet rails should be configured per zone. The rails split as follows:
  - Wallets licensed by CBY-Sana'a under the non-bank model (e.g. One Cash, Jawali, Jaib, Floosak in practice) operate in the north.
  - The bank-led model operates in the south.
  - Kuraimi and exchange companies straddle both zones.
  - Exact licensing of each wallet in each zone needs confirmation by the wallet-rails researcher.
- Buyer preference for COD is likely stronger in the south, where volatility makes prepaying riskier, and among first-time buyers of unknown sellers. Repeat buyers of a known seller accept prepayment. This is inferred from the trust-via-known-customers pattern; there is no zone-split survey.
- SAR pricing is used for imported goods in both zones as a hedge, which helps explain why da5m keeps a Kuraimi SAR account.

### Gaps
- No survey compares buyer payment preference between Sana'a-controlled and government areas, or between cities.
- No current (2026) figure for inter-zone transfer commission was found from a dated source.
- I did not find the Findex 2024/2025 update for Yemen, if one exists.
- No data on how wallet use for e-commerce differs by zone.
