# Setting up your Shopify Payments account

To get started with Shopify Payments, first check the [list of supported countries](/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/index) to confirm availability in your region. After confirming your eligibility, you can activate the service and complete the verification process. 

Your Shopify Payments payouts might be placed on hold until [two-step authentication](/manual/your-account/logging-in/secure-sign-in) is activated on your store. Two-step authentication helps to prevent security breaches that can lead to misdirected payouts or other financial loss.

You need to choose your [store currency](/manual/intro-to-shopify/initial-setup/setup-business-settings#select-a-store-currency) before making your first sale. It’s the currency that will be used throughout your Shopify admin, for pricing your products and viewing your reports. If you need to change your store currency after making your first sale, then contact Shopify Support.

## Activating Shopify Payments {#set-up-shopify-payments}

You can activate Shopify Payments as your payment gateway from the [**Payments**](https://admin.shopify.com/payments) section of your Shopify admin. If you don't have a payment gateway set up to accept credit and debit cards at checkout, then you can set up Shopify Payments to begin selling. If you have a third-party payment gateway set up, then you can switch to Shopify Payments, or you can [use Shopify Payments alongside your existing provider](/manual/payments/shopify-payments/using-shopify-payments-with-a-third-party-provider) to offer additional payment methods and recover failed transactions.

## Activate local payment methods {#local-payment-methods}

Some local payment methods for Shopify Payments require additional approval before they can be activated. Once you activate Shopify Payments, the review process for applicable [local payment methods](/manual/payments/shopify-payments/local-payment-methods) begins automatically and approved payment methods will become available for you to activate in the payment methods page. 

## Information requirements for Shopify Payments {#documents}

Shopify and its banking partners are required to collect and verify information about you and your business, to comply with regulations that are designed to prevent financial crimes. As local rules and regulations evolve, you might be contacted by Shopify and its banking partners to provide additional information throughout the lifespan of your Shopify Payments account.

The information that is collected is used to confirm the identity of the business, the identities of individuals linked to it, and the nature of the relationship between the two.

Shopify never physically mails documentation to you. Any important documents or communications are emailed, or made digitally available to the person listed as Account Owner of the store in your Shopify admin.

## Uploading documents for Shopify Payments verification {#required-documentation}

<aside class="note"><h4>Note</h4><p>Once documentation is uploaded, it can take up to 24 hours to be reviewed. Your Shopify Payments account status will show the submission as &quot;pending&quot; until a response is received.</p>
</aside>

Due to the sensitive nature of the personal information that's requested, Shopify can only accept documents that are uploaded through the secure banners in your Shopify admin. For your security and privacy, any documents sent through email or support chat are redacted and rendered unusable.

To complete the verification process, ensure that any documentation you submit meets the following requirements:

* The documentation is photographed instead of scanned, copied, or screenshotted. Ensure photographs are in color and not grayscale.
    * For documents that are issued to you digitally, you must provide the original PDF  without any alterations, and with all original pages included.
* The documentation is clear enough to read, with all four borders of the document in the frame.
* The documentation is complete with all details visible and nothing censored, blurred, or cropped out.
* The information on your documentation matches the information that you entered on your Shopify Payments account.

![Instructional graphic showing photo ID requirements: a clear, full-color US passport image with all four borders visible is marked with a green check. Below it there are examples of bad photographs: One is too blurry, one is black-and-white, one has the left side of the document cropped out, and one has been altered to include a watermark.](/manual/finances/kyc-document-guidance.png)

You can submit photo ID in PNG or JPEG format. Proof of address and business documents can additionally be in PDF format. Refer to [your country’s specific page](/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/index) to learn which types of documents are accepted in your region.

Your Shopify Payments payouts might be placed [on hold](/manual/payments/shopify-payments/payouts/account-holds) until your information and documents can be verified. If documentation is required, then an alert is sent to notify you and take you to the upload page where you can provide new information. 

If you receive a notification that your information wasn't verified, then you need to review the reasons provided on the upload page and take further action, such as uploading new documentation or updating your account information to match the uploaded documentation.

When you upload documents for Shopify Payments verification or you try to update your Shopify Payments details, you might receive an error message indicating that you've reached the maximum number of attempts. If this happens, then you can contact Shopify Support to make further changes to your details or to submit additional documents.

### Common reasons a document isn't verified {#document-not-verified-reasons}

If a document isn't verified, then the upload page states the reason. The most common reasons are the following:

* **The document is cropped or incomplete.** Upload a new photo that shows the whole document, with all four borders in the frame and no details cut off.
* **The document is a scan or a copy, or it isn't readable.** Photograph the original document in color instead of submitting a scan, a photocopy, or a screenshot, and make sure that every detail is in focus.
* **The document appears to be altered.** Upload the document without watermarks, annotations, or redactions, and don't censor or blur any details.
* **A name or a date of birth doesn't match your account.** This reason applies to the name of a person and to the legal name of your business. For a person, enter their name and date of birth exactly as they're displayed on their photo ID. For your business, enter the official registered name.
* **Your business details don't match government records.** Check that the legal business name and the registration number on your account are the ones that are registered with your government. Requirements are different in each region, so [check your region's requirements page](/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/index).
* **An address doesn't match your account.** The reason names the person or the business whose address doesn't match. Correct that address on your account, or upload a document that matches the address that's already on your account.

Your account details and your documents must match. Depending on the reason, you can correct the details on your account, or you can upload a document that matches your account. Before you change a business address, review the address requirements for Shopify Payments.

### The account representative {#account-representative}

Every Shopify Payments account must list one individual as the account representative, even when the business is a registered company or is owned by another company. Shopify's banking partners require identity information for a real person on every account, so you can't list a company as the account representative.

The person you list depends on your business type:

* **Individuals and sole proprietorships**: The person who owns and operates the business.
* **All other business types**: An owner or a senior executive who has the authority to legally bind the business to the [Shopify Payments Terms of Service](https://www.shopify.com/legal).

Who qualifies as an account representative may vary slightly depending on your region. [Check your region's specific requirements page for more information.](/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/index)

The account representative doesn't need to be the store owner, and doesn't take on operational responsibility for managing the store. They provide and verify their personal information, which may involve providing documentation.

Listing someone as the account representative doesn't report them as a beneficial owner. Ownership is recorded in a separate field on the account, and only applies if the person is simultaneously given the **Owner** role.

Businesses where no individual owns 25% or more, such as publicly traded companies, government entities, and non-profits, still need to name an account representative. This requirement comes from banking regulations and can't be waived.

### Understanding the terms Owner, Director and Executive

For most regions, owners are defined as any individual person who directly or indirectly (through ownership in a parent or holding company) owns 25% or more of the business’s equity. If there are individuals who own equity of your business through their ownership of the holding company, then you must list them as beneficial owners. [See your country’s specific page for more guidance](/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries/index) on who is required to be listed on your account, and who qualifies as a beneficial owner.

Directors are individuals who are members of the governing board of your company, and are typically shown on most business registration extracts. 

Executives are individuals who have a significant control over the business and are responsible for managing and directing the business. They might include executive officers or senior managers, such as, but not limited to, a Chief Executive Officer (CEO) or a Chief Financial Officer (CFO), Chief Operating Officer (COO), Managing Member, General Partner, President, Vice President, or Treasurer.

### Address Requirements

You can't use PO Box or Private Mail Box addresses with Shopify Payments. 

If your business is based in the United States, and you want to use [Shopify Balance](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/finance/shopify-balance/eligibility#shopify-balance-account-requirements) or [Shopify Credit](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/finance/shopify-credit/getting-started#eligibility), then you're unable to use virtual addresses, mail forwarding services, co-working spaces, registering agent addresses, or similar addresses which your business might use on documentation, but doesn't physically occupy. To use these products and services, you must provide the real home addresses for the people on file, and a business address where your business operations can be physically located.

### Name requirements

Everyone listed on your Shopify Payments account should provide the full legal name that is displayed on their government-issued photo ID or documentation. Don't use nicknames, abbreviations, aliases, or placeholders, such as Admin or Manager. Don't use numeric characters or asterisks in a name.

When you submit documentation for a person, ensure that the documents display the person's full legal name as registered with their government. Don't submit documents that use abbreviations, such as J. Jones.
