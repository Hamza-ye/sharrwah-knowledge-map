# Q6: Who can register as a seller, and from where, a draft proposal

## 1. How the platforms handle it

### [1]

- Registering: all five require an account and accurate details, a match on role. Shopify and BigCommerce use the same term, "Account" (basis 1). Shopify asks for full legal name, business address, phone number and email; Zid for the name as it appears in official papers, the current address, a mobile number and an email; BigCommerce for "true, accurate, current, and complete information". Salla sends a code to the phone to check the number, and WooCommerce may limit access until it can verify account information, "like your email address". Q6-SH-K1238; Q6-BC-003; Q6-ZD-010; Q6-SA-002; Q6-WC-024
- Refusal: Shopify may reject an application "for any reason, at our sole discretion", WooCommerce may deny access "to any individual or entity for any reason", and Zid states "يحق لزد رفض طلب إنشاء أي حساب": a match on role, with no ground required. Salla may refuse a store that does not comply with Saudi law or its agreement, and BigCommerce accounts that violate its Agreement or Acceptable Use Policy: a partial overlap, since both tie refusal to a breach. Q6-SH-K1238; Q6-WC-027; Q6-ZD-011; Q6-SA-016; Q6-BC-004
- Age: Shopify takes 18 or "the age of majority in the jurisdiction where you reside", whichever is older, and Zid 18 or the age of majority under the rules of "الدولة التي تستخدم منها الخدمة": a match on role, both taking the age from the seller's own place. Salla sets eighteen "كما هو معتبر في القوانين والأنظمة المرعية بالمملكة العربية السعودية", one country's law. BigCommerce requires "the age of majority", and its services are "not intended to be used by individuals under the age of 18". Q6-SH-001; Q6-ZD-009; Q6-SA-011; Q6-BC-001
- Minors: Shopify has a parent or guardian set up the account with their own details, Salla lets a minor trade with proof of the guardian's consent, and WooCommerce allows use under 18 only under a parent's or guardian's supervision: a match on role. Q6-SH-004; Q6-SA-012; Q6-WC-026
- Persons and purpose: Salla's merchant is whoever registers to create a store, "سواءً كان شخص طبيعي أو معنوي", and Shopify's business entity is "the registered business, or the individual, that your store operates as": a match on role. Salla and Zid both require an establishment or company that registers to have legal capacity itself, in near-identical clauses. Shopify requires "a business activity and not for any personal, household or family purpose", and WooCommerce use "that of a representative of a business": a match on role. Q6-SA-014; Q6-SH-K1260; Q6-SA-013; Q6-ZD-K0242; Q6-SH-002; Q6-WC-K0501
- The contracting party: on Shopify the person who opens the account is the contracting party, the "Store Owner"; someone signing up for an employer makes the employer the Store Owner, and a Store Owner "may have multiple Shopify Stores". BigCommerce lets an agent register for a "Client" when it has "the authority as agent for Client to bind Client to the Agreement": a match on role. On Salla, "لا يوجد حد أعلى لعدد المتاجر التابعة لمالك واحد". Q6-SH-K1237; Q6-SH-003; Q6-SH-K1240; Q6-BC-K0469; Q6-SA-038
- Entity types: Salla and Zid name the same four types in the same words, a match on term: Salla's "فرداَ, مؤسسة, شركة, أو جمعية خيرية" and Zid's "فرد، مؤسسة، شركة، جمعية خيرية". For country code YE, Shopify's admin offered "Individual", "Company" and "Non-Profit", observed for store ctcbwa-r1 on 30 September 2026: a partial overlap on role, with no type for an establishment. Q6-SA-026; Q6-ZD-K0239; Q6-SH-K2674; Q6-SH-K2675; Q6-SH-K2676
- Types by place: Salla's Saudi verification gives the individual type, with a freelance document, "للمواطنين السعوديين فقط", and an establishment or company, with a commercial registration, "للمواطنين والمقيمين"; WooCommerce states "Businesses in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) do not have access to the Individual account type." Q6-SA-026; Q6-WC-006
- Where the seller says it is: four of the five ask, a match on role, each in a help page: Shopify's step "Specify your business location", Salla's "حدد من أي دولة تسجل متجرك", Zid's "تحديد موقعك أو موقع المخزون", and WooCommerce's store address, "the location where you are based as a seller". BigCommerce's sign-up asks only "Where should your store be hosted?", and a field for the seller's own country was not in what was read. ## 2. Proposed: names, structure and behaviour

- **Seller**: in Sharrwah's proposed design, the party Sharrwah contracts with, an individual or an organization; it accepts Sharrwah's seller terms on registering, and may own several stores. It draws on Shopify's contracting "Store Owner", who "may have multiple Shopify Stores", and on Salla's merchant, "سواءً كان شخص طبيعي أو معنوي". Rule 3: the data model is expensive to change after launch. Q6-SH-K1237; Q6-SH-K1240; Q6-SA-014
  - **Account user**: the person who signs up and acts in Sharrwah. Someone registering for an employer or a client makes that party the Seller, as on Shopify and BigCommerce. Sharrwah checks the user's phone or email at sign-up, as Salla and WooCommerce do. Rule 3, for the same reason. Q6-SH-003; Q6-BC-K0469; Q6-SA-002; Q6-WC-024
- **Eligibility rules**: what Sharrwah requires to register, held per place in the **Place set-up**: a minimum age of 18 or the place's age of majority, whichever is older, following Shopify and Zid, which take it from the seller's own place, and not Salla's tie to one country's law, since Sharrwah is built once for several places (rule 1); a business purpose, following Shopify and WooCommerce; and Sharrwah's right to refuse, which all five keep. Whether minors may register through a guardian is a company decision (section 7). Q6-SH-001; Q6-ZD-009; Q6-SA-011; Q6-SH-002; Q6-WC-K0501; Q6-SH-K1238
- **Seller entity**: the registered business, or the individual, that a Seller trades as: its type, its place of registration, its documents and its bank details. The types offered are set per place, since Salla limits its individual type to Saudi citizens and WooCommerce gives UAE businesses none; Salla's and Zid's four shared types are the starting list, and the types Shopify's admin offered for Yemen are the nearest record for Yemen. A Seller may hold several entities, as Shopify lets a seller add one for another country; the first build gives each store one, and the data model keeps more (rule 3). Selling through several at once, which Shopify offers only with its own payment service on two plans, waits (rule 4). Q6-SH-K1260; Q6-SA-026; Q6-ZD-K0239; Q6-WC-006; Q6-SH-K2674; Q6-SH-K2675; Q6-SH-K2676; Q6-SH-015; Q6-SH-K1265
- **Business place**: the place a Seller's business is based, which sets its requirements, its contracting company and the parts open to it. For a registered business it is where the business is registered, following Salla's question and WooCommerce's rule; for an individual, where they live and trade, as Shopify's age rule reads. Sharrwah asks it at sign-up and requires it, as Salla does, offering the places Sharrwah has set up and an option for any other. Rules 1 and 3. Q6-SA-059; Q6-WC-K0402; Q6-SH-001
  - Sharrwah also records the address the seller sells or ships from, which Zid accepts as the location, and a billing address. Not taken as the key: Zid's seller-or-inventory location, because a place's documents follow where the business is registered, as Salla's step gives its reason, "لأن ذلك يحدد الوثائق الحكومية"; and Shopify's billing address, which Shopify itself ties to an entity's country. Q6-ZD-K0231; Q6-SA-K0083; Q6-SH-K1259
- **Place set-up**: for each place Sharrwah serves, one record of what differs there: whether registration is open; the entity types and the documents each needs; the age of majority; the contracting company and any supplement to the terms; the parts available, with their providers in order; the billing methods and currency for Sharrwah's own fees; and the wording of notices. This is rule 1, built once and set up per place, following Salla's country choice that sets legal requirements, Zid's requirements by type and country, Shopify's billing address and WooCommerce's list per country. Q6-SA-008; Q6-ZD-K0240; Q6-SH-013; Q6-WC-K0054
  - A place is a country, or a region within one, since WooCommerce records a state or province and Shopify's restrictions name regions of one country. Section 6 says what Yemen's evidence adds. Q6-WC-011; Q6-SH-K2275
- **Restricted places**: lists of places from which Sharrwah takes no registration, or serves no part, each naming the regime or provider it comes from and what it bars: opening an account, reaching storefronts and accounts, or orders shipping there, as Shopify's restrictions do. The lists sit behind their own part (rule 2), since the platforms' lists differ and Shopify's grounds include commercial partnerships as well as regulation. A provider may bring its own list for its part, as Zid Pay's Kuwait terms name "اليمن" among others; such a list bars that part, not registering. Which regimes Sharrwah follows is a company decision (section 7). Q6-SH-K1270; Q6-SH-K2277; Q6-SH-K2270; Q6-WC-K0526; Q6-ZD-035
- **Contracting company map**: which Sharrwah company contracts with a Seller, chosen by its **Business place**, with a row for any other place, and each place's supplement to the seller terms. It follows Shopify, BigCommerce and WooCommerce, which choose the company by the seller's place, and BigCommerce's supplements. It sits behind its own part (rule 2), and its structure is decided now (rule 3: legal structure and partner contracts); with one company, it has one row. Not taken: one company in the terms for every place, as Salla's and Zid's terms read, since adding a company later would then touch every seller's contract. Q6-SH-K1243; Q6-BC-008; Q6-BC-K0425; Q6-WC-K0498; Q6-BC-010; Q6-SA-023; Q6-ZD-K0214
- **Verification**: checks in stages, each opening particular capabilities, with requirements set per place and entity type in the **Place set-up**. First, the Account user's phone or email at sign-up. Second, the Seller entity's identity and documents, before Sharrwah activates any part that takes payments or pays out, as Salla's data showed `"blocks_payment_activation": true` and as WooCommerce verifies with Stripe before its payments work. Third, a provider's own checks when it plugs in. A store can be built and opened before the second stage, as on Zid, where a commercial registration is needed only to connect payment gateways. Q6-SA-061; Q6-WC-K0393; Q6-ZD-K0241; Q6-BC-P0048
  - Gating activation, not money: Sharrwah takes no payment for a seller before the second stage, rather than suspending payments and settlement afterwards, as Salla's FAQ describes, or holding funds, as BigCommerce's payment service may. Either would hold sellers' money, and under the guard could go live only by the founder's decision. Rule 3: money flows are decided now. Q6-SA-037; Q6-BC-P0084
  - Who reviews, Sharrwah's own team as on Salla, or the provider as on Zid, stays open (section 5). Q6-SA-K0087; Q6-ZD-K0003
- **Part availability**: for each part Sharrwah offers, its own or a provider's, where it is available: the places, and, where the evidence shows a second key, the currencies, plans and entity types; with a reason when a seller can't use it. The platforms limit parts by place, BigCommerce and WooCommerce add currency and Shopify plan and product type, and Salla's payment data carry, per method, `"available_countries": []` and `"unavailable_reason": null`. Rule 2: availability is data per part, so opening a part in a new place changes no code. Q6-ZD-K0177; Q6-BC-022; Q6-WC-K0838; Q6-SH-030; Q6-SA-062
- **Provider choice**: for each place, the providers Sharrwah offers there for each kind of part, payments first, in order; the seller picks and connects one, and each provider approves the seller itself. It follows WooCommerce's list per country, BigCommerce's list by region, Shopify's third-party providers where its own service isn't available, and each provider's own application process. Where Sharrwah has no payment service of its own, this list is how a seller takes online payments, as Shopify's Yemen store showed `"thirdPartyDirectEligible":true`. Q6-WC-K0054; Q6-BC-P0038; Q6-SH-022; Q6-BC-P0048; Q6-SH-037
  - Two arrangements are possible: the seller contracts with the provider directly, as Salla tells stores outside Saudi Arabia to do with Tabby, or an account is opened through the platform, as WooPayments creates a Stripe Express account. Under the guard, an arrangement in which a provider holds sellers' money in an account opened through Sharrwah goes live only by the founder's decision. Q6-SA-056; Q6-WC-K0393
- **Availability notice**: how Sharrwah says something isn't available, built from the **Place set-up** and **Part availability** and never written screen by screen (rule 2): at sign-up, when the Business place isn't set up, a message naming where Sharrwah serves now, as Salla's string does; in the admin, where the part is used, naming the seller's place and the way forward, as Salla's bank-transfer string and Shopify's help do; and through the API, as Zid's message does. Q6-SA-007; Q6-SA-058; Q6-SH-025; Q6-ZD-025
  - **Place interest list**: a seller from a place not set up can leave its details and be told when Sharrwah opens there, as Salla promises; the requests count toward which places come next, as WooCommerce counts them. Q6-SA-007; Q6-WC-K0403
- **Change of place**: how a Seller moves its Business place: parts tied to the old place, and above all any money, are closed or settled first, as Shopify and Zid require; then the new place's set-up, contracting company and verification apply. WooCommerce's answer for its payments, no change at all, is not taken, since Sharrwah's places are to grow. Rule 3: money flows and legal structure. Q6-SH-006; Q6-ZD-K0230; Q6-WC-020

## 3. What can plug in, what is set per what, and who sets it

- What can plug in: payment providers, and providers of other parts such as pay-later, each with its own places, approval and rules, as Shopify's listing caveat, BigCommerce's application process, Zid's gateway approval and Salla's direct Tabby account show. Q6-SH-026; Q6-BC-P0048; Q6-ZD-K0003; Q6-SA-056
- Identity checks run by others: Salla's verification sends an SMS from Absher, Zid Pay requires Nafath, and WooCommerce verifies with Stripe. Q6-SA-K0089; Q6-ZD-K0261; Q6-WC-K0393
- Restricted-place lists from a regime or a provider: US sanctions on WooCommerce, and Zid Pay's lists by terms country. The screening tool behind such lists was not found on Shopify, and who screens is open on Zid. Q6-WC-K0526; Q6-ZD-035; Q6-SH-G0217; Q6-ZD-G0055
- Shipping companies by place, as Zid's Gulf store-creation article offers one (not checked on BigCommerce). Q6-ZD-008
- Set per place and entity type: the entity types, their documents and the age of majority, as Salla's table and Zid's note show for documents, and Shopify and Zid for age. Q6-SA-030; Q6-ZD-K0240; Q6-SH-001; Q6-ZD-009
- Set per Business place: the contracting company and the terms supplements, as on Shopify, BigCommerce and WooCommerce. Q6-SH-K1243; Q6-BC-008; Q6-WC-K0498; Q6-BC-010
- Set per regime, and per provider and the place its terms serve: the restricted places, as Zid Pay's two lists show. Q6-ZD-034; Q6-ZD-035
- Set per part: availability, by place; by place and currency; by plan; and by business category, as Shopify's own service excludes some categories and Salla Payments in the UAE some plans. Q6-BC-022; Q6-WC-K0838; Q6-SA-K1522; Q6-SH-022
- Set per region and per payment processor: prohibited products and categories, as on Shopify. Q6-SH-K2261; Q6-SH-K2265
- Set per place: trial and pricing, which on Shopify "might vary by store location", and the billing methods for Sharrwah's own fees, which on Shopify "depend on your store's region and billing currency". Q6-SH-009; Q6-SH-016
- Set per place, in order: the providers offered, as WooCommerce's list order "will be used to determine the priority of the suggestions". Q6-WC-K0055
- Sharrwah sets the places, the **Place set-up**, the **Contracting company map** and the provider lists, and applies the **Restricted places**, as Shopify sets its own list and contracting table and WooCommerce its lists per country. Q6-SH-018; Q6-SH-K1243; Q6-WC-K0054
- The seller sets its **Business place** and **Seller entity**, keeps them current, as Shopify requires of its location, and picks a provider. Q6-SA-059; Q6-SH-K1271; Q6-SH-025
- Each provider sets its own places, approval and prohibited categories: Shopify's categories "are provided by each Payment Processor in your Designated Country", and WooCommerce's account types follow its partner Stripe, which "strictly adheres to the laws and regulations of the country each account is based in". Q6-SH-K2265; Q6-WC-K0405
- Government bodies issue the documents a place requires, as Salla's step says, and run identity checks such as Absher and Nafath. Q6-SA-K0083; Q6-SA-K0089; Q6-ZD-K0261

## 4. Parts, in the order they can be built

- Place register: the **Place set-up**, with places as countries and the regions within them. Needs nothing before it.
- Restricted lists: the **Restricted places**. Needs the place register.
- Seller records: **Seller**, **Account user**, **Seller entity** and **Business place**. Needs the place register.
- Contracts: the **Contracting company map**, with the terms each **Seller** accepted. Needs the place register and the seller records.
- Sign-up: the flow that applies the **Eligibility rules**, asks the **Business place**, checks the **Restricted places**, and for a place not set up shows the **Availability notice** and offers the **Place interest list**. Needs the four parts above.
- Availability: **Part availability**, and the admin side of the **Availability notice**. Needs the place register.
- **Verification**: its stages, document capture per place and entity type, a review queue, and a hook for outside verifiers. Needs the seller records, sign-up and availability.
- **Provider choice**: the provider interface and the lists per place. Needs availability and verification. A provider arrangement holding sellers' money goes live only by the founder's decision.
- **Change of place**. Needs all the parts above.

## 5. What the design must keep open

- What a place is below the country: WooCommerce records a state or province, Zid an address down to the street, and Shopify's restrictions name regions of a country. The design keeps places as a hierarchy, a country and the regions within it, so that a rule can attach at either level; Yemen's evidence (section 6) and each place's entry settle which levels are used. Q6-WC-011; Q6-ZD-006; Q6-SH-K2275
- Which address decides what: the place of registration on Salla and WooCommerce, residence on BigCommerce, the billing address on Shopify, and the seller's or inventory's location on Zid. The design records each address apart, and each rule names the address it reads; the company decision on contracting, with a lawyer's advice to the founder, settles which address keys the contract. Q6-SA-059; Q6-WC-K0402; Q6-BC-008; Q6-SH-K1243; Q6-ZD-K0231
- Whether a seller from a place not set up may register with fewer parts, or is turned away: Shopify, observed, let a sign-up from Yemen create a store while its own payment service was not eligible for a Yemen entity; Salla's strings turn another country away with a notice. The design keeps registration and each part's availability as separate settings per place; the founder settles each place on deciding to enter it. Q6-SH-011; Q6-SH-036; Q6-SA-007
- Who verifies: Salla's own team, or the payment provider on Zid, WooCommerce and BigCommerce. The design keeps **Verification** behind an interface that a Sharrwah review or a provider's check can fill; partner contracts settle it. Q6-SA-K0087; Q6-ZD-K0003; Q6-WC-K0393; Q6-BC-P0072
- Which places are restricted, and who screens: the platforms' lists differ, Shopify's screening interface was not found, who screens for Zid Pay is open, and BigCommerce puts screening on the seller. The design keeps the lists as data, per regime and per provider, with a screening step at sign-up and before payments; the company decision on regimes and on a screening partner settles it. Q6-WC-K0526; Q6-ZD-035; Q6-SH-G0217; Q6-ZD-G0055; Q6-BC-K0431
- A payment service or wallet of Sharrwah's own: Salla suspends payments and settlement until a store is verified, and BigCommerce's payment service holds a new account's first payments before payout. The design keeps payments behind **Provider choice**, so that such a service, if the founder decides on one, plugs in as one more provider; under the guard, nothing that holds sellers' money goes live without the founder's decision. Q6-SA-037; Q6-BC-P0084
- Several entities and stores per seller: Shopify allows several stores per owner, and selling through several entities on two plans; Salla sets no limit on stores per owner. The data model keeps a Seller with many **Seller entity** records and stores from the start; the first build uses one entity per store. Q6-SH-K1240; Q6-SH-K1265; Q6-SA-038
- Businesses registered outside the place they sell in: Salla requires a Saudi commercial registration of a foreign merchant, Zid serves sellers "من جميع الجنسيات", and Zid Pay asks foreign companies for "ترخيص الاستثمار (للشركات الأجنبية)". The design keeps an entity's place of registration apart from the places it may sell in; a company decision settles who may register from abroad (section 7). Q6-SA-035; Q6-ZD-K0226; Q6-ZD-020
