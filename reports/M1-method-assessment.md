# Why the method didn't catch what you caught

  - Its checks cover "only the facts a decision rests on" (`METHOD.md`:204), and investigation stops "on risk, not on completeness" (:239).

- **My framing narrowed your concern of 24 October.** You said a record "can narrow an area it never examined" (amendment 2's reason, `METHOD.md`:336). I turned that into a rule about words, Terms, not about coverage. Choice 2's "never" was the same failure in another place.
- **Your fear that the plan changes shadowed earlier work holds in part.**
  - Four earlier bodies of work are referenced by neither the method nor any current record.
  - Two of them held what M1 missed. One also held an error M1 inherited.
  - None is better on every count. Each is a source to index and check, not a plan to bring back.
- **The fix is small.** Amendment 4 restores three dropped practices and makes the method's own changes traceable. It adds no pass, no agent and no step.

## Why they escaped

1. **Safeguards were lost when the method changed (an execution failure).**
   - It has happened three times:
     - the market guard, and your decisions F-00 to F-06, lost with PR #3 and restored at M0;
     - the pilot's absence rule;
     - the concern map as the subject index.
   - Section 13 traced the method's changes to your decisions ("Founder decisions changed under this method", `METHOD.md`:413), never to its own practices. Nothing set the old practices beside the new ones.
2. **No check looks for what is missing (a missing safeguard).**
   - IBM's classification of defects separates something missing from something incorrect or extraneous (Orthogonal Defect Classification).
   - M1's checks found the incorrect and the extraneous well: the glossary's narrowing, the posting key, the reversal-twin count, the Tabby quote, and the paragraph that broadened D-002.
   - They found the missing only when a checker happened to search past the text, as with D-002's nine items and Shopify's PayPal case. You found the rest.
3. **My framing narrowed your concern (an execution failure).** Point 4 above.
4. **The author frames the checks.** I write each checker's brief around the claims of a record I also wrote (W-19). Since amendment 3 the checkers try to refute, but they refute what is written.

## The completeness pass you proposed

**What already exists:**
- **For capture:** the repositories' denominators (sitemaps, route manifests), their ledgers (599,589 rows for Shopify) and their gap lists.
- **For concerns:** Q1, linked to the 34 areas through R1's platform areas (`studies/proposals/areas/areas-*.tsv`).
- **For the money subject:** the pilot's statements, absences and audits for S-01, and the Shopify payments study, eight reading passes over Shopify's payments, finance and billing (`shopify-payments-study/`).

**What is missing** is using them. A record never asks which entries of its areas it didn't use. A claim of absence never asks what the repository says it doesn't hold.

**A trial on Decision 1,** by script (`method/slice_coverage.py`; output `decisions/checks/M1-slice-coverage-D001.tsv`):
- **Decision 1's own areas** (A-03, A-11, A-12, A-13) hold 66 concerns and 931 statements. At the version you were sent (`d03af1b`):
  - 35 concerns have a source cited by the records or their checks;
  - 29 more are cited by the pilot that Decision 1 rests on;
  - 2 are cited by neither: Shop Cash, and complaints about financial products. Neither bears on who holds money.
- **The 31 the records don't cite.** Their titles pointed to 6 that bear on choice 2. Their statements show:
  - **Shopify's two ways for fees,** which your question surfaced: a separate bill for its own charges, and deductions from its own payment service's payouts. The pilot had recorded the same variation (S01-VP-19), and its Decision 9 tied it to who holds the money.   - **Partners' charges, which no M1 record had:**

- **Result:**
  - Nothing changes the line or the three choices.
  - Choice 2 now carries the partners' facts, and apps (S-06) join what Decision 1 leaves open.
  - The script runs in seconds and the triage took minutes: about an hour in all, most of it writing the script once.

**The practice behind it:**
- **Systematic mapping** (Petersen et al. 2008) builds a classification of a field before reviewing questions within it. Q1 is one. `PLAN.md` cited domain analysis of existing systems (FODA) for it (`PLAN.md`:441).
- **Search evaluation against a known set** (Zhang, Babar and Tell 2011) judges a search by how much of a known collection it finds. The concern map is that collection for each slice.
- **Traceability in both directions** (ISO/IEC/IEEE 24765's definition).
  - The method traces each decision to its evidence.
  - It doesn't trace the evidence back to the decisions. That direction is the one that shows what was never used.

**Better than making claims, then checking them?** Neither replaces the other. Checking claims finds what is wrong; mapping finds what is missing. The trial shows the second is cheap where the map exists. So it belongs inside the checks, not as a pass before every question.

**Its limits:**
- **It works at the level of a concern.** It can't see a miss inside a concern the record already cites: the "under review" error sat inside settings the records cited. Rule 4a catches that kind.
- **The map is frozen at commits of 9 to 11 October.**
  - Some structures are not among its lists, such as Salla's help-center tree and its dashboard translations.
  - M2's capability map is where it is brought up to date.
- **Choosing which concerns bear on a record is judgment.** The titles are the platforms' own words. Giving the choice to the independent checker keeps it away from the author.

## Amendment 4, proposed

It restores three safeguards the plan changes dropped, and makes the method's own changes traceable. Part two gives the evidence for 4c and 4d.
- **4a. Claims of absence** (section 6).
  - A claim that something isn't there ("only", "none", "doesn't show", "not found") is a fact only when it names two things:
    - its searches, run with `method/corpus_search.py` over every held corpus and language;
    - the repositories' gaps that bound it (`docs/GAPS.md`, the ledgers).

    Otherwise it is written as an interpretation.
  - At levels 2 and 3 the checker audits each one, as the pilot's A3 step did.
  - *Fits the method:* the search is targeted to the claim, so "nothing is read broadly" still holds.
- **4b. The omission check** (sections 5 and 6).
  - At framing, `method/slice_coverage.py` lists the concern map's slice for the areas the record's Terms and Scope reach, not only its subject's areas.
  - At verification, the checker receives the slice's concerns that the record doesn't cite. It says which bear on a claim, or on an item the record leaves open.
  - The coordinator records each disposition.
  - *Fits the method:* it is limited to what the record reaches and to what bears on its claims, so checking still covers "only the facts a decision rests on". It adds no step and no agent.
- **4c. The method's own changes,** checked by function (sections 12 and 13).
  - An amendment names, for each practice it retires, the risk that practice controlled. It either reassigns the risk or enters it in the register as accepted.
  - Section 13's map of earlier work gives every body of work a disposition, as M0 did for the Knowledge Map.
  - *Fits the method:* it carries section 10's change control over to the method itself.
- **4d. Where the references differ** (section 5, step 3).
  - Each variant becomes an option.
  - When one is a restricted form of another that the general one can reach by configuration, the general one is kept, and the restricted one is bound where it is needed. This is the first plan's rule, from Czarnecki, Helsen and Eisenecker (2005); the second change dropped it.
  - *Fits the method:* section 2 already names variability and binding time; this is that practice at step 3. Choices 2 and 3 already follow it.

**Not proposed:**
- a new mapping pass before each decision: Q1 is the map, and M2 refreshes it;
- a rule on older and newer founder decisions: sections 3 and 10 cover it;
- the first plan's cause labels and per-subject variation points, whose cost was measured;
- more rounds of checks.

The two risks behind it, W-20 (omissions) and W-21 (safeguards lost when the method changes), are now in the register as open.
