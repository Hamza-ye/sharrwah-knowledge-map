## 1. What the first plan and the pilot had, and what G1 decided

**The first plan** (`2842819:PLAN.md`) had:
- subjects "from evidence only", ranked by cost of change (:134, :147);
- a test form per subject, ending in "what follows by rule", with a generality rule (:184–222);
- absence counted "only from a complete list", with the search listed (:93, :196, :241);
- Yemen marked after the analysis (:17, :222), and a close-out sweep (:167);
- briefs "reviewed before they run" (:254);
- a kill rule: if "the errors come from the method again …, nothing scales until the frame has been re-examined" (:130).

It ended at a register. Design would start "under its own plan" (:22).

**The pilot** (S-01) produced 23 variation points, 614 variants, 183 Yemen marks and 14 founder items (`method-report.md`:7–16). Its review passed on the second re-check (`review.md`:621).
- **The method, in its report:** it "protects against the right faults … Its cost doesn't scale" (:182).
- **What it left:** seven proposals (:185–196) and three open items (:146–149).
- **On Decision 1:** C "for Yemen now", from phase D (`entry.md`:123–124).

**G1**, in the founder's words (`founder-messages/2026-10-18-g1-answer.md`):
- "The entry let me decide. I couldn't follow the work behind it, or the rulings made without me." (:15)
- "Decisions 1 to 14: defer, all of them." (:19)
- "No arrangement that holds sellers' money through Sharrwah goes live anywhere without my decision" (:20)
- "What next: re-frame" (:21), within "about the pilot's cost, 2 billion tokens" (:47).
- **Keep:** "a separate check of each brief before it runs"; "one independent review of each result before it reaches me"; quotes; the absence audit (:62–66).
- **Stop:** "labelling why platforms differ"; "checking each money question against Yemen's cases"; "going subject by subject in batches" (:68–71). Questions "don't take the test form" (:97).

*Interpretation:* the reasons given are following and cost; the Stop list removes the steps that multiplied cost (`method-report.md`:185).

## 4. The routes

**A. Continue.**
- *Keeps:* everything current, including the 53 founder rows after G1's (`founder-decisions.tsv`:11–63).
- *Risks:*
  - amendment 4 settles items 1 and 2 only;
  - its list of losses came from the method's author (W-19), and item 3 shows the list is incomplete;
  - M2 is one-way, over 34 areas (`decisions/subjects.tsv`:4). Starting it scales a method whose errors trace to itself (`M1-market-binding.md`:31), which the first plan's kill rule forbids (`2842819:PLAN.md`:130).
- *Cost to one baseline:* the lowest. Fold 4 into the text.
- *Wrong if:* an independent ledger finds no further loss.

**B. Rebaseline at G1.**
- *Keeps:* by default, every safeguard lost on 18 and 24 October, found or not.
- *Loses unless carried again:* `METHOD.md`'s outcome and checks, the decisions since G1, and M1's records.
- *Risks:*
  - the first plan leaves design to another plan (`2842819:PLAN.md`:22), so a further re-frame is built in;
  - the base is unproven. "Its cost doesn't scale" (`method-report.md`:182), its proposals never ran, and its open items stand. *Interpretation:* at about half a billion tokens per subject (:182), the 2-billion ceiling (`founder-decisions.tsv`:13) covers about four of 15 open subjects (`decisions/subjects.tsv`:3–17);
  - every wholesale re-cut has lost safeguards: PR #3, 18 October and 24 October (`M1-method-assessment.md`:52–57). The last was itself "the re-baseline" (`HISTORY.md`:44), after the founder asked this same question (`method-accepted.md`:42).
- *On Decision 1:* no change: the pilot recommended C (`entry.md`:123), D-001's line (:25).
- *Cost to one baseline:* the highest. A new method, 53 rows to re-take or carry, and six records to re-file.
- *Wrong if:* the ledger shows `METHOD.md`'s core blocks widening beyond what 4b fixes: "only the facts a decision rests on" (:204), and "nothing is read broadly" (:175).

**C. Rebaseline at the current state, by function.**
1. **Freeze:** no M2, no acceptance.
2. **One ledger.** An agent that wrote none of the versions, from a separately checked brief, lists every practice of the first plan, the pilot, G1's Keep and Stop, the re-frame, and `METHOD.md` with amendments 1–4. Each gets the risk it controls and its disposition: kept where, replaced by what, or retired with whose acceptance. This is 4c over the whole history: the management of change `M1-plan-changes.md`:66 cites.
3. **One text, `METHOD.md` version 2,** with no deltas.
   - It resolves items 1–9, and states each reversal of G1.
   - The founder approves it once, and `HISTORY.md` names the baseline (IEEE 828, as `METHOD.md`:40 cites it).
4. **Re-verify D-001 to D-006** under version 2, then put Decision 1.
5. **Run version 2 on one decision** with a measured budget before anything scales (`2842819:PLAN.md`:254; Boehm 1988, cited at :116).

- *Keeps:* what holds up from both lineages. *Loses:* only what the ledger retires, by name.
- *Risks:* it is another rewrite (W-03). But it doesn't re-cut the unit of work, which is where the losses came from (`M1-plan-changes.md`:59).
- *Cost to one baseline:* one ledger run, one rewrite, one founder review and six re-checks: more than A, far less than B.
- *Wrong if:* the ledger finds nothing beyond amendment 4 and item 3 (A was enough), or version 2 needs an amendment within its first subject (a deeper re-frame is due).

## 5. Recommendation

*Interpretation:* the losses share one cause, and it isn't G1: changes "checked for what they add, not for what they drop", carried "by summary, not by trace" (`M1-plan-trace.md`:59, :28).
