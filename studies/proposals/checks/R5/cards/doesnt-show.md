# Cards: what the proposals say Yemen's evidence doesn't show

16 points, from Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7, Q8, each named by its question and line.

## The cards

### Q1:129

**Cited at:** Q1, section 6, line 129

**Point:** - Yemen's evidence doesn't show which governorates or districts each area of control covers, which the register needs to place a seller's or a buyer's address in an area. A dated source listing each area's governorates and districts would show it.

### Q2:127

**Cited at:** Q2, section 6, line 127

**Point:** - Yemen's evidence doesn't show an identity service that a platform can use to check a seller's identity or documents, on which the identity **Provider** of **Seller requirements** for Yemen depends. What would show it: a source naming such a service in Yemen, who runs it and who may use it, with its place and date.

### Q2:131

**Cited at:** Q2, section 6, line 131

**Point:** - Yemen's evidence doesn't show a personal-data protection law, or a rule on where a platform must hold sellers' or buyers' personal data, at any place in Yemen, on which **Data location** for Yemen depends. What would show it: a law, regulation or authority instruction on personal data or its location, with its place and date.

### Q3:156

**Cited at:** Q3, section 6, line 156

**Point:** - Whether a store that sells only online needs a commercial register entry or a ministry's approval before it opens to buyers: Yemen's evidence doesn't show. The requirement set for launching a store (**Store launch state**) in Yemen depends on it, and the ministry's rule on registering or approving online stores would show it; the fact cited shows one approval given, not a rule requiring it. YK-200 (approval given to an online store @ the Ministry of Trade and Industry's area, as of 2026-10-10)
