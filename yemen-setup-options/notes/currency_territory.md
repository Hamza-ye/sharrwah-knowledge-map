# Yemen currency regimes and the territorial split, as they affect pricing, collecting and settling commerce payments

Scope note: researched 9 October 2026. Every figure is dated. Items older than 2024 are flagged **[pre-2024]**. "IRG areas" means areas held by the internationally recognized government (Aden, Hadramawt, Marib, Taiz city and others), where the CBY-Aden's post-2016 "new" notes circulate. "DFA/Houthi areas" means areas held by the Houthi de facto authorities (Sana'a, Hodeidah, Ibb and others), where only pre-2016 "old" notes are accepted. Source quality: World Bank, Sana'a Center, ACAPS and IGC are the strongest sources. Asharq Al-Awsat, Al-Araby Al-Jadeed, Al Jazeera, Al-Ayyam and Yemen Monitor are reputable news sources. yemen-press.net, almahriah.net and similar rate sites are sensationalist aggregators. I used them only for daily price quotes, which agree closely with the ACAPS and Sana'a Center figures.

## 1. Current (2025–2026) rate levels, spreads and volatility in Aden and Sana'a, and how old and new notes are treated

### Takeaway
Yemen has two de facto currencies sharing the code "YER". In Sana'a, old notes have been held at about 531–536 YER/USD since 2023. In Aden, new notes have traded at about 1,555–1,580 YER/USD for the whole of 2026, after a violent 2025 (a record low near 2,900 in mid-July 2025, then an appreciation of about 45% within weeks). One old rial is now worth about 2.94–2.96 new rials. New notes are banned and can be confiscated in Houthi areas. Old notes are the only rial cash Sana'a accepts. The Sana'a authorities have added their own coins and notes (2024–2025), which Aden calls counterfeit.

### Cited Findings
**Treatment of old and new banknotes**
- In January 2020, the CBY in Sana'a banned circulation of new YER notes issued by the CBY in Aden. Old notes are used in the DFA north, and new notes in IRG-controlled areas in the south and east. — [ACAPS YETI exchange-rate page](https://yemen.yeti.acaps.org/xr-commodities/)
- Houthi authorities ban new notes in their areas. Notes found on travellers may be confiscated or torn up at checkpoints (Al Jazeera, 19 July 2025). — [Al Jazeera, "بين عدن وصنعاء.. الريال المنقسم ينهك اليمنيين"](https://www.aljazeera.net/ebusiness/2025/7/19/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%86-%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%86%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%86%D9%82%D8%B3%D9%85-%D9%8A%D9%86%D9%87%D9%83)
- "Old" rials are used in Houthi-controlled areas, while banknotes issued by CBY-Aden after 2017 circulate in the south. The "old rial" zone has a fixed exchange rate enforced by restricting money-transfer operations, obligating exchange businesses to use the official rate, and penalizing violators (Sana'a Center policy brief, 17 Feb 2026). — [Sana'a Center, Supporting Trade Finance in Yemen Amid Uncertainty (PDF)](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Trade_Finance_in_Yemen_NO36_en.pdf)
- Old notes in Houthi areas are heavily worn and damaged and past their 18-month lifespan. When the CBY relocated to Aden in 2016, about 90% of the YR1.3 trillion nominally circulating was unusable **[2016 figure]**. Banks headquartered in Sana'a were barred from using newly issued rials for trade finance (Sana'a Center, Feb 2026). — [Sana'a Center trade-finance brief (PDF)](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Trade_Finance_in_Yemen_NO36_en.pdf)
- Houthi-issued currency: a 100-rial coin (March 2024), then a 50-rial coin (13 July 2025, about YR5 billion, under 1% of old-rial supply) and a 200-rial note (15 July 2025, about YR35 billion, over 2% of old rials), presented as replacements for worn notes. CBY-Aden called them "counterfeit, destructive." — [Sana'a Center, The Yemen Review Jul–Sep 2025, Economy](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jul-sept-2025/25633); [Al Jazeera, 19 July 2025](https://www.aljazeera.net/ebusiness/2025/7/19/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%86-%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%86%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%86%D9%82%D8%B3%D9%85-%D9%8A%D9%86%D9%87%D9%83)
- CBY-Aden warned banks and exchangers against handling the 50-rial coin, citing possible sanctions exposure linked to the Houthi terrorist designation. — [Sana'a Center, "Currency War Threatens to Deepen Yemen's Monetary Rift"](https://sanaacenter.org/publications/analysis/24985)

**Cross-check against prices**

### Inferences
- **Ratio (my arithmetic):** on 16 Sept 2026 sell rates, 1,571 / 533 = 2.95 new rials per old rial. On ACAPS 8 Oct 2026, 1,576 / 533 = 2.96. One old-YER unit is therefore worth about three new-YER units. The same nominal amount, "10,000 YER", differs about threefold in value depending on area.
- **Buy/sell spreads (my arithmetic, Sept 2026):**

  | Area | USD spread | SAR spread |
  |---|---|---|
  | Aden | ~0.6% (1,562/1,571) | ~0.5% (411/413) |
  | Sana'a | ~0.4% (531/533) | ~0.3% (139.8/140.2) |

  Retail FX spreads are therefore small. The large cost is the gap between zones, not the spread within a zone.
- **SAR/USD cross-rate (my arithmetic):** about 3.80 in both zones (Aden 1,571/413; Sana'a 533/140.2), close to the SAR's 3.75 peg. USD and SAR are interchangeable as a bridge unit in both zones, at about a 1.3% markup.
- **Volatility regime:** Sana'a has effectively been fixed for about three years. Aden has been in a narrow band (about 1,550–1,632) for 14 months under administrative controls. Its 2025 history shows a 45% move in weeks and a 23% intraday spike, so the stability is policy-dependent, not market-anchored. A platform should design for both a quiet regime and a shock regime.
- **USD purchasing power is roughly comparable across zones:** basket about $81 in Sana'a (43,300 / 533) and about $83 in Aden (130,366 / 1,576), using Aug 2026 basket and Oct 2026 rates. A USD- or SAR-referenced base price is therefore a defensible anchor for cross-zone pricing.

### Gaps
- I found no CBY-Sana'a primary publication of its official rate. All Sana'a figures come from market trackers, the World Bank or the Sana'a Center.
- I found no primary CBY-Aden circular giving both the buy and sell USD fixed rates for Aug 2025 or Feb 2026.
- I could not get a daily time series for 2025–2026. ACAPS YETI has one, but only via its Telegram/CSV export, which the page does not render.
- There is no data on intra-zone geographic variation in rates (for example Mukalla, Marib or Taiz city versus Aden), apart from general statements that rates differ by governorate.

## 2. What it costs and takes to move money between the two areas, and what rules restrict it

### Takeaway
No rial-to-rial channel crosses the line at face value. Every cross-line transfer embeds the about 2.95× conversion between old and new notes, which providers present as a "commission" (reported at about 180% Aden→Sana'a in July 2026), plus margins and fees on top. Most cross-line flows are therefore priced in USD or SAR. Physical new notes cannot be carried north. The CBY-Aden's Unified Network (UNMONEY) is mandatory in IRG areas but banned in Houthi areas, and Sana'a funnels transfers through its own network under ceilings. Cross-line settlement is structurally an FX conversion through exchange houses in a contested regulatory space.

### Cited Findings
**Costs**
- "Money transfer networks imposed commissions reaching 180% when transferring funds from Aden to Sana'a to compensate for the difference in value of the two editions." Rates on the day: Aden USD 1,554/1,562, Sana'a 531/533 (10 July 2026). — [Yemen Press, 10 July 2026](https://yemen-press.net/news190645.html)
- **[pre-2024, historical]** In 2020, banks and providers charged "30 percent or more" to process cash transfers from government to Houthi areas. A sender paid 1.33 new rial units for 1 old unit delivered, when the gap was about 33% (Aden 805 vs Sana'a 605 YER/USD, Aug 2020). Exchangers price cross-area transfers "in an intermediated foreign currency," usually SAR or USD. — [Sana'a Center Yemen Economic Bulletin, 9 Sept 2020](https://sanaacenter.org/publications/perspectives-and-analyses/11562)
- **[pre-2024]** Earlier reported charges: 10,000 YER per 100,000 YER (2019), and about 20% described by exchangers as "the exchange difference, not a commission". — [Al-Mahrah Post](https://almahrahpost.com/news/14745); [Laa Media](https://laamedia.net/news.aspx?newsnum=33644)
- Anecdote (Al Jazeera, 19 July 2025, when Aden was about 2,830/USD): a worker earning 10,000 new rials a day in Aden says it shrinks to "barely 1,000 riyals" in Sana'a. He converts savings into USD or SAR to avoid losses. This is a journalistic anecdote; at July 2025 rates the pure FX conversion would give about 1,900 old rials, so the claim implies large extra fees or exaggeration. — [Al Jazeera, 19 July 2025](https://www.aljazeera.net/ebusiness/2025/7/19/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%86-%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%86%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%86%D9%82%D8%B3%D9%85-%D9%8A%D9%86%D9%87%D9%83)
- "A riyal sent from Aden cannot be used at face value in Sana'a … many transactions across the North-South divide now take place in foreign currencies, mainly USD or SAR, adding new layers of transaction costs." "Most internal transfers between IRG and HC areas are conducted in foreign currency." USD transfers cost more for northern recipients because USD banknotes are scarce in the north (IGC, Feb 2026). — [IGC, "From cash to capital: leveraging remittances for Yemen's economic future" (PDF, Feb 2026)](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/YEM-24338%20From%20cash%20to%20capital.pdf)
- The IGC report also says exchangers "routinely exploit regulatory loopholes, manipulating exchange rates and imposing excessive or arbitrary fees," and that "the absence of a harmonised payment system enables financial operators to impose additional fees when transferring funds between IRG and HC areas." — [IGC (PDF)](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/YEM-24338%20From%20cash%20to%20capital.pdf)

**Rules and restrictions, Sana'a side**
- Feb 2024: the Sana'a-based Yemeni Money Exchange Association mandated that remittances be disbursed in the original currency sent (for example USD). March 2024: the CBY-Sana'a required all transfers to be disbursed in SAR. — [IGC (PDF)](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/YEM-24338%20From%20cash%20to%20capital.pdf)
- March 2024: the Sana'a exchangers' association banned exchange firms from dealing with UNMONEY. — [Al-Masdar Online, on the unified network](https://almasdaronline.com/articles/297946); [IGC (PDF)](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/YEM-24338%20From%20cash%20to%20capital.pdf)
- Dec 2025: more than 14 exchange companies in Sana'a closed in the preceding three months, after the Houthis cut their banking system off from CBY-Aden. Transfers were "restricted to one specific network and subjected to strict monetary ceilings," and exchangers were barred from the Aden unified network. The Sana'a Exchangers Association cites an "acute liquidity crisis" caused by stagnation, restrictions on transfers to government areas, and the UNMONEY ban (Asharq Al-Awsat, 15 Dec 2025). — [Asharq Al-Awsat, 15 Dec 2025](https://aawsat.com/5219669-%D8%B4%D8%A8%D8%AD-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A5%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B3-%D9%8A%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AD%D9%82-%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%83%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%81%D8%A9-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D9%85%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%B7%D9%82-%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%B7%D8%B1%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D9%88%D8%AB%D9%8A%D9%8A%D9%86)
- Mid-August 2025: CBY-Sana'a banned banks and exchangers under Houthi control from using the Aden import-financing committee's mechanism. — [Sana'a Center, Yemen Review Jul–Sep 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jul-sept-2025/25633)
- **[undated, likely pre-2024]** Reported Houthi limits: no cash transfer over 100,000 YER between governorates, and no sale or transfer over $3,000 to a person without keeping an ID copy. **[pre-2024, 2018]** Banks were barred from transferring over YR450,000 Sana'a→Aden without approval. — [Al-Ayyam](https://www.alayyam.info/news/8BDOXFT9-KCVSIR-3282); [Sana'a Center bulletin, via search](https://sanaacenter.org/publications/analysis/12004)

**Rules and restrictions, Aden side**
- UNMONEY (الشبكة الموحدة للأموال) was registered in 2021. It is a closed joint-stock company with capital of YR5 billion and 47 founding exchange-company owners, under CBY supervision. Exchangers had to link to it by end-January 2023. A mid-February 2024 circular required exchangers in government areas to use it. On 26 June 2024, CBY-Aden ordered a "full and permanent halt" to domestic transfer networks owned by banks or exchange companies. — [Al-Ayyam on UNMONEY launch](https://www.alayyam.info/news/9QC6JW9G-VE3NRB-0C0C); [Al-Masdar Online](https://almasdaronline.com/articles/297946); [Al-Ayyam, CBY halts exchange-company networks](https://www.alayyam.info/news/9PVH0GTE-PLTSNO-54A3)
- Date conflict: the IGC dates the UNMONEY launch to July 2024, while Yemeni outlets report a Feb 2024 mandate and a June 2024 halt of other networks. — [IGC (PDF)](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/YEM-24338%20From%20cash%20to%20capital.pdf)
- Early Aug 2025: the CBY-Aden cut the ceiling on FX sales for personal purposes from US$5,000 to US$2,000, with documentation of purpose required. — [Sana'a Center, Yemen Review Jul–Sep 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jul-sept-2025/25633)
- Feb 2026: banks may buy FX from citizens up to SAR 10,000 per person per day. — [Sana'a Center, Yemen Review Jan–Mar 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jan-mar-2026/26962); [Al-Araby, 15 Feb 2026](https://www.alaraby.co.uk/economy/%D8%A3%D8%B2%D9%85%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%84%D8%A9-%D8%AA%D8%AF%D9%81%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D9%83%D8%B2%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%86%D9%8A-%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AB%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%AA-%D8%B3%D8%B9%D8%B1-%D8%B5%D8%B1%D9%81-%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%AF-%D9%84%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%84)
- The CBY-Aden also revoked licenses of "dozens of entities involved in currency speculation" and inspects exchange outlets regularly (Q1 2026). In Q3 2025, numerous exchange outlets and transfer networks were suspended. — [Sana'a Center Jan–Mar 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jan-mar-2026/26962); [Sana'a Center Jul–Sep 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jul-sept-2025/25633)

**Banking-system split (affects any bank-based cross-line settlement)**
- April 2024: CBY-Aden ordered banks to move headquarters to Aden within 60 days, and Sana'a retaliated by banning dealings with 13 Aden-aligned banks. Some restrictions were partially reversed under the July 2024 UN-brokered de-escalation. — [IGC (PDF)](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/YEM-24338%20From%20cash%20to%20capital.pdf)
- After the US FTO designation of the Houthis (effective March 2025), "nearly all banks had transferred their main operations to the government-controlled areas as of July [2025]." CBY-Aden "said it would suspend any transactions with the CBY-Sana'a or any other institutions under Houthi control" (Sana'a Center, Feb 2026). — [Sana'a Center trade-finance brief (PDF)](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Trade_Finance_in_Yemen_NO36_en.pdf)
- No national RTGS or switch exists, which makes internal bank transfers "nearly impossible." World Bank-funded RTGS and Fast Payment System work is underway, expected over about three years. — [IGC (PDF)](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/YEM-24338%20From%20cash%20to%20capital.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Example, using 16 Sept 2026 rates (my arithmetic):**
  - Aden→Sana'a: 100,000 new YER buys about $63.65 in Aden (at 1,571), which sells for about 33,800 old YER in Sana'a (at 531). Delivering 100,000 old YER therefore costs about 296,000 new YER, a "commission" of about 196%.
  - Sana'a→Aden: 100,000 old YER yields about 293,000 new YER.
  - The reported 180% figure is therefore mostly the exchange-rate difference, not a fee. Real fees are the provider's rate margin plus any explicit fee, which I could not isolate.
- Cross-line settlement for a platform is an FX trade between two currencies, not a domestic transfer. It needs a counterparty with liquidity on both sides (exchange houses or hawala), and it is exposed to sudden rule changes on either side (network bans, ceilings, SAR-only disbursement).
- Because CBY-Aden-regulated institutions have been told to suspend dealings with Houthi-controlled institutions, and the US FTO designation applies, a platform whose IRG-side bank or wallet partner settles with a Sana'a-side counterparty carries sanctions and licensing risk. The likely compliant pattern is two separate in-zone ecosystems, with cross-zone value moved only via licensed exchange houses, if at all. This needs legal review.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable 2025–2026 schedule of explicit cross-line fees, separate from the FX difference, for named providers, and no figure for the Sana'a→Aden direction.
- I found no current text of Houthi ceilings on transfers (amounts per transaction or day), only the Dec 2025 report of "strict ceilings" on "one specific network", which is unnamed.
- I could not confirm whether, in late 2026, UNMONEY agents can pay out in Houthi areas at all, or which network Sana'a mandates.

## 3. How merchants and online sellers price goods (YER, USD, SAR) and handle a buyer in the other area

### Takeaway
In IRG areas, the law since August 2025 requires prices, invoices and contracts in Yemeni rials. Before that, the Saudi riyal dominated Aden pricing, and compliance is uneven. In Houthi areas, everyday goods are priced in old rials at a fixed rate, while transfers and larger values gravitate to SAR. Online, at least one Yemeni multi-vendor marketplace exposes four currencies, including two separate "Yemeni rial" options for Sana'a (old) and Aden. Cross-zone buyers are effectively foreign-currency customers.

### Cited Findings
- Cabinet Decision No. 13 of 2025 (August 2025) prohibits using foreign currencies instead of the national currency in commercial and service transactions and financial contracts. All dealings in the "liberated" governorates must be in Yemeni rials. — [Yemeni PM's office on X](https://x.com/Yemen_PM/status/1954999121284304979); [Saba News](https://www.sabanew.net/story/ar/132823); [Al-Masdar Online](https://almasdaronline.com/articles/323695)
- The Ministry of Justice issued a circular banning foreign-currency dealing in financial and commercial transactions and barring invoices or bonds issued in foreign currency. The Public Prosecution directed prosecutors to enforce exclusive use of the national currency. — [Yemen Future](https://yemenfuture.net/news/33166); [Al-Mashhad](https://www.almashhad.news/335316)
- The World Bank describes "a ban on using foreign currency in local transactions" as one of the measures behind Aden's 2025 stabilization. — [World Bank YEM Spring 2026 (PDF)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099630405192627146/pdf/IDU-915b334e-33e9-407b-b634-2ee6167b4790.pdf)
- A report noted the government still charges Hajj and Umrah fees in SAR despite the ban, and that the SAR "dominates" transactions in Aden and government areas (Aug 2025). — [Yemen Press](https://yemen-press.net/news146875.html)
- Price controls in Aden (2025): the PM ordered field inspection teams, transparent pricing based on actual import costs, linking local prices to the daily exchange rate, mandatory official invoices, and licence withdrawal and blacklisting for violators. — [Yemen Monitor](https://www.yemenmonitor.com/?p=146776); [Al-Ain](https://al-ain.com/article/yemeni-riyal-recovery-government-control-prices)
- After the Aug 2025 appreciation, food prices in Aden fell 20–40% and diesel fell from YR1,550 to YR1,200/litre. Hayel Saeed Anam Group warned that uncoordinated price cuts could cause supply disruptions. This shows how fast YER prices are re-marked when the rate moves. — [Sana'a Center Jul–Sep 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jul-sept-2025/25633)
- Customs valuation: the customs dollar rate rose from YR750 to YR1,550/USD in mid-May 2026, for non-essential goods only. — [Sana'a Center Apr–Jun 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2026/28066)
- Same goods, different nominal prices: a tomato basket costing 5,000 old rials in Houthi areas needs 30,000 new rials in Aden (Al Jazeera, July 2025, when Aden was about 2,830/USD). — [Al Jazeera, 19 July 2025](https://www.aljazeera.net/ebusiness/2025/7/19/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%86-%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%86%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%86%D9%82%D8%B3%D9%85-%D9%8A%D9%86%D9%87%D9%83)
- Food prices in the south fluctuate because nearly all food imports depend on the Aden rate. In the north, higher customs and levies have raised prices despite the stable rate (July 2026). — [Yemen Press, 10 July 2026](https://yemen-press.net/news190645.html)
- Online example: Bab Al-Mandab, a Yemeni multi-vendor store based in Sana'a, offers a currency selector with "دولار ($)", "ريال يمني صنعاء (ر.ي.قديم)", "ريال سعودي (ر.س)" and "ريال يمني عدن (ر.ي عدن)". The cart defaults to USD and a coupon threshold is stated as "100 دولار" (fetched Oct 2026). — [bab-almandab.com](https://bab-almandab.com/)
- Remittances (the main source of household FX) come about 60% from Saudi Arabia and 18% from the UAE (World Bank, Spring 2026). — [World Bank YEM Spring 2026 (PDF)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099630405192627146/pdf/IDU-915b334e-33e9-407b-b634-2ee6167b4790.pdf)
- Digital payment usage is low: only 9% of Yemenis make digital payments, versus 40% regionally. Account ownership is 18% for men and 5% for women (Findex 2022, cited Feb 2026). — [IGC (PDF)](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/YEM-24338%20From%20cash%20to%20capital.pdf)

### Inferences: consolidated payment-model implications (all inference, built on findings in sections 1–5)
- **Denomination of the obligation.**
  - Denominate each buyer obligation in the rial of the zone where the buyer pays: old YER for DFA areas, new YER for IRG areas.
  - In IRG areas this is a legal requirement (Decision 13/2025): a USD- or SAR-denominated consumer obligation or invoice is exposed to enforcement, even if a USD/SAR reference price is shown.
  - For DFA areas I found no comparable decree, but the enforced fixed rate makes old YER the natural unit, with SAR as the transfer currency.
  - Keep an internal reference or base price in USD or SAR, with SAR the better bridge given the remittance mix and the March 2024 Sana'a SAR-disbursement rule. Derive each zone's YER price from it.
- **Where the exchange rate must be fixed.**
  - (a) *Order:* when a seller's base price is in USD/SAR, or the seller's zone differs from the buyer's, lock the zone-YER price at checkout with a short validity, since Aden has re-marked prices 20–40% within weeks.
  - (b) *Collection:* if the buyer pays in a different instrument (for example SAR cash on delivery, or a wallet balance in the other zone's rial), fix the conversion at collection and record it.
  - (c) *Refund:* refund in the same currency, zone and nominal amount as collected, never re-converted, so the platform does not create cross-zone exposure. If the rate moved, the refund must state whose loss or gain it is.
  - (d) *Payout:* pay out in the seller's zone currency. A cross-zone payout is an FX trade, so fix its rate at payout time against a named source and pass the conversion cost through.
- **Authoritative rate source by zone.**
  - IRG zone: the CBY-Aden fixed rate set by board decision (currently USD ~1,558, SAR 410/413), because exchangers and banks are legally bound to it, with a market check against exchanger quotes (Sept–Oct 2026 USD 1,562–1,580).
  - DFA zone: the CBY-Sana'a enforced rate (~531–536), verified against market quotes.
  - Cross-zone: no official rate exists. It is implied through USD/SAR as Aden rate divided by Sana'a rate (~2.95), and in practice is the exchange house's quote.
  - Never use global FX APIs: Wise shows ~236 YER/USD (Oct 2026), wrong for both zones.
- **Who bears the difference.**
  - Within a zone, the seller should bear post-order rate moves, because the price was fixed in local YER at order.
  - Across zones, the conversion (~196% nominal gap plus margin) must be shown to the buyer as part of price or fees, or absorbed explicitly by the seller.
  - The platform should hold no open FX position between collection and payout unless it prices that risk. The precedent is a 23% one-day move in Aden in Aug 2025.
  - If CBY-Aden official and parallel rates diverge (Feb 2026: 1,558 versus 1,520), contract terms should say which one governs.
- **A single seller selling into both zones.**
  - The seller needs two price lists (or one base price with two zone conversions), two collection rails (one licensed or operating in each zone), and two payout ledgers: an old-YER balance payable in the north and a new-YER balance payable in the south.
  - Alternatively, the seller accepts conversion of one side through a licensed exchange house at the cross rate.
  - Physical consolidation is not viable: new notes are confiscated at Houthi checkpoints, and Aden-regulated institutions are barred from dealing with Houthi-controlled ones.
  - Compliance (FTO designation, CBY-Aden circulars) may stop a single legal entity from operating rails on both sides. This needs legal review.
- **What the platform must represent.**
  - A currency identity richer than ISO 4217: "YER" plus a zone or note-edition qualifier (for example YER-OLD/Sana'a versus YER-NEW/Aden), as Bab Al-Mandab already does in its UI.
  - A zone attribute on buyer, seller, delivery address and payment instrument, which can each differ.
  - Rate records with source (CBY-Aden fixed, CBY-Sana'a, exchanger quote), side (buy or sell), timestamp and validity.
  - Per-zone ledgers; conversion entries as explicit FX transactions.
  - A governorate-to-zone mapping that can change, since front lines move and some cities (for example Taiz) straddle zones.
  - Coin and note-edition awareness for cash on delivery: Houthi 50-rial coin and 200-rial note in the north, and worn-note refusal in Aden.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic survey of how Yemeni online sellers actually price for and fulfil buyers in the other zone, beyond the Bab Al-Mandab currency selector. The site shows no listed payment methods or delivery zones.
- I found no 2026 evidence on compliance with the Aden rial-only decree (for example whether rents, cars and gold are still quoted in SAR), and no Houthi-side rule on pricing in foreign currency.
- I found no data on whether wallet or bank balances carry a discount or premium against cash rials in Aden during the 2026 cash shortage.

## 4. Which institutions publish rates, and how reliable they are

### Takeaway
No single authoritative national rate exists.

| Source | What it gives | Reliability |
|---|---|---|
| CBY-Aden | Fixed, legally enforced rates by board decision, announced in news releases rather than as a maintained feed | Its website's rate-history page stops in January 2022 |
| CBY-Sana'a | An enforced rate around 531–536 | No accessible primary feed found |
| ACAPS YETI, YemenSul, Telegram channels, local news rate pages | Day-to-day market quotes | Unofficial but mutually consistent |
| World Bank, Sana'a Center, WFP, FSNIS | Periodic, lagged series | Credible |
| Global FX providers (Wise, XE, Ria, Trading Economics) | About 236–239 YER/USD | Matches neither zone |

### Cited Findings
- The CBY-Aden website's "أسعار صرف العملات" page shows buy/sell entries for USD and SAR only up to 11 January 2022 (USD 1,103/1,123; SAR 290/295) **[pre-2024]**. It is not a live source. — [CBY-Aden currency history](https://cby-ye.com/currencyhsitory)
- CBY-Aden publishes monthly monetary and financial developments reports, listed through March 2026 (from search summary; contents not opened). — [CBY-Aden monthly reports](https://cby-ye.com/pages/28)
- CBY-Aden rate fixes are communicated by board decision and press: SAR 425/428 (Aug/Sept 2025), then SAR 410/413 and USD 1,558 (Feb 2026), with a threat of legal action for non-compliance. — [Al-Ayyam, 13 Feb 2026](https://alayyam.info/news/AIOVZNTN-XIA26F-F8B0); [Yemen Monitor, 1 Sept 2025](https://www.yemenmonitor.com/en/Details/ArtMID/908/ArticleID/148920)

- The World Bank uses ACAPS YETI and "Telegram FX monitoring" for its Yemen rate figures and charts. — [World Bank YEM Spring 2026 (PDF)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099630405192627146/pdf/IDU-915b334e-33e9-407b-b634-2ee6167b4790.pdf)
- WFP has run market monitoring in Yemen since 2009, collecting prices, fuel and exchange rates across 276 districts, including 23 main-city markets (monthly). — [WFP Market Monitoring in Yemen (PDF via fscluster)](https://fscluster.org/sites/default/files/documents/WFP%20Market%20Monitoring%20in%20Yemen.pdf)
- The CBY has discontinued remittance data production, "leaving a critical gap" (World Bank, 2026). — [World Bank YEM Spring 2026 (PDF)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099630405192627146/pdf/IDU-915b334e-33e9-407b-b634-2ee6167b4790.pdf)
- Local rate pages (for example Boqash, exrye.com, ye-rial.com, khabaragency, 2dec.net) publish Aden and Sana'a quotes. One page dated 6 Oct 2026 was found showing rates from 19 May 2026, so staleness is a real risk. — [Boqash](https://boqash.com/price-currency/); [exrye Aden](https://exrye.com/aden); [2dec.net rate page](https://www.2dec.net/rate.html)
- Wise shows a YER mid-market rate of 1 YER = 0.004234 USD (about 236.2 YER/USD) with no mention of Yemen's dual rates (fetched 9 Oct 2026). Trading Economics showed about 238.65 on 6 April 2026. — [Wise YER→USD](https://wise.com/gb/currency-converter/yer-to-usd-rate); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/yemen/currency)
- In GCC corridors, a sender "may be quoted three different rates for the same transfer depending on which exchange house they use, with no way to verify if the rates are fair." — [IGC (PDF)](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/YEM-24338%20From%20cash%20to%20capital.pdf)

### Inferences
- **IRG legal reference:** the CBY-Aden board-fixed rate. A platform should record the decision date and value manually or via news monitoring, because there is no machine-readable feed.
- **Market reality:** ACAPS YETI (or YemenSul/Telegram) for both zones, cross-checked against two or more exchanger quotes.
- **DFA reference:** the market quote, which equals the enforced rate in practice.
- **Divergence:** when the CBY-Aden fixed rate and the market diverge (2.4% in Feb 2026), the platform's terms must name which applies.
- **Third-party FX libraries:** any integration that pulls YER from a global FX API will misprice by roughly 2.2× (Sana'a) to 6.7× (Aden). This is a concrete engineering hazard.

### Gaps
- I did not find whether CBY-Sana'a publishes a daily official rate online.
- I did not find whether CBY-Aden publishes a daily rate bulletin anywhere other than press releases.
- I did not verify YemenSul's methodology.
- I could not access WFP's 2026 market bulletins directly (fscluster PDF returned 403).

## 5. Recent policy moves (2024–2026) on unification, new banknotes or coins, and exchange controls

### Takeaway
Both sides have entrenched the split rather than unified it:
- Houthi coins and notes (2024–2025).
- Aden's rial-only decree, fixed rates, FX caps, import-financing committee and exchanger crackdown (2025–2026).
- Bank relocation to Aden after the FTO designation (2025).
- Rival payment infrastructures: UNMONEY versus Sana'a's single network, and the Aden YPCC and World Bank-funded FPS versus a Houthi FPS.

Aden faces a rial cash shortage in 2026 (worn notes, hoarding, withdrawal caps), and Sana'a faces chronic old-note scarcity. I found no official unification timetable.

### Cited Findings
**Aden-side measures**
- The National Committee for Regulating and Financing Imports was created in mid-July 2025, chaired by the CBY-Aden governor and covering 25 essential commodities. It had approved over US$3 billion since August 2025, including US$600 million in January 2026. — [Sana'a Center Jul–Sep 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jul-sept-2025/25633); [Sana'a Center Jan–Mar 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jan-mar-2026/26962)

### Inferences
- **Durability:** the split should be treated as a permanent design constraint for at least the planning horizon. No unification process with a timetable was found, and each side's 2024–2026 moves raise the cost of reversal.
- **Cash fragility in both zones:**
  - In Aden, worn notes, hoarding and daily withdrawal caps (80,000 YER, about US$51, in April 2026) mean cash on delivery and cash-out are capacity-constrained. Digital balances can be hard to convert to cash, which favours keeping value in digital form inside the platform.
  - In Sana'a, scarce old notes plus new Houthi coins and notes mean cash collection must handle denomination mix and counterfeit or legitimacy disputes.
- **Policy-shock risk in Aden:** rate fixes are changed by board decision (twice in six months), and enforcement includes confiscation of FX holdings (Sept 2025). Platform FX buffers held as USD or SAR at exchangers in Aden carry policy risk.
- **Infrastructure:** the YPCC and the World Bank FPS/RTGS (IRG side) and the Houthi FPS (DFA side) point toward two separate domestic switching systems. A platform would integrate twice.

- I could not confirm whether CBY-Aden's reported 100-billion-rial issuance (Feb 2026) happened, or whether new denominations or coins were issued by Aden in 2025–2026.
- I could not confirm whether the Sana'a ban on Al-Kuraimi (June 2025) persisted or was reversed, or Al-Kuraimi's current operating status in Houthi areas.
- I found no evidence of a 2026 Houthi currency issuance beyond the July 2025 coin and note.
