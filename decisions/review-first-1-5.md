# Review of first decisions 1 to 5

**For the founder, in five lines**
1. Every quote in `first-decisions.md` is on its pinned line (`check_report.py`: 151 checked, 0 not found). The problems are in its wording and its readings.
2. The wording gives Shopify's answer in place of a question in decisions 1, 3, 4 and 5. Decision 2's question is neutral.
3. Every practice source cited for these five exists and says what the file says. One is superseded (INCITS 359-2004). **Reading:** another, Chong et al., answers decision 3's question rather than decision 1's.
4. **Reading:** the largest gap is decision 5. A Shopify market is a group of buyers. The founder's markets carry providers, law and limits, which Shopify ties to the store's own country. Shopify's own evidence also contradicts decision 3's reading that pods can carry a market's rule on where data is held.
5. **Readings:** the rules of decisions 1 and 2, and decision 5's place for each market's values, are needed before any area. Decision 3's mechanism, decision 4's roles and organizations, and buyers' markets could wait.

*Italics* are `first-decisions.md`'s words, or the founder's where marked. Words in double quotes are Shopify's, on the pinned line that follows them. Lines marked *the capture's* are the evidence repository's own descriptions.

## 1. The shop is the tenant

- **Wording.** The title, and the question's *the one unit that every record, request, job and event belongs to, so that no shop ever sees another shop's data*, give Shopify's answer: there is one unit, and it is the shop. **Reading:** a neutral question would be: what is the unit of tenancy, and what data sits above it or outside it?
- **Practice citations.** All are real and match what the file says:
  - Chong, Carraro and Wolter, *Multi-Tenant Data Architecture*, Microsoft, June 2006 (copy read: https://docs.citusdata.com/en/v8.0/_static/mt-data-arch.pdf). It sets out three approaches, from separate databases to a shared schema where a Tenant ID column marks each row. **Reading:** it answers how tenants' data is kept apart (decision 3), not what the tenant is.
  - Kabbedijk, Bezemer, Jansen and Zaidman, *Defining multi-tenancy*, Journal of Systems and Software 100, 2015, pp. 139–148 (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2014.10.034). The definition matches, but I saw it only as quoted in a 2023 slide deck (https://cug.org/proceedings/cug2023_proceedings/includes/files/pap132s2-file2.pdf), not in the paper.
  - AWS, *SaaS Architecture Fundamentals*, 3 August 2022 (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/saas-architecture-fundamentals/saas-identity.html). It connects each signed-in user to a tenant context that is used throughout the system.
- **Needed first.** **Reading:** yes, because every area's records key on it. Shopify later rebuilt the primary keys of some of its main tables around the shop: "we create a composite primary key [shop_id, order_id]" ([Composite Primary Keys, 2020, L270](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L270)).
- **Not borne out:**
  - *every area keys its data, requests, jobs and events on the shop*. Shopify says a shop "is the identifying entity for most data models" and puts its id on "all shop-owned tables" ([Shard Balancing, L210](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L210)). Some data sits above the shop: "centralized users and billing" ([manage-orgs, L6](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/help-center/help.shopify.com@en-ar/md/en/manual/your-account/manage-orgs-and-stores/manage-orgs.md?plain=1#L6)), and "A Shopify ID belongs to an individual user, and not to a store." ([Shopify ID, L3](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/help-center/help.shopify.com@en-ar/md/en/manual/your-account/logging-in/sso-migration-guide.md?plain=1#L3)).
  - *Every call names one shop*. Some of the admin's own calls name no shop, such as "ShopsOnlyActiveDestinations/destinations" ([operations index, L43](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/index/operations.jsonl?plain=1#L43)). Others name an organization: "OrganizationFlags/org_scoped_organizations/org/{id}" ([L22596](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/index/operations.jsonl?plain=1#L22596)). Most do name one: 24,105 of the index's 29,983 rows (one row per operation per run) have the area shopify (counted by this review).

## 2. Areas and their borders

- **Wording.** Nothing found: the question is neutral. Its reading supplies the answer (see the last point).
- **Practice citations.** All are real and match what the file says:
  - Cervantes and Kazman, *Designing Software Architectures*, Addison-Wesley, 2016. Its contents list *Iteration 1: Establishing an Overall System Structure* (§4.3.2). I saw the contents through search only (sample: https://www.ciscopress.com/content/images/9780134390789/samplepages/9780134390789.pdf).
  - Parnas, *On the Criteria To Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules*, Communications of the ACM 15(12), 1972, pp. 1053–1058 (https://doi.org/10.1145/361598.361623). It says to start from the design decisions likely to change, with each module hiding one. I confirmed this through secondary quotations; the full-text copies returned errors.
  - Evans, *Domain-Driven Design*, Addison-Wesley, 2003. It covers bounded contexts and context maps. I confirmed this through Fowler, *BoundedContext*, martinfowler.com (https://martinfowler.com/bliki/BoundedContext.html; its year, 2014, is cited from memory).
- **Needed first.** **Reading:** the rule for how one area uses another's data is needed first. The list of areas, and any split into services, can wait. Shopify: "The best time to refactor and re-architect is as late as possible" ([Deconstructing the Monolith, 2019, L338](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L338)). Adding the rule later was costly: for one package, "our effort to reach full isolation had taken many months of hard work" ([A Packwerk Retrospective, 2024, L55](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L55)).
- **Not borne out:**
  - *areas meet only through each other's published interfaces, never through each other's tables* (a reading in the file). This was Shopify's aim in 2019 ([L338](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L338)), not its shape. A 2021 post: "our database is still common for all domains" ([Shopify-Made Patterns, L222](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L222)). A 2024 post: "privacy checks were removed from Packwerk with the release of version 3.0", and "domains and the boundaries between them do not reflect the way" Shopify's code runs ([L55](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L55)).
  - *the tool that holds the borders*. Shopify credits Packwerk only with "holding the line against new dependencies at the base layer" ([L55](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L55)).
  - *staff permissions are grouped by the same areas*. The 21 categories are the "admin staff permission selector" (*the capture's*, [REGISTER, L132](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/REGISTER.tsv?plain=1#L132)). The code is divided differently: "We currently have 37 components in our main monolith" ([Under Deconstruction, 2020, L277](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L277)). No held source maps one onto the other.

## 3. Where a shop lives

- **Wording.** The question's *how is a shop moved, so that shops fail apart rather than together* gives Shopify's answer: movable shops in isolated pods. **Reading:** a neutral question would be: how are tenants' data and work placed, and how far must one tenant's load or failure be kept from others?
- **Practice citations.** All are real and match what the file says:
  - AWS, *Reducing the Scope of Impact with Cell-Based Architecture*, 20 September 2023 (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/reducing-scope-of-impact-with-cell-based-architecture/reducing-scope-of-impact-with-cell-based-architecture.html): cells, chosen by a partition key, behind a thin router.
  - AWS, *SaaS Tenant Isolation Strategies*, 1 August 2020 (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/whitepapers/latest/saas-tenant-isolation-strategies/saas-tenant-isolation-strategies.html): the silo, pool and bridge models.
  - Helland, *Life beyond Distributed Transactions*, CIDR 2007 (https://www.ics.uci.edu/~cs223/papers/cidr07p15.pdf): entities with unique keys, no transaction across entities, and entities that move as a system scales. The paper's host returned errors, so I confirmed this through Colyer's quotations of it (https://blog.acolyer.org/2014/11/20/life-beyond-distributed-transactions/, 2014).
  - Homer, Sharp, Brader, Narumoto and Swanson, *Cloud Design Patterns*, Microsoft patterns & practices, January 2014 (https://msdn.microsoft.com/en-us/library/dn568099): it includes the Sharding pattern.
- **Needed first.** **Reading:** only its constraint is needed first: decision 1's key on every record a shop owns, and no work that spans shops. The placement mechanism can wait. Shopify ran on one database server until 2015: "In 2015, it was no longer possible to continue buying a larger database server for Shopify." Pods came after that: "In 2016 we sat down to reorganize" ([Pods, 2018, L369](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L369)). For its Shop app: "We were holding off on sharding the primary database" ([Shop app with Vitess, 2024, L56](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L56)).
- **Not borne out:** *The pod's location is also the place where a market's rule on where data is held can later go.* To meet Canada's rule, Shopify built "a new instance of Shopify, in an entirely new region". It also had to deal with systems outside the pods, its "storage and communications systems located outside of Canada, such as our data warehouse and network infrastructure" ([Cannabis in Canada, 2019, L340](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L340)).

## 4. Who may act, and what they may do

- **Wording.** The question gives Shopify's answer twice: who acts (*people, through an account and an organization; apps, through an installation*), and how their access is stated (*one permission model*). **Reading:** a neutral question would be: who can act on a tenant's data, and how is each actor's access stated and checked?
- **Practice citations.** All are real and match what the file says; one is superseded:
  - Sandhu, Coyne, Feinstein and Youman, *Role-Based Access Control Models*, IEEE Computer 29(2), February 1996, pp. 38–47 (https://doi.org/10.1109/2.485845).
  - ANSI INCITS 359-2004 is real but superseded. It was republished as INCITS 359-2012, reaffirmed in 2022, and ANSI lists the 2004 edition as historical (https://blog.ansi.org/ansi/role-based-access-control-rbac-incits-359/). The reference should name the 2012 edition.
  - Hardt (ed.), *The OAuth 2.0 Authorization Framework*, RFC 6749, October 2012, §3.3 *Access Token Scope* (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6749#section-3.3).
  - AWS 2022, as under decision 1: each signed-in user is bound to a tenant context.
- **Needed first.** **Reading:** each area must name its actions as permissions (and scopes, for apps) from its design onward. Roles, organizations and one login across shops could come later, as they did in Shopify, at the cost of a migration. Roles replaced permissions assigned "instead of assigning permissions directly" ([migrate to roles, L6](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/help-center/help.shopify.com@en-ar/md/en/manual/your-account/users/roles/migrate-to-roles.md?plain=1#L6)), and legacy access was "automatically converted to roles" from May 2025 ([L29](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/help-center/help.shopify.com@en-ar/md/en/manual/your-account/users/roles/migrate-to-roles.md?plain=1#L29)). **Reading:** logins were per store before the Shopify ID, since the page warns it isn't easy to "go back to separate logins" ([Shopify ID, L37](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/help-center/help.shopify.com@en-ar/md/en/manual/your-account/logging-in/sso-migration-guide.md?plain=1#L37)).
- **Not borne out:** *One tree covers every area*. The 142-permission tree is the "admin staff permission selector" (*the capture's*, [REGISTER, L132](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/REGISTER.tsv?plain=1#L132)). Shopify lists other kinds of permission besides: "Organization-level permissions apply to roles with the" organization category, and "Shopify POS app-level permissions apply to" POS roles ([Permissions, L5-L7](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/help-center/help.shopify.com@en-ar/md/en/manual/your-account/users/roles/permissions.md?plain=1#L5-L7)). The role the file cites for reaching several stores, the "Organization POS administrator", is in the ORGANIZATION category ([admin roles, L10](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/admin-roles/ctcbwa-r1/items.jsonl?plain=1#L10)). Apps use scopes, as the file's next bullet says. **Reading:** there is one role mechanism, with several permission sets.

## 5. Markets: the place for each market's values

- **Wording.** The title (*the place for each market's values*) and the question (*how is a buyer matched to one, and which of the shop's settings can a market hold differently*) give Shopify's answer: a market is a group of buyers that overrides the shop's settings. The founder uses *market* differently: *Each market's providers, law and limits are that market's setup* (the founder, `founder-decisions.tsv`, row of 1 November 2026). **Reading:** a neutral question would be: where do the values that differ by market live? And is a market a place Sharrwah is set up in, whose providers, law and limits apply to its shops; a group of buyers a shop sells to; or both? What a market is remains the founder's to decide; `CLAUDE.md` lists S-02's rulings on it as under review.
- **Practice citations.** All are real and match what the file says:
  - Krebs, Momm and Kounev, *Architectural Concerns in Multi-Tenant SaaS Applications*, CLOSER 2012, pp. 426–431 (https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2012/39576/). Customization is one of its concerns (§3.5). **Reading:** it means customization per tenant, not per market.
  - Mietzner, Metzger, Leymann and Pohl, PESOS 2009 (an ICSE workshop), pp. 18–25 (https://doi.org/10.1109/PESOS.2009.5068815): variability models for each tenant. I read only the abstract. The same reading applies.
  - Esselink, *A Practical Guide to Localization*, John Benjamins, 2000 (https://doi.org/10.1075/liwd.4). It has a chapter on internationalization; I saw its contents, not its text.
  - ISO 3166-1:2020 (https://www.iso.org/standard/72482.html): the current edition, confirmed in 2025.
- **Needed first.** **Reading:** a place for the values the founder's markets set (providers, law, limits) is needed before any area that holds them. Buyers' markets can wait. Shopify's monolith has been "under continuous development since at least 2006" ([L277](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/engineering-blog/20261031T004307Z-shopify-engineering-posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L277)). Selling "in up to 10 currencies" from one store became "available for all stores using Shopify Payments" on "June 19, 2019" ([changelog, L1535](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/merchant-changelog/changelog.shopify.com@posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L1535)), and the changelog has Shopify "introducing Shopify Markets" on "February 11, 2022" ([L1229](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/merchant-changelog/changelog.shopify.com@posts/items.jsonl?plain=1#L1229)).
- **Not borne out:** *the market is where every market's values go*. In Shopify, "A market is a set of customers that you define in your store." ([Markets overview, L82](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/help-center/help.shopify.com@en-ar/md/en/manual/markets/getting-started/overview.md?plain=1#L82)). Providers follow the store's own country: "which payment gateways are available in your store's location" ([gateway availability, L7](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/help-center/help.shopify.com@en-ar/md/en/manual/payments/third-party-providers/payment-gateway-availability.md?plain=1#L7)). So do law and limits: "Changing your store's country might affect the following details:", including "Your tax and duty obligations." and "Your access to Shopify products and services." ([store details, L56-L62](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/help-center/help.shopify.com@en-ar/md/en/manual/your-account/manage-orgs-and-stores/manage-store-details.md?plain=1#L56-L62)). Where a market does reach payment processing, it is through a business entity: Market "objects can be assigned to a business entity to determine payment processing" ([admin schema, L217](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/admin-graphql-schema/2026-10/items.jsonl?plain=1#L217)), and to sell from several entities, "your entities must be located in a" country where Shopify Payments is supported ([multiple entities, L12](https://github.com/Hamza-ye/observed-sh/blob/04de369da9ac84ae530ae80a55749167ff4139d4/structures/shopify/help-center/help.shopify.com@en-ar/md/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/onboarding/selling-with-multiple-entities.md?plain=1#L12)).
