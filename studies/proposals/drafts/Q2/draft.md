# Q2: What a place's rules and the platform's own choices change for a seller there, a draft proposal

## 1. How the platforms handle it

### [1]

- Shopify lists unsupported countries and regions, among them "The Crimea region of Ukraine, and the so-called Donetsk and Luhansk People’s Republics in Ukraine". Individuals and businesses there may not create a Shopify account, products originating there are prohibited, and so are orders shipping there. Q2-SH-K2275; Q2-SH-K2276; Q2-SH-K2278; Q2-SH-K2279
- WooCommerce's terms exclude anyone located or residing in a place "subject to comprehensive US sanctions (currently Cuba, Crimea, Iran, North Korea, Donetsk People’s Republic, Luhansk People’s Republic, and Syria)". BigCommerce's terms have the merchant warrant that it, its Relevant Parties and its customers are not nationals or residents of a region "subject to comprehensive U.S. sanctions", and conduct "sanctions screenings of your Relevant Parties and customers". WooCommerce and BigCommerce match on the same term; Shopify's list matches theirs by role, as places where the platform may not be used. All three are official pages. Q2-WC-K0526; Q2-BC-K0430; Q2-BC-K0431; Q2-SH-K2275
- Zid's sanctions clauses sit in the terms of Zid Pay, its payment service, and its Saudi and Kuwaiti terms list different countries: the Saudi terms name (إيران، سوريا، جنوب السودان، كوبا، ارتريا، جمهورية افريقيا الوسطى), the Kuwaiti terms more, among them اليمن. A clause outside Zid Pay restricting stores by country or by sanctions was not in what was read, nor who screens. Shopify Payments, too, has its own sanctions term, naming embargoes by "the United States, Canada, or the European Union". Q2-ZD-K0252; Q2-ZD-K0254; Q2-ZD-N01; Q2-ZD-G0055; Q2-SH-K2268
- Salla's reading didn't show a statement on sanctions screening, or on countries refused for sanctions. Q2-SA-N01
- Shopify Payments needs "a business located in your Designated Country", and is absent for countries its supported list doesn't name. WooPayments counts the country "where your business is registered — not the one in which you personally live", and leaves out outlying territories: a U.S. business must be located in "the 50 states, Washington D.C., or Puerto Rico". Zid Pay is for merchants of المملكة العربية السعودية و دولة الكويت. The three match by role, the seller's place that admits it to the platform's payment service, and differ on which place counts: where the business is located (Shopify), where it is registered (WooCommerce), the country of its merchants (Zid). Shopify's is from its terms, an official page; the others are help pages. Q2-SH-K1536; Q2-SH-A0002; Q2-WC-K0402; Q2-WC-K0407; Q2-ZD-K0005
- The payment company behind the service is set by place too. Shopify identifies, "for each Designated Country, (a) the Payment Processor(s)", and reserves "the right to change the Payment Processors"; the Stripe entity contracting with WooPayments sellers is "the entity specified below for User's Stripe Account Country"; Zid reserves the right to switch its payment processors (نحتفظ بالحق في تبديل موردي معالجة الدفع); Salla provides payments "عبر شركائها", and the page that would name them is held but not read. Shopify's processors and Stripe's entity match by role. All are official pages. Q2-SH-K1563; Q2-SH-K1561; Q2-WC-P0108; Q2-ZD-002; Q2-SA-P0079; Q2-SA-G0113
- Salla's UAE verification help (12 September 2026) asks for a bank account "لدى بنك إماراتي و بعملة الدرهم الإماراتي", while its agreement's en-ae page (6 October 2026) asks every store for "a bank account in a Saudi bank". The two disagree for UAE stores. The proposal rests on neither for what a UAE store needs, only on a bank account's place being set by place. Q2-SA-K1964; Q2-SA-035
- Shopify activates eligible payment methods "based on the regions where you sell", and shows each local method only "when a customer is in a supported country and selects an eligible currency". WooCommerce shows a local method only "if the customer billing country and the customer currency meet the requirements", hiding it otherwise "since the customer cannot use it anyway". BigCommerce makes local methods depend on "your store’s country and currency combination". They match by role, all help pages, and differ on whose place counts: the store's markets, or the buyer's place and currency (Shopify, WooCommerce); the store's country and currency (BigCommerce). Q2-SH-K1745; Q2-SH-K1743; Q2-WC-029; Q2-WC-039; Q2-BC-038
- Providers are named per country, and some by the seller's legal form. Zid lists its gateways by country, Tap for merchants in six Gulf countries. WooPayments offers only the Business account type in the UAE; Przelewy24 "no longer supports individual business types" from 6 August 2026 (not checked on Zid or BigCommerce); Zid Pay's KNET network is "متاحة للمؤسسات والشركات فقط". Q2-ZD-K0087; Q2-WC-K0411; Q2-WC-021; Q2-ZD-K0042
- The platforms also choose among providers themselves. Activating Zid Pay turns off the store's other payment gateway. Salla's dashboard texts, as a recorded session loaded them on 8 October 2026, say Salla Payments can't be activated while a Tap link is active (not checked on BigCommerce). These match by role, one gateway shutting out another, from Zid's terms and Salla's observed texts. Q2-ZD-K0088; Q2-SA-K1653
- Shopify charges Transaction Fees on sales through "all payment providers other than Shopify Payments", and BigCommerce a monthly fee on self-service plans for orders processed through Open Payment Providers. They match by role, both official pages. Q2-SH-K1337; Q2-BC-K0143
- Salla's onboarding page (store store-1-da, observed 8 October 2026) says "اختيارك للدولة يحدِّد المتطلبات القانونية اللازمة لمتجرك", and asks the seller: "اختر الدولة التي تصدر منها وثائقك الحكومية لتهيئة متجرك وفقًا لأنظمتها". Its options were six Gulf countries and دولة أخرى. Salla's freelance document is "للمواطنين السعوديين فقط". Q2-SA-061; Q2-SA-062; Q2-SA-063; Q2-SA-K0084
- Zid Pay's documents follow the entity type (فرد – شركة – مؤسسة) and the place: a foreign company adds an investment licence (ترخيص الاستثمار), and the Nafath check runs "باسم مزود الخدمة". Salla's help says the store's country sets its government documents. Salla's and Zid's document rules match by role, both help pages. Q2-ZD-K0003; Q2-ZD-017; Q2-ZD-K0263; Q2-SA-K0083
- Shopify Payments' prohibited and restricted business categories "may be imposed by law or through the Payment Network Rules, by Shopify or the requirements of a Payment Processor's Financial Services Providers", and Shopify's list "varies depending on your region". WooPayments names card networks, its payment processors and their financial service providers, on a WooPayments policy page: whether such pages count among WooCommerce's terms is open. Zid Pay bars dealings "محظورة من زد باي أو المملكة العربية السعودية أو بلد النشاط". They match by role, from terms (Shopify, Zid) and help pages (Shopify, WooCommerce), and overlap in part: Shopify adds the law and itself, Zid the country of the activity. Q2-SH-K2262; Q2-SH-K1541; Q2-WC-K0416; Q2-ZD-K0251
- Where its own service refuses a category, Shopify points to others: Shopify "has partnered with integrated third-party payment providers that support the sale of these products" for hemp, approval being "at the discretion of each third-party payment provider". WooPayments sends CBD sellers to Square or Viva Wallet. These match by role. Q2-SH-002; Q2-SH-003; Q2-WC-K0417
- Managed Markets hides a product prohibited in a destination country "from the catalog for customers in that country" (not checked on Zid). Q2-SH-019
- On Shopify, the requirement for each additional address field "depends on the customer's country or region and can't be changed" (not checked on BigCommerce). Salla's dashboard texts (recorded 8 October 2026) let a store require the national address of customers in Saudi Arabia, calling it mandatory from الهيئة العامة للنقل, with the seller who turns it off bearing the consequences. Zid lets the seller set address fields per country (لتخصيص العنوان حسب الدولة). Shopify's privacy settings are set "for each country and region where you sell to", BigCommerce's cookie consent by "a storefront-level setting". Q2-SH-032; Q2-SA-K0800; Q2-SA-K0798; Q2-ZD-022; Q2-SH-K2134; Q2-BC-K0205
- Shopify's tax services "depend on the location of your business", and Shopify Tax calculates only for orders whose destination is in the United States, the European Union, the United Kingdom or Canada. BigCommerce takes tax engines through its Tax Provider API. Salla's ZATCA link is for stores in Saudi Arabia only. Q2-SH-K1865; Q2-SH-K1867; Q2-BC-K0381; Q2-SA-K1936
- Zid Pay's Saudi deposits stop on official Saudi holidays. Salla's agreement subjects its transfer of merchants' funds to the Saudi Central Bank, and warns of delays from its restrictions. Q2-ZD-P0034; Q2-SA-P0090

Q2-SH-058; Q2-SH-064

## 2. Proposed: names, structure and behaviour

- Everything in this section is Sharrwah's proposed design, not what a platform does. Its core is one mechanism: each thing that differs between places is a **Place rule** on a **Place**, at whatever level the evidence shows things differing. Q2-SH-K2275; Q2-WC-K0496; Q2-BC-K0425
- **Place**: a node in one tree: a group of countries, a country, or an area within a country, under a root standing for every place not set up otherwise. A node with no setting of its own takes its nearest ancestor's. Q2-SH-K2275; Q2-WC-K0407; Q2-WC-K0496; Q2-WC-055
  - Draws on: areas within a country (Shopify's Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk; WooPayments' states, Washington D.C. and Puerto Rico); a group (WooCommerce's Designated Countries); defaults for places not listed, by row (Shopify's all other jurisdictions; BigCommerce's "Any other jurisdiction") and by region (Stripe's Regional Terms for unlisted countries), which the tree's inheritance combines. Q2-SH-K2275; Q2-WC-K0407; Q2-WC-K0496; Q2-SH-K1248; Q2-BC-K0425; Q2-WC-055
  - Why: rule 1, with no level fixed in advance, as the platforms set things at all three levels. Q2-SH-K2275; Q2-WC-K0496
  - Not taken: a flat list of countries (Salla's onboarding options; Zid Pay's two countries), which holds neither an area within a country nor a group. Q2-SA-063; Q2-ZD-K0005
- **Place reference**: which of a party's places a **Place rule** reads. A seller's are kept apart (where its business is registered, where it is located, the country issuing its documents, its billing address, where it resides), as are a buyer's billing country and shipping destination, a product's origin, and the regions a store sells to. Q2-WC-K0402; Q2-SH-K1243; Q2-SA-062; Q2-WC-029; Q2-SH-K2278
  - Draws on: WooPayments' registered country beside WooCommerce's contracting company by residence; Shopify's contracting party by billing address beside Shopify Payments' business located in its Designated Country; Salla's issuing country (observed); the buyer's billing country (WooCommerce), an order's destination and a product's origin (Shopify); the regions where a Shopify store sells. Q2-WC-K0402; Q2-WC-K0499; Q2-SH-K1243; Q2-SH-K1536; Q2-SA-062; Q2-WC-029; Q2-SH-K2279; Q2-SH-K2278; Q2-SH-K1745
  - Why: rule 2, which place a rule reads is likely to change, so it is a field of the rule. Q2-WC-K0402
  - Not taken: one store country for every rule, since one platform reads two seller places for two services. Q2-WC-K0402; Q2-WC-K0499
- **Place rule**: one thing Sharrwah offers, restricts, withholds, requires or does differently at a **Place**: its **Place reference**, the point where it acts (sign-up, verification, payment setup, storefront, checkout, shipping, payout, data storage), its source, its setter, its stated reason, and its dates. Q2-SH-K2262; Q2-ZD-K0251; Q2-WC-021; Q2-SA-061
  - Draws on: the sources the platforms name: law, Payment Network Rules, the platform, processors' financial service providers (Shopify; WooPayments' policy page), the place of the activity (Zid Pay); a provider's dated change (Przelewy24, not checked on Zid or BigCommerce); a reason shown where the rule acts (Salla's onboarding, observed). Q2-SH-K2262; Q2-WC-K0416; Q2-ZD-K0251; Q2-WC-021; Q2-SA-061
  - Why: rule 3, the data model is expensive to change after launch, so these are fields from the first build; how reasons show is left to the build. Q2-SA-061; Q2-WC-021
- **Restricted places**: lists of places where Sharrwah refuses business, each entry with its regime and its effects (opening an account, reaching a storefront, a product's origin, an order's destination, using a service), held per **Contracting entity** and per service. Screening named persons is a step whose doer is set per company. Q2-SH-K2276; Q2-SH-K2279; Q2-ZD-K0254; Q2-BC-K0431
  - Draws on: Shopify's effects; Zid Pay's Saudi and Kuwaiti lists, which differ; Shopify Payments' own sanctions term beside Shopify's list; BigCommerce's screening by the merchant. Q2-SH-K2278; Q2-ZD-K0252; Q2-ZD-K0254; Q2-SH-K2268; Q2-BC-K0431
  - Why: rules 3 and 4, which regimes bind Sharrwah depends on where its company is based and on its providers (section 7). Q2-ZD-K0254; Q2-SH-K2268
  - Not taken: one list for the whole platform (Shopify's list; WooCommerce's terms), since Zid's lists differ by contracting place. Q2-WC-K0526; Q2-ZD-K0252
- **Contracting entity**: the Sharrwah company a seller contracts with, per **Place** on the **Place reference** Sharrwah's terms choose, and per service (the platform, a payment service, a regulated activity), each row with its governing law, forum and prevailing language. The root's row covers every other place. Q2-SH-K1243; Q2-SH-K1615; Q2-BC-K0419; Q2-BC-K0425
  - Draws on: Shopify's table by billing address and its Canadian company for regulated payment activities; BigCommerce's table with law per row; WooCommerce's split by residence; Salla's and Zid's single Saudi company, here a one-row table; the prevailing language (Arabic at Salla, English at BigCommerce). Q2-SH-K1248; Q2-SH-K1615; Q2-BC-K0425; Q2-WC-K0499; Q2-SA-033; Q2-ZD-K0212; Q2-SA-030; Q2-BC-015
  - Why: rule 3, legal structure is expensive to change, so the table exists from the first build even with one row; rule 1, a company for a new place is then set up, not built. Q2-SA-033; Q2-BC-K0425
  - Not taken: one company fixed for every place, as Salla's agreement and its en-ae page read, since a second company would then need building. Q2-SA-033; Q2-SA-020
- **Provider**: an outside company whose service plugs in (payments, instalments, shipping, tax, identity checks, financing, messaging, hosting), with its own **Availability**: **Place rules** on the seller's place, the buyer's place, the currency and the seller's legal form. The provider behind a service at a place can change without the service changing. Q2-SH-K1563; Q2-SH-K1561; Q2-WC-029; Q2-ZD-K0042
  - Draws on: Shopify's processors per Designated Country, which it may change; Stripe's entity per account country; availability by the buyer's place and currency (Shopify, WooCommerce), the store's country and currency (BigCommerce), legal form (WooPayments in the UAE; Zid Pay's KNET); gateways by country (Zid). Q2-SH-K1563; Q2-SH-K1561; Q2-WC-P0108; Q2-SH-K1743; Q2-BC-038; Q2-WC-K0411; Q2-ZD-K0087
  - Sharrwah's own choices among providers, one turning another off (Zid Pay; Salla Payments, observed) or a fee on providers other than its own (Shopify; BigCommerce), are **Place rules** whose source is Sharrwah; whether to have any is a company decision. Q2-ZD-K0088; Q2-SA-K1653; Q2-SH-K1337; Q2-BC-K0143
  - Why: rule 2, each provider behind its own part, as providers change by place and date; rule 1. Q2-SH-K1561; Q2-WC-021
- **Provider agreement**: the seller's own agreement with a **Provider**, named per **Place**. Sharrwah is not a party by default. Q2-SH-K1580; Q2-WC-P0103
  - Draws on: agreements all five platforms aren't party to (Shopify's Payment Processor Terms; Stripe's Connected Account Agreement; BigCommerce Payments with PayPal; Salla's contracts with providers; Zid's logistics agreements). Q2-SH-K1580; Q2-WC-P0103; Q2-BC-P0040; Q2-SA-004; Q2-ZD-K0347
  - Provided for, not by default: Sharrwah as a party, as a Shopify affiliate acts as Payment Facilitator in certain Designated Countries. If that would hold sellers' money through Sharrwah, by Sharrwah or by a payment company in an account opened through Sharrwah, it goes live only by the founder's decision. Q2-SH-K1603
  - Why: rule 3, partner contracts; rule 4, whether Sharrwah is a party stays open. Q2-SH-K1580; Q2-SH-K1603
- **Seller requirements**: documents and checks per **Place** (on the seller's **Place reference**), legal form and service, each with its source; identity checks run through identity **Providers** per place. Q2-SA-K0083; Q2-ZD-K0003; Q2-ZD-K0263
  - Draws on: Salla's documents by the store's country and its freelance document for Saudi citizens only; Zid Pay's documents by entity type, its investment licence for foreign companies, its Nafath check in the provider's name. They sit in the place's set, apart from the terms' text, where Salla's agreement and UAE help disagree. Q2-SA-K0083; Q2-SA-K0084; Q2-ZD-K0003; Q2-ZD-017; Q2-ZD-K0263; Q2-SA-035; Q2-SA-K1964
  - Why: rules 1 and 2. Q2-ZD-K0263
- **Restricted goods**: what may not be sold, or only through named providers, per source and per **Place reference** (the seller's place, the buyer's destination), with its effects: refuse the store, refuse a provider, send a category to providers that accept it, hide a product in a destination. Q2-SH-K2262; Q2-SH-002; Q2-SH-019
  - Draws on: the sources Shopify, WooPayments and Zid Pay name; Shopify's list varying by region; Shopify's hemp providers and WooPayments' Square or Viva Wallet for CBD; Managed Markets hiding a product (not checked on Zid). Q2-SH-K1541; Q2-WC-K0416; Q2-ZD-K0251; Q2-SH-002; Q2-WC-K0417; Q2-SH-019
  - Why: rule 1. Q2-SH-K1541
- **Buyer-place rules**: **Place rules** on the buyer's place at storefront and checkout: which payment methods and products show, which address fields are required, the consent banner, and the message explaining a refusal. Some Sharrwah fixes from a place's law, some the seller sets per place. Q2-SH-032; Q2-SA-K0800; Q2-SH-K2134
  - Draws on: Shopify's address fields by the customer's country, which can't be changed; Salla's national address for customers in Saudi Arabia, which the seller may turn off at its own risk (observed); Zid's address fields per country set by the seller; Shopify's privacy settings per country beside BigCommerce's per storefront. Q2-SH-032; Q2-SA-K0798; Q2-ZD-022; Q2-SH-K2134; Q2-BC-K0205
  - Why: rule 1. Q2-SH-032; Q2-ZD-022
- **Data location**: where a store's data, orders and customers' personal data are held, per **Place** on the seller's **Place reference**, with the root's location by default, shown to the seller; with, per place, who is controller and who processor, and the basis for any transfer abroad. Q2-SH-K2321; Q2-SH-031; Q2-BC-031; Q2-BC-K0436
  - Draws on: Shopify's storage in Europe by default for new European merchants, shown in its admin; BigCommerce's regional hosting, and its processor and controller roles; WooCommerce's controller by service and location; Zid's transfers abroad under safeguards. Q2-SH-K2321; Q2-SH-031; Q2-BC-031; Q2-BC-K0436; Q2-WC-K0502; Q2-ZD-K0283
  - Why: rule 3, where data sits is part of the data model and the infrastructure, expensive to change after launch, so a store's data is placeable by region from the first build; the regions stay open. Q2-BC-031; Q2-SH-K2321
  - Not taken: one location for every place with no setting, as Shopify and BigCommerce set it per place. Q2-SH-K2321; Q2-BC-031
- **Payout settings**: per payment **Provider** and **Place**, the bank account's country and currency, the schedule, the minimum and the holiday calendar. Q2-SA-K1964; Q2-ZD-P0034
  - Draws on: Salla's UAE verification asking for an Emirati bank in dirhams; Zid Pay's deposits stopping on official Saudi holidays; Salla's agreement tying its fund transfers to the Saudi Central Bank. Q2-SA-K1964; Q2-ZD-P0034; Q2-SA-P0090
  - The guard: any arrangement holding sellers' money through Sharrwah, by Sharrwah or by a payment company in an account opened through Sharrwah, is provided for only as an option left off, and goes live at a place only by the founder's decision. Q2-SA-P0090
