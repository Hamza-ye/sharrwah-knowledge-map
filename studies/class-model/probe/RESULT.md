Counted by `probe.py rates` from the four checkers' verdicts, against the rule in `README.md`, which was fixed before any result. All four checkers read only their items, their claims and the sources (an audit of their tool calls found no read of any other working file).

**By the rule: usable as an index.**
- 1 of the 100 cells is wrong in its strongest placement (Shopify's fulfillment orders: the three lines it rests on are API access scopes for fulfillment-service apps, which supports extension-point, not core). None is wrong in capability.
- No ruling fails. The most any ruling has wrong is 2 of its 5 lines.

**What the rule doesn't decide, and matters:**
- **Conditions.** 12 of the 100 cells, and 7 of the 70 ruling lines, have a condition missing or wrong: a plan, a beta, a feature flag, a market such as "U.S. merchants only". One ruling drops a parent route's gate from the condition by design, and 4 of its 5 lines were judged wrong for that. Read a condition at its source, not from the model.
- **"Not held" and "not stated".** 4 of the 100 cells say something isn't held or isn't stated when the cited source shows it.
- **The rulings' own conventions.** 10 of the 70 ruling lines are wrong in placement or capability. 8 of the 10 are a convention of the model against a plain reading: a feature behind a flag, a beta or a preview placed as shows; a platform's own category of suggested providers placed as shows; a partner's request form placed as shows; a maker claimed from another statement. 2 are plain misreadings. They rarely change a cell's strongest placement, since a cell takes it from many lines.
- **Shopify alone:** 64 items. 46 stand. 5 are wrong in placement (3 by those conventions, 2 plain misreadings), 1 in capability, and 10 in a condition.

**What it doesn't measure:** whether the model is the right instrument for building. It measures whether its readings match their sources.
