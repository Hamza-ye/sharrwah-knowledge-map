# Build Yemen payments around roles, not wallets

Sharrwah can launch in Yemen without a payments licence only if it never receives buyers' money. In the government-held south, the Aden central bank's 2022 rules require any non-bank that manages "collection and/or payment of electronic funds" or initiates payments for others to be a licensed joint-stock company with **YER 500m paid-up capital** (about US$317,000 at October 2026 Aden rates), and the texts contain no aggregator, payment-facilitator or closed-loop exemption. That leaves two launch positions, recording outcomes that sellers, couriers or rails establish and integrating wallets and banks on each seller's own merchant account, plus a realistic third step in which a licensed wallet or bank holds the funds, settles each seller and deducts Sharrwah's fee, as the rules expressly allow. The analysis only holds when the unit is the **setup option**, meaning one configuration of roles over a rail (who captures the buyer's proof, who confirms the payment, who holds the money, and where Sharrwah stands), because a single bank, Al-Kuraimi, supports four configurations with different licence consequences, and a wallet licensed in Sana'a counts as unlicensed in Aden. Yemen is in effect two payment markets: old rials trade at about **533 per dollar** in the Houthi-held north and new rials at about **1,576** in the south (8 October 2026), two central banks license against each other, US designations reach northern banks and exchange houses, and commerce still runs on cash on delivery (preferred by 74% in one Aden survey) and on "pay my number and send a screenshot". Of the **17 setup options** catalogued here, nine need no Sharrwah licence (five where it only records, four integrations on sellers' own accounts), three need a licensed partner to agree terms that no Yemeni rail publishes, three need Sharrwah's own licence, and two hinge on open legal questions. Sharrwah's model therefore has to go beyond the Shopify-derived one in five places: money tagged with zone and note edition, payment outcomes tagged with who vouches for them and on what evidence, custodians that include couriers and exchange networks, rail adapters that collect one-time passwords and poll for outcomes, and Sharrwah's position recorded per payment setup so it can move up, or fall back, without rewriting orders.

## Analyse the setup option, not the wallet

"Which wallets should we integrate?" produces the wrong map in Yemen, because one provider supports several configurations with different consequences. Al-Kuraimi is the clearest case. A buyer can transfer to a seller's Kuraimi account or "Hasib point" number and send a screenshot, as Sana'a stores already ask ([da5m](https://da5m.com/pages/pay)); the seller confirms receipt and Sharrwah only records it. A seller with its own Kuraimi business account and e-payment credentials can let Sharrwah's checkout call the bank's payment API, so the bank confirms the debit synchronously and the money lands in the seller's account ([Kuraimi API service](https://kuraimibank.com/ar/services/50); [kuraimiepay client](https://github.com/Alsharie/kuraimiepay)). If Sharrwah is instead the listed store and the money lands in Sharrwah's company account, Sharrwah becomes a holder of other people's funds, an activity CBY-Aden licenses ([PSP instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf)). And if Kuraimi opened an account for each seller onboarded through Sharrwah and settled to them directly, the bank would hold the money and Sharrwah never would, an arrangement Kuraimi does not offer publicly. That is **one provider, four licence positions and three different parties confirming the payment**.

"Method" fails the same way. "Mobile wallet" covers a merchant-initiated debit confirmed by a one-time password (OTP), where the rail confirms the outcome; a buyer-made voucher the merchant redeems, where funds are held under the buyer's control until redemption; and a personal transfer plus screenshot, where the screenshot is only the buyer's claim and the seller confirms by checking their own balance. These have different reversal paths and different fraud exposure. The licence follows the activity, and the activity is a property of the configuration: CBY-Aden defines "payment services" by what is done (cash deposit and withdrawal, money transfer, payment initiation, management of collection and payment of electronic funds), not by brand or instrument ([PSP instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf)).

Provider lists also go stale faster than a platform can build against them. In June 2024 CBY-Aden barred its banks and exchangers from dealing with **12 Sana'a-based wallets** ([CBY-Aden circular](https://cby-ye.com/files/667c32dabd223.pdf)). The US designated Yemen Kuwait Bank, which is linked to the Floosak wallet, in January 2025 ([US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2794)) and the International Bank of Yemen in April 2025 ([US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0092)). Eight banks announced in March 2025 that they were moving their headquarters to Aden ([CBY-Aden](https://english.cby-ye.com/news/187)), and in June 2025 the Sana'a central bank ordered institutions to stop dealing with Al-Kuraimi ([Sana'a Center](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2025/25056)). A setup option survives this churn: when a partner is cut off, the option still exists, and Sharrwah can swap the provider or drop to a lower-exposure option on the same rail. The zone belongs to the configuration too, since the same wallet can be lawful on one side of the front line and banned on the other.

A setup option is therefore **one configuration of roles over a rail in one authority zone**, described on fixed dimensions: the arrangement and rail; who acquires the buyer's proof; who establishes the outcome and how the platform learns it; whether funds can be reserved before they are taken; the reversal and dispute path; who holds the money, for how long and how it reaches the seller; Sharrwah's position and the licence or partner it needs; currency and zone coverage; cost, limits and timing; risks; and confidence in the evidence. These are the arrangement dimensions proposed in PAY-08, with three variables made explicit: the outcome source of PAY-14, the custodian of PAY-24 and Sharrwah's own money role from PAY-42. PAY-42 assumed one role for the whole platform. Here the role is recorded per payment setup, because Sharrwah can be record-only on cash on delivery while it acts as an integrator for one wallet in the same week. The five positions below recur through the rest of the report.

| Sharrwah's position | What Sharrwah does with the money | What it needs in the Aden zone | What it needs in the Sana'a zone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Record-only** | Records outcomes that sellers, couriers or rails establish. Never receives funds and never initiates a debit. | No CBY licence on the texts read. Needs commercial and tax registration. Must not call itself a "payment service" without approval ([PSP instructions §3](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf)). | Commercial registration with the Sana'a ministry, which revoked 4,225 commercial-agency licences in April 2026 ([Sana'a Center](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2026/28066)). Whether Sana'a's e-payment rules reach software platforms is unknown. |
| **Technical integrator on the seller's own account** | Runs checkout and calls the rail with each seller's own credentials. Money lands in the seller's account. | No licence for Sharrwah on the texts. Sharrwah is a "third party" to a licensed issuer, which needs prior CBY approval for outsourcing and for linking with other parties ([e-money instructions §5](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)). Initiating a debit from the buyer's wallet comes close to licensable "payment initiation" (inference). | Depends on the partner, which holds a Sana'a licence that Aden does not recognise. |
| **Aggregator or collection agent under a licensed partner** | Onboards sellers and runs collection. The partner holds the funds, settles each seller and deducts Sharrwah's fee. | The partner's PSP or e-money licence, CBY approval of the arrangement, and no exclusivity (§5(12)). The rules let the issuer deduct agreed fees from payments ([e-money instructions, fees](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)). | The Sana'a framework of Decision 1/2020; detail not public. |
| **Holder of funds** | Receives buyers' money, electronic or cash on delivery, and pays sellers later. | A PSP licence: joint-stock company, YER 500m paid-up capital, main data centre in Yemen, settlement bank, AML manual, 60-day decision, then a 6-month preliminary licence before a 5-year final one ([PSP instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf)). | A Sana'a licence for non-banks is possible, but Aden contests it and it carries US sanctions exposure ([Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)). |
| **Issuer of stored value** | Keeps balances usable across sellers, such as a wallet or platform-wide credit. | A PSP licence plus an e-money licence: 100% trust account owned by holders, capital of at least 25% of the float, no interest ([e-money instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)). | As above. |

Restated at this level, the founder's research question becomes reusable for any market, zone or date:

> **For each authority zone Z of a market at date T: which setup options can discharge a buyer's obligation to a seller? A setup option is a rail together with who acquires the buyer's proof, who establishes the outcome, who holds the money and for how long, how it reaches the seller, and which position Sharrwah occupies. For each option, what does it allow (reserving, reversing, currencies, limits, timing) and what does it require (licence, partner, seller onboarding, sanctions screening)? Which options can Sharrwah support from its current position, which next, and what evidence or event moves it up a position, or forces it back down?**

In working form the question has four steps: list every combination of rail, role assignment and Sharrwah position in each zone; describe each combination on the fixed dimensions; mark which combinations are lawful and available from Sharrwah's current position; and name the trigger and precondition for every move between positions. The rest of this report applies that template to Yemen as of 9 October 2026.

## Yemen is two payment markets with one country code

The decade to 2026 split Yemen's payment system into two rival legal orders over two physically distinct banknote series. The government moved the central bank's head office from Sana'a to Aden in 2016, and since then two central banks have issued separate rules, neither with the parliamentary authority to pass new financial law ([Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)). Between 2024 and 2026 each side used licensing to cut off the other's institutions, and in 2025 the US designated the Houthi movement (Ansarallah) as a Foreign Terrorist Organization (FTO), banks moved their headquarters south, and the two rials settled at roughly three to one. The subsections below cover the conditions that decide which setup options exist: regulation, the split, sanctions, wallets, banks and exchange houses, cards, commerce practice and currency.

### Aden's rules license activities, and collecting money for others is one of them

The statutory base predates the split, and both sides still cite it. **Law No. 40 of 2006** gives electronic contracts and signatures legal effect and makes electronic fund transfer "an accepted means of making payment", without reserving payments to banks or penalising unlicensed payment activity as such ([Law 40/2006](http://www.yemen-nic.info/db/laws_ye/detail.php?ID=18872)); Central Bank Law No. 14 of 2000 and AML/CFT Law No. 1 of 2010 complete the base. The working rules are central-bank instruments, and CBY-Aden's set is fairly complete. Its **PSP instructions of 14 September 2022** define payment services to include cash deposit and withdrawal, money transfer, payment initiation and **"management of collection and/or payment of electronic funds"**. They forbid providing these services without a licence, registering a company with that purpose, or using the words "payment services" in a name or printed material without approval, and they require a joint-stock company with **YER 500m paid-up capital** (YER 750m for a payment-system operator), a main data centre in Yemen, and CBY approval before any function is outsourced ([CBY-Aden PSP instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf)).

E-money needs a second licence on top of the PSP licence. The **non-bank e-money instructions of 8 August 2023** require a **100% trust account** at a settlement bank, owned by the holders and reconciled daily, capital of at least 25% of e-money outstanding, no exclusivity, and systems ready for interoperability (ISO 20022). They expressly permit "purchase of goods and services from merchants", which issuers report monthly, and they let issuer and user agree to deduct fees from the payment amount ([CBY-Aden e-money instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)). **Decision No. 4 of 2025 (9 July 2025)** raised the caps on individual accounts with non-bank wallets, let issuers hold USD, SAR and AED while banning conversion from rials into foreign currency, and left caps on merchant (legal-entity) accounts to each issuer with prior CBY approval ([Decision 4/2025](https://cby-ye.com/files/6875fc55a253e.pdf)). Dollar equivalents below are my arithmetic.

| Cap on individual non-bank wallet accounts (Aden, from July 2025) | YER | Approx. US$ at 1,576 |
|---|---|---|
| Per transaction | **1,000,000** | 635 |
| Per day | 2,000,000 | 1,269 |
| Per month | 5,000,000 | 3,173 |
| Maximum balance | 8,000,000 | 5,076 |

Three further Aden instruments bear on a platform: e-KYC rules (November 2023) that banks and e-money PSPs may use only with prior CBY approval ([e-KYC instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/65806fe2b3757.pdf)); a November 2025 circular requiring banks and exchangers to screen customers against UN, OFAC, EU and UK lists ([Circular 4/2025](https://cby-ye.com/files/690e0e39ce5d9.pdf)); and a September 2026 crypto ban that explicitly covers PSPs, wallets and their agents ([Circular 1/2026](https://cby-ye.com/files/6aaa38b226baa.pdf)). The licensed perimeter is small: in the first half of 2025 CBY-Aden counted **nine e-wallets with 2.1 million subscribers**, 791 POS terminals and 2.34 million cards, 98% of them debit ([CBY-Aden H1 2025 report](https://cby-ye.com/files/693a7845e7f26.pdf)).

Two features of the Aden regime shape Sharrwah's options. It has no payment-facilitator, sub-merchant or limited-network category, so holding sellers' money or issuing cross-seller credit lands squarely in a licensed activity. And its fee-deduction clause lets a licensed wallet net Sharrwah's commission, so a partner-held collection model can be compliant without Sharrwah touching funds. That second point is an inference from the definitions, since no CBY guidance on marketplaces was found. Sana'a's regime is far less documented: CBY-Sana'a's Decision No. 1 of 2020 opened e-money issuance to exchange companies, traders and non-financial firms with no cap on issuance, but its full text is not public and CBY-Aden calls it illegitimate ([Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)). Neither side has an e-commerce law or a data-protection law in the sources found. General sales tax is 5%, with registration from YER 50m turnover, per a January 2022 professional summary not re-verified for 2026 ([Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.global/en/insights/indirect-tax-guide/indirect-tax---Yemen/)). That threshold is worth about US$32,000 in new rials but about US$94,000 in old rials, so even statutory amounts are ambiguous about which rial they mean (my arithmetic).

### Since 2024 each central bank has used licensing against the other

On **26 June 2024** CBY-Aden made two moves. **Decision 23/2024** forced every new domestic cash transfer by exchange companies onto its Unified Money Network (UNMONEY), exempting licensed e-wallets and PSPs within their ceilings ([CBY-Aden](https://cby-ye.com/news/706)). **Circular CBY/L/282/2024** barred Aden-supervised banks and exchangers from dealing with 12 wallets and payment services operating from Sana'a: Cash, Al-Dawli Money, Jawali, Floosak, Saba Cash, Mobile Money, Yemen Wallet, E-Rial, Rial Mobile, Jaib, WeCash and Al-Mutakamila ([circular](https://cby-ye.com/files/667c32dabd223.pdf)). The Sana'a exchangers' association had already told its members not to deal with UNMONEY ([Yeni Yemen](https://yeniyemen.net/p-271984)), and the Houthis banned dealings with 13 Aden-aligned banks ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/yemen/al-houthis-yemen-govt-reach-financial-de-escalation-deal-un-envoy-1.103583412)). A UN-brokered deal on **23 July 2024** cancelled the measures both sides had taken against banks ([The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/houthis-yemeni-government-reach-financial-de-escalation-deal)).

The FTO designation reopened the fight. On **15 March 2025** eight banks, including Tadhamon, Al-Kuraimi and Shamil, told CBY-Aden they were moving their headquarters to Aden ([CBY-Aden](https://english.cby-ye.com/news/187)). On **22 June 2025** CBY-Sana'a ordered institutions to stop dealing with Al-Kuraimi, which held 91% of active microfinance depositors and runs the mFloos wallet, and to liquidate balances within 15 days ([Sana'a Center](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2025/25056)). A June 2025 report that Aden lifted the 12-wallet ban under Saudi pressure cites no instrument, and the circular is still posted ([Al-Mushahid](https://almushahid.net/131223/)).

The two sides are now building separate infrastructure. CBY-Aden adopted a binding national QR standard on 29 March 2026 ([CBY-Aden](https://cby-ye.com/news/922)) and in May 2026 licensed the Yemen Payments and Clearing Company (YPCC) to run a fast payment system whose launch "may take several months", while the Houthi authorities run their own fast payment system that works only in their areas ([Sana'a Center](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2026/28066)). Freezes now happen inside government areas too: in mid-June 2026 the Aden prosecutor froze all funds of the Southern Transitional Council (STC), the southern separatist movement, at banks and exchange companies (same source). **No partner can be assumed lawful and operational in both zones at once**, and either authority can cut any partner off at short notice.

### US designations make northern counterparties a compliance hazard

The US designated Ansarallah a Specially Designated Global Terrorist effective **16 February 2024** and a Foreign Terrorist Organization on **4 March 2025** ([US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0092)). Knowingly providing material support to an FTO is a US crime, and financial institutions must freeze and report funds in which it has an interest ([Baker McKenzie](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/us-redesignates-ansarallah-as-a-foreign-terrorist-organization/)). The designations reach payment infrastructure directly. **Yemen Kuwait Bank**, designated on 17 January 2025, co-owns the Quality Connect e-rial platform behind Floosak and is named by Jawali as a partner bank ([Willkie](https://complianceconcourse.willkie.com/articles/united-states-sanctions-yemen-kuwait-bank-for-supporting-the-houthis/); [Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf); [Jawali terms](https://jawali.com.ye/Terms/)). The **International Bank of Yemen**, designated on 17 April 2025, runs Al-Dawli Money ([US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0092)), and an 11 September 2025 action against 32 targets included Shibam Holding's exchange holdings ([US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0243)).

Multilateral measures add to this. The UN asset freeze on the Houthis runs to 14 November 2026 ([UN SC/16222](https://press.un.org/en/2025/sc16222.doc.htm)), and Yemen has been under FATF increased monitoring since 2010 and was still listed in June 2026 ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/fatf-gafi/en/publications/High-risk-and-other-monitored-jurisdictions/increased-monitoring-june-2026.html)). OFAC's General License 24A authorises only **noncommercial, personal** remittances to non-blocked individuals ([OFAC](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-programs-and-country-information/counter-terrorism-sanctions)), so a diaspora buyer paying a seller is commerce with no general licence behind it. The inference for counsel is that any Sharrwah entity with a US nexus must screen sellers, beneficial owners and partners, exclude rails tied to designated banks, and treat fees or taxes paid to Houthi authorities as a material-support question.

### At least a dozen wallets, no switch in the south and no public APIs

Government areas follow a bank-led wallet model under CBY-Aden, and Houthi areas a non-bank model under Decision 1/2020 ([Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)). **ONE Cash** (Hayel Saeed Anam group) is the largest non-bank wallet, reporting more than 700,000 active customers, 14.7 million transactions worth US$2.5bn in 2024, and more than 15,000 agents ([Forbes Middle East](https://www.forbesmiddleeast.com/lists/the-middle-easts-fintech-50-2025/one-cash/)). It holds a Sana'a licence and is absent from Aden's ban list, which makes it an unverified candidate for both zones. Al-Kuraimi's **mFloos** held 80% of e-money accounts in 2019 ([Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)). Merchants accept wallet money in five live ways: a merchant-initiated debit confirmed by the buyer's OTP (CashPay, Floosak, SabaCash, Aden Cash, Al-Qutaibi); a buyer PIN entered after activating the store once in the bank app (Kuraimi); a buyer-made voucher the merchant redeems (Jawali); an in-store QR or merchant code (Jaib, Shamil Money, Kuraimi Hasib points); and an informal transfer to the seller's number with proof.

Every integration interface found is bilateral, needs credentials and is onboarded by hand, and **none has public documentation**; their shapes are known only from open-source community clients updated in 2023–2025 ([CashPay client](https://github.com/Alsharie/cash-payment); [Floosak client](https://github.com/Alsharie/floosak-payment-js); [Jawali client](https://github.com/Alsharie/jawali-payment)). None shows a webhook. Status queries after confirmation exist for CashPay, SabaCash and Aden Cash, refunds exist for Floosak (partial allowed) and as a reversal call at Kuraimi, and no wallet offers sub-merchant or split payments. The clients also expose weak security: MD5-based tokens ([CashPay client](https://github.com/Alsharie/cash-payment)), AES with a zero initialisation vector ([Al-Qutaibi client](https://github.com/Alsharie/AlQutaibiBank-payment)), and a Jawali endpoint at a bare IP address with an option to disable TLS verification ([Jawali client](https://github.com/Alsharie/jawali-payment)). In the north the WeNet switch links some wallets and banks, and ONE Cash added "a merchant payment service via WeNet" ([ONE Cash App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1661455867)). The south has no live switch; the World Bank approved US$20m in June 2025 for a fast payment system and RTGS under CBY-Aden ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/06/17/world-bank-approves-us-30-million-to-support-financial-inclusion-and-education-in-yemen)). Sellers route around the gap: a 2026 open-source "unified gateway" publishes a seller's number on each wallet, reads the seller's SMS and notifications to detect incoming transfers, and falls back to "manual WhatsApp support" ([YemenPay](https://github.com/thejulan/yemenpay)).

### Banks reach under a tenth of adults; exchange houses move domestic money

The World Bank's December 2024 diagnostic found that only **4.8%** of adults hold deposits, that there are 1.6 bank branches per 100,000 adults, that formal banking is "largely restricted to major cities", and that **59%** of formal firms used exchange companies as their main financial intermediary in 2022, against 20% using banks ([World Bank FSD](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099102324070011985/pdf/P177631-d18a7ee4-3fcf-4393-9665-77f98b3e43a7.pdf)). A January 2025 World Bank survey found that firms pay 68.1% of suppliers in cash, 20.6% through exchange-company transfers and 7.6% through banks ([Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)). No interbank instant payment exists, and no bank was found offering virtual accounts or per-order payment references, so a "bank payment" for a platform is either an intra-bank transfer the payee checks by hand or a bank-specific API such as Kuraimi's.

Exchange houses cannot legally open accounts ([SEMC](https://economicmedia.net/en/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Parallel-Market-in-Yemen-Banking-Sector-December-2025.pdf)), yet their code-based transfer, the hawala, is the national domestic rail. CBY-Aden says UNMONEY consolidated **22 transfer networks**, carried more than **YER 251bn** in 2024, and counts "all banks and e-wallets" as members ([CBY-Aden, 22 January 2026](https://cby-ye.com/news/915)); a July 2026 general assembly made the banks its shareholders ([CBY-Aden](https://cby-ye.com/news/957)). No public API, fee schedule or cancellation rule was found. Since 28 May 2024, only banks and exchangers qualified by CBY-Aden may pay out international transfers, and they must pay in the currency sent ([Asharq Al-Awsat](https://aawsat.com/node/5025163)).

### Cards serve the diaspora, and no global processor serves Yemen

In 2019 about **400,000 debit cards** were active, mostly at ATMs, against 1,206 active credit cards ([Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)). MEPS Yemen runs an e-commerce gateway that accepts Visa, Mastercard, Amex and UnionPay through Mastercard Payment Gateway Services, CyberSource and PayTabs, offers payment links, and says it is licensed by the Central Bank of Yemen, but it publishes no 3-D Secure, fee or settlement terms ([MEPS Yemen](https://www.mepsyemen.com/electronic-payment-gateway)). Global processors stay out: Stripe does not list Yemen ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/global)), and Wise lists it among countries it does not support ([Wise](https://wise.com/help/articles/2978049/what-countries-and-regions-can-i-send-money-to)). Diaspora money is large. Banks processed **US$4.9bn** of inbound transfers in 2022 and Western Union and MoneyGram another US$1.1bn ([World Bank FSD](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099102324070011985/pdf/P177631-d18a7ee4-3fcf-4393-9665-77f98b3e43a7.pdf)), and about 60% of remittances come from Saudi Arabia ([World Bank, Spring 2026](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099630405192627146/pdf/IDU-915b334e-33e9-407b-b634-2ee6167b4790.pdf)). Cards are therefore a diaspora rail, not a domestic one.

### Commerce runs on cash on delivery and "send a screenshot"

Yemen's largest e-commerce business, **Bazzarry**, runs its own fleet in Aden and Sana'a and lets customers "choose between paying cash on delivery or via a number of locally available e-wallets and ATMs" ([Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)). Its app history shows each rail arriving as a separate bilateral integration: a streamlined Kuraimi Hasib flow (June 2025), an in-app wallet with refunds credited to it (July 2025), bank-approved instalments (September 2025), Jaib (February 2026) and Al-Qutaibi bank (March 2026) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ae/app/bazzarry-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A/id1631702675?l=ar)). In a convenience survey of 180 Bazzarry users in Aden, **74.44%** preferred cash on delivery ([UKR Journal, August 2026](https://ukrpublisher.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/The-Impact-of-Determinants-of-Customer-Buying-Behavior-on-the-Market-Performance-of-E-Commerce-Platform.-A-case-stud-on-BAZARRY-e-commerce-company-Aden-Yemen.pdf)), and in the 2021 Findex round **98.9%** of Yemeni respondents had made no digital payment to a merchant ([Sana'a Center 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/files/RYE_E_Commerce_in_Yemen_NO35_en.pdf)).

One Sana'a store's payment page shows the de facto payment setup a Sharrwah seller would want to configure: cash on delivery inside Sana'a only, a Kuraimi Hasib point with rial and Saudi-riyal accounts, three wallets on one phone number, four exchange houses paying to a named person, "payment in old currency only", and a WhatsApp screenshot as proof ([da5m](https://da5m.com/pages/pay)). No Yemeni courier was found publishing cash-on-delivery remittance with statements or an API; the nearest, Torod Express, mentions "collection fees" and says merchant integration is "coming soon" ([Torod](https://torodexpress.com/faq)). Fake stores that take prepayment and vanish are a reported pattern ([Yemen Eco, March 2026](https://yemeneco.org/archives/127400)).

### One old rial buys about three new ones, so every option is bound to a zone

On 8 October 2026 old notes traded at **533 YER per dollar** in Sana'a and new notes at **1,576** in Aden, a ratio of 2.96 ([ACAPS YETI](https://yemen.yeti.acaps.org/xr-commodities/)). Sana'a has held near 535 since 2023 through enforced rates, while Aden swung from about 2,905 in mid-July 2025 to about 1,600 a month later ([World Bank, Spring 2026](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099630405192627146/pdf/IDU-915b334e-33e9-407b-b634-2ee6167b4790.pdf)). New notes are banned in the north and can be confiscated at checkpoints ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.net/ebusiness/2025/7/19/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%B9%D8%AF%D9%86-%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%86%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%86%D9%82%D8%B3%D9%85-%D9%8A%D9%86%D9%87%D9%83)). In April 2026 Aden banks capped withdrawals at YER 80,000 (about US$51) per customer per day, and more than 70% of notes in circulation were worn ([Asharq Al-Awsat](https://aawsat.com/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B1%D8%A8%D9%8A/5266997-%D8%A3%D8%B2%D9%85%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%84%D8%A9-%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%A8%D9%83-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%82%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%86%D9%8A-%D9%88%D8%AA%D8%B9%D8%B7%D9%84-%D8%B5%D8%B1%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%A8)).

Moving money across the front line carries a "commission" reported at **180%** from Aden to Sana'a in July 2026 ([Yemen Press](https://yemen-press.net/news190645.html)). Most of that is the exchange-rate gap rather than a fee: at September 2026 rates, delivering 100,000 old rials costs about 296,000 new rials (my arithmetic). In government areas Cabinet Decision 13/2025 (August 2025) requires prices, invoices and contracts in rials ([Al-Masdar Online](https://almasdaronline.com/articles/323695)), and CBY-Aden fixes reference rates by board decision, at USD 1,558 and SAR 410/413 from February 2026 ([Sana'a Center](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jan-mar-2026/26962); [Al-Ayyam](https://alayyam.info/news/AIOVZNTN-XIA26F-F8B0)). The market already treats "YER" as two currencies. Global FX feeds quote about 236 per dollar, which is wrong for both zones ([Wise](https://wise.com/gb/currency-converter/yer-to-usd-rate)); a Sana'a marketplace offers two separate Yemeni rials in its currency selector ([Bab Al-Mandab](https://bab-almandab.com/)); and Kuraimi's API configuration takes a currency zone of "old", "new" or "all" ([Kuraimi client](https://github.com/al-saloul/kurimibanck)).

### Cash at the door keeps custody with whoever carries it

Every verified Yemeni platform offers cash on delivery, and sellers keep it inside their own city; da5m offers it only inside Sana'a ([da5m](https://da5m.com/pages/pay)). Cash on delivery is structurally a single-zone option because new notes cannot travel north: cash collected in Aden for a Sana'a seller would have to be converted through an exchange house at the cross-zone rate (inference from the note ban). What separates A1 from A2 is who holds the cash after the door. In A1 the seller and its driver hold it and Sharrwah stays a recorder, Shopify's manual-payment shape in which the order is "unpaid" until the merchant marks it paid ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/manual-payments)). In A2 couriers remit to Sharrwah, which pays sellers net of fees, as Salla does with cash-on-delivery balances paid out on Sundays and Wednesdays ([Salla](https://help.salla.sa/en/article/manage-cash-on-delivery-salla-waybills/nonfrowzfu421ewhv6klylv6)). A2 adds service but also two hazards. The first is courier leakage: Jumia found about **US$800,000** of uncollected cash in Kenya in 2018 and moved to daily cash monitoring ([Caribou Digital](https://caribou.global/publications/how-financial-exclusion-and-lack-of-trust-hamper-e-commerce-platform-models/)). The second is licensing, because paying sellers from Sharrwah's account looks like money transfer or collection and payment for others, and moving collected cash between cities resembles the domestic cash transfers that Decision 23/2024 reserves to UNMONEY for exchangers, a rule whose reach to non-exchangers is unclear.

A3 removes cash from the door: the buyer pays the seller's wallet QR or merchant code at delivery. Merchant QR acceptance is documented at Jaib, Shamil Money and Kuraimi Hasib points; its use at delivery is inferred. A variant worth tracking turns Sharrwah pickup points into a licensed issuer's agents for cash-in. The 2023 instructions allow agents that are Yemeni entities with fixed premises and a settlement-bank account, acting under the issuer's full liability and charging no extra fees, so a buyer's cash could become a credit to the seller's wallet without Sharrwah acting as principal ([e-money instructions §§16–17](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)). No Yemeni platform was found doing this.

### Buyer-pushed transfers put the outcome in the seller's hands

In the three B options the buyer pushes money to an instrument the seller already holds, whether a wallet number, bank account or Hasib point (B1), a named-beneficiary exchange transfer (B2) or an international remittance (B3), and Sharrwah only records, so it needs no licence. The weakness is that the buyer's proof is only a claim: the seller establishes the outcome by checking a balance or collecting at a counter. Sharrwah's value here lies in the evidence model rather than the money, through a structured upload of the screenshot or transfer number, a seller verification step, and a distinct order state for "buyer claims paid, seller has not confirmed". The same mechanism sits behind manual receipt-upload plugins elsewhere, such as Sudan's WooCommerce plugin, where the store admin verifies each receipt by hand ([Sudan plugin](https://emoji.wordpress.org/plugins/sudan-online-payments-for-woocommerce/)). A variant of B1 has the seller's own phone parse SMS and wallet notifications, as YemenPay's app does ([YemenPay](https://github.com/thejulan/yemenpay)); the evidence improves, but the outcome still comes from a device the seller controls.

B2 has a property no domestic wallet offers: funds sit at the network until the beneficiary collects with code and ID. If the buyer gave the code to Sharrwah, Sharrwah could release it to the seller on delivery, a quasi-escrow. Whether senders can cancel unpaid transfers is unverified, and so is whether holding codes counts as handling transfers, so that variant should not ship before PAY-56 and PAY-67 are answered.

### Integrations on the seller's own account move the outcome to the rail

In the C options the rail confirms the payment and the money lands in the seller's own merchant account. This is the Shopify Payments Apps arrangement, in which the provider settles to the merchant directly ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/payments/requirements)), but three things differ in Yemen. First, Sharrwah's checkout must collect buyer secrets: the phone and OTP for CashPay, Floosak, SabaCash, Aden Cash and Al-Qutaibi, CashPay's "online purchase code", and Kuraimi's four-digit PIN, forwarded base64-encoded ([kuraimiepay](https://github.com/Alsharie/kuraimiepay)). Shopify's buyer-action modal can display a QR code or message but is "controlled by Shopify and is not customizable" ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/payments/alternative/build-an-alternative-payment-extension)), so Sharrwah needs a checkout step that collects input. Second, no rail pushes outcomes, so Sharrwah learns from the synchronous confirm response or by polling where a status call exists, which CashPay documents as "useful in cases of time out" ([CashPay client](https://github.com/Alsharie/cash-payment)). Third, every seller must onboard with every rail by hand, through branch visits, commercial registers and client certificates, so per-seller friction caps this stage.

The zone constraint is severe. CashPay, SabaCash, Jawali and Floosak are Sana'a-based and on Aden's 2024 ban list, and Floosak and Jawali are also linked to the sanctioned Yemen Kuwait Bank. An Aden-zone launch can integrate Aden Cash and Kuraimi, Al-Qutaibi if its wallet's licensing side is confirmed, and Shamil Money or ONE Cash only if they expose interfaces, which is unconfirmed.

### Partner-held collection is the lawful way to aggregate, but nobody offers it yet

The C options are capped by per-seller onboarding, and the analogue answer is a licensed party that onboards sellers through the platform and holds the funds. Saudi Zid Pay describes merchants transacting "through payment service providers on a platform belonging to Zid" ([Zid](https://zid.sa/en/legal/zidpay-terms-conditions-kw)), and Egypt separates "technical payment aggregators", which provide only the platform, from "payment facilitators", which receive funds in their own name ([IBA](https://www.ibanet.org/article/6FC3317B-4F9C-4DFC-BDF1-80CF98B68B21)). The Yemeni version under CBY-Aden's rules is D1: a licensed e-money issuer or bank opens a merchant account for each seller with caps the CBY approves, takes Sharrwah on as an approved third party, settles sellers directly and deducts Sharrwah's fee. No wallet publicly offers it and no sub-merchant or split API was found, so D1 is a negotiation target, not a product.

D2 copies the Bangladesh answer to the Evaly collapse: after a marketplace took advance payments it could not honour, the regulator required prepaid funds to sit at a licensed gateway until the platform proved delivery ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/no-more-direct-payments-e-commerce-accounts-293197)). No Yemeni bank was found offering such a product, and the CBY-Aden texts read contain no escrow instrument. D3 makes Sharrwah a payment facilitator under MEPS's acquirer, and card schemes hold acquirers "responsible for the acts of both PFs and sponsored merchants" ([Visa](https://usa.visa.com/dam/VCOM/global/support-legal/documents/visa-payment-facilitator-model.pdf)). Because the facilitator receives settlement, D3 is a holding position in Yemen's terms.

### Holding money or value requires Sharrwah's own licence

Mechanically, holding is the easiest option: Sharrwah opens its own merchant account on any wallet and pays sellers by wallet transfer or bulk disbursement, which Cash and Jawali both advertise ([Cash](https://www.cash.com.ye/en/companies-merchants-features); [Jawali](https://jawali.com.ye/en/about)). Legally it is the clearest case: receiving buyers' funds and paying sellers is "management of collection and/or payment of electronic funds", which needs the YER 500m licence. The failure mode is documented. A Bangladesh Bank inspection of Evaly found **Tk213.94 crore** of customer advance payments for undelivered products, and the outcome was forced escrow and stranded refunds ([Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/economy/e-commerce/news/evaly-has-no-money-pay-back-2182056)).

Store credit sits on the e-money boundary. CBY-Aden defines e-money as value issued against funds, "accepted as a means of payment by the parties dealing with it" and redeemable at par, so platform-wide credit usable across sellers comes close to the definition, while credit specific to one seller and not redeemable arguably falls outside it. The point is untested, and unlike India and the EU ([RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scriptS/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12156)), the Aden texts contain no closed-loop exemption ([e-money instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)). The end state, Sharrwah's own PSP and e-money licences, can also be reached by merger, as when the Iraqi card issuer QiCard bought the marketplace Miswag in December 2024 ([Iraq Business News](https://www.iraq-businessnews.com/2024/12/03/qicard-acquires-miswag/)).

### Ruled out or not yet live

Two rails are ruled out. Crypto is banned for PSPs, wallets and their agents by CBY-Aden's September 2026 circular ([Circular 1/2026](https://cby-ye.com/files/6aaa38b226baa.pdf)), and OFAC has designated Houthi-linked USDT addresses on the Tron network ([TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/post/us-treasury-sanctions-houthi-financial-networks-including-eight-crypto-addresses)). Wise, PayPal and Payoneer are not receiving rails for Yemeni sellers: Wise refuses Yemen, and PayPal is send-only at best ([PayPal](https://www.paypal.com/va/cshelp/article/why-does-my-paypal-account-have-only-send-capabilities-help1027)). Two others are not yet live. YPCC's fast payment system and the national QR standard, approved between March and May 2026, would turn A3 and the C options into interoperable scan-to-pay rails in the Aden zone, so Sharrwah should design for them without depending on them at launch. Bank-approved instalments at checkout, which Bazzarry has offered since September 2025, would make Sharrwah an integrator with the lender paying the merchant, but the evidence is a single app changelog.

### Four stages, each opened by a legal or partner precondition

The analogue markets converge on one sequence: cash on delivery and manual methods first, then integrations with dominant wallets on sellers' accounts, then a platform-branded layer run through a licensed partner, and finally the platform's own licence or a merger with an issuer ([Caribou Digital](https://caribou.global/publications/how-financial-exclusion-and-lack-of-trust-hamper-e-commerce-platform-models/); [Trilegal](https://trilegal.com/knowledge-repository/rbis-guidelines-on-regulation-of-payment-aggregators-and-payment-gateways/)). Jumia still collected only about 28% of its 2025 gross merchandise value through its own payment gateways ([Jumia Q4 2025](https://s205.q4cdn.com/370993272/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Jumia-EX-99-1-Q4-2025-2-10-2026-Final-v5.pdf)), India forced marketplaces that collected for sellers to move that activity under licensed aggregators by 2021, and Bangladesh moved prepaid funds into escrow at licensed gateways after Evaly. Yemen's rules point the same way, with one addition: **the floor must be reachable from every stage**, because either authority can remove a partner overnight.

Cross-zone selling is an FX trade through licensed exchangers, and it should stay off until counsel answers PAY-59 and the founder settles PAY-48.

## Conclusion

The founder's first payment decision in Yemen is which zone to serve, not which wallet to integrate, because each zone has its own regulator, banknotes, rails and sanctions profile, and partners do not carry across the front line. Within a zone, Sharrwah's early advantage will not come from a gateway. No Yemeni rail offers split payments, webhooks or public documentation, and CBY-Aden's ban on exclusivity, combined with its rule that the licensed wallet deducts fees, means Sharrwah can neither lock up a wallet nor take a cut without one. What Sharrwah can own is the seller's multi-instrument receiving profile, an evidence model that tells a buyer's screenshot apart from a rail's confirmation, and a custody ledger that knows who holds every rial and in which edition. Those three assets let it climb from recording to integrating to partner-held collection, and they make each step reversible.

Reversibility matters as much as ambition. Between 2024 and 2026 both central banks, the US Treasury and the Aden prosecutor each cut off payment institutions at short notice, and a platform whose orders assume a particular partner, or a single platform-wide money role, would have to be rewritten each time. Recording Sharrwah's position per payment setup instance, and keeping the record-only options live in every zone as a floor, turns a partner loss into a configuration change rather than an outage. The most valuable answers now sit with Yemeni banking counsel on PAY-54 and PAY-56, with one Aden-licensed issuer willing to discuss partner-held seller accounts on PAY-64, and with couriers prepared to publish cash-on-delivery terms on PAY-68. Each answer either opens the next stage or confirms that Sharrwah should stay longer at the one below.
