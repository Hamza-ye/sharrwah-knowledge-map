# The coordinator's rulings on the dry run's review (30 October 2026)

**What this is:** the coordinator's answers to the method questions the review's parts asked, where B-extent.md doesn't settle a case (`briefs/H-review.md`; `held/review-H-R1.md` to `R5b.md`; the join, `review-H.md`, section 5, M-1 to M-14). Each is a reading, the coordinator's, listed for the founder's veto at the next report (`PLAN.md`, section 3). Each goes into H-held.md for the other batches, and into the corrections of BC-1 and ZD-1. The join's D1 (a page's `beta` badge) and D4 (a mark as a quality) are settled within B-extent.md, as the join shows, and are restated here only for the corrections.

## Who does it, and `supports`

- **Maker evidence held outside the entry counts** (M-1; R1, M3). A held statement that names a company as of the platform's group counts wherever it is held: the entry, another source of the batch, or a cell's own maker evidence. The line cites it in `reading`. B-extent: "Maker evidence is a statement naming the company". So BigCommerce's Feedonomics cards are `own-service`, on the terms page's "any of Commerce's affiliates, subsidiaries, including, but not limited to Feedonomics Holdings, LLC".
- **The platform's own list of third parties' things to connect or install** (a channel catalog, a list of tax services, a section for accounting software) is "something the platform provides" (M-3, M-7; R1, M2; R3, F1 and M1). Accounting software at `/admin/apps/accounting` is therefore `extension-point`; the page itself, not held, would show more. A card or row that names a third party's solution is `extension-point`; one that names no one, or only the venue, is `core` by what it shows, with the missing maker noted as a gap; one that names a group company is `own-service`. The venue is the filler.
  - **Refined after WC-3** (30 October): the platform's suggestion to buy or install an extension from its marketplace, naming no maker (WooCommerce's `woocommerce_marketplace_suggestions`), shows the thing: `shows`, as the model already places the same extensions (CM-SEL-001's WooCommerce Subscriptions, CM-SEL-004's Bookings). `core` is for a card of the platform's own catalog of connections, which shows the platform connecting the thing (BigCommerce's channels). A suggestion that names a third party's product is `extension-point` on its capability, with the third party as filler.
- **A list of providers the platform's own words call its integrations is `core`,** each provider its filler (M-4; R2, M1). BigCommerce's tables 1 and 3: "BigCommerce gateway integrations", and "payment gateway integrations" that BigCommerce "supports", each row linked to the platform's own "Connecting with" article.
- **A provider's role is placed only from the entry and its sources** (M-5; R2, M2). A role known only from another platform's cell (instalments, cryptocurrency) is a lead for the second stage, not evidence.
- **A page the platform's navigation lists under its brand at an app's address,** with no maker and no gate stated (Zid Ads, Zid Voice), is `shows` on what the app does, with the missing maker a gap, and `core` on CM-APP-007 as the dashboard's page for an app (M-10; R4, M1).
- **A tooltip's "you can build X with it"** makes the merchant responsible: `shows`. What a plan line "includes" is `core` (M-9; R3, M3).
- **A third party's own feature through the platform's interface** (Avalara's return filing) is `extension-point` on the capability whose thing it is (CM-TAX-006), beside the provider's line (M-8; R3, M2).

## What an entry is, and where its other things go

- **A thing an entry names counts in each capability it names** (M-6; R2, M4; R3, F4). A child's entry whose own words name the parent's thing (its section is the parent's term) takes the parent's line too. That is domain C's rule, "a statement that names several things counts in each".
  - **Refined after the calibration** (30 October; `held/calibration.md`): the child takes the parent's line where it is a kind of the parent's thing, as Bank deposit, under OFFLINE PAYMENT METHODS, is an offline payment method (R2's case). A group the entry only sits under, whose title says where the entry is and not what it is (BigCommerce's `group: General`), gives it no line: the group is placed once, as a container (rule 3). Read as every section's group, the rule gave BigCommerce's settings lines that changed no cell: 7 at `high`, each of the General group's sections on store details (CM-ACC-006), and 14 at `xhigh`, over five groups.
- **A mark is never a quality** (D4, within B-extent.md; R2, M3; R3, F2):
  - a plan's mark of inclusion (a check, "Available", "Included") is the line's scope;
  - a provider's feature mark (Recurring Payments ✔) is a statement of the marked feature's capability: a line there, `shows` unless the row says who does it, the provider its filler.
- **A listing condition** (M-11; R4, M2; R5a; R5b):
  - a condition the platform sets for a store (a named store flag, a kind of store, a store's state such as trial or suspended, a plan) is the entry's scope, in the source's own identifiers. A computed one with no name of its own is named by the module it comes from (`use-is-ngo-store`), marked as a reading;
  - a condition on the store's own data (the blog turned on, an app installed) is one store's data, and goes in `reading`.
- **A conditional title is a statement of its own** (M-14; R4, M5): the platform states the page under each condition, and the condition is its scope. Zid's charity-store titles (Donations, Donation opportunities, Donors) go to the second stage, for CM-SEL-007.
- **A release badge** (D1, within B-extent.md; R4, M6; R5a, F4): a page the platform lists with a badge, `new` or `beta`, is offered to the store: `core`, with the badge in `reading`. Only a flag stated as not released (`released: false`, `is_experimental: true`) is `shows`. H-held.md had listed "a beta" among flags not released, which reads a listed page's badge as a flag.
- **A container** goes on the capability its title or its group names, as the cells' readings show; failing that, on the capability whose page its own path opens; failing both, `new:<its title>` with `core`, since it is the platform's own admin place, and the coordinator decides it with the other new things. It takes a second line where its own page holds another thing. A section whose title names only a grouping is placed the same way (M-13; the join's D3; R4, F6 and M4; R5a, F3).
- **BigCommerce's Extensions aren't CM-APP-011's** (M-2; R1, M1). The terms define an Extension as "a service developed by a third party but implemented and provided by Commerce"; CM-APP-011 is apps the platform's company makes. The five held lines take `new:Extensions` with `shows`, no place of its own. The model's own CM-APP-011 BigCommerce cell rests on the same homonym: its `own-service` from BC-S-0428 and BC-S-0432 becomes `shows`, and CM-APP-011's count drops by one platform.

## What an agent may read

- **Beyond the entry's item and its source,** an agent may read, to clarify an entry only: the run steps the platform's ledger or `index/runs.tsv` joins to it, the locales, and the model's cells (R3, F3; R4, F1, M3). So may the source page around a segment, and the answers `index/` names. A reading that rests on one cites it at its file (`<run_id>/steps/<NNN>`), and quotes it exactly. Scope, terms, filler and quality still come from the entry and its source (the join's FX-1).
- **What a run step or a locale states that no entry holds** isn't placed by the held pass (M-12; R4, M3; the join's D6). It goes in the notes, as a lead for the second stage, which reads held pages as statements for the cells that hold nothing. So R5a's scope "Saudi Arabia" for line 91, read from the Mokafaa page, isn't taken; the Mokafaa and Qitaf rates, Zid's three settings toggles (CM-INV-004, CM-MKT-023, CM-ORD-014), the feed's named platforms and Zid Insights' trial go to the second stage.

## After the batches under version 3

- **A maker field must be one merchants are shown** (after WC-4). WooCommerce's `is_built_by_wc: true` is a maker statement, since its admin shows it as 'Built by WooCommerce'. The payments suggestions' tag `made_in_woo` isn't: the release defines it only in a constant's comment, 'For extensions developed by Woo.' (`src/Internal/Admin/Suggestions/PaymentsExtensionSuggestions.php`, line 105), no admin asset shows it, and for PayPal Payments it contradicts what merchants read, 'PayPal Payments by PayPal' (WC-S-0911).
- **A list of permissions** (after ZD-7): each permission is `core` on the capability it gates, as CM-STF-002's note keeps them; only a permission over the staff's permissions themselves goes on CM-STF-002.
- **An offer the platform lists but states unavailable where it is listed is `shows`** (after WC-5). WooPayments' onboarding states 'WooPayments is not supported in the selected business location.' for EG, OM, SA and YE: there its line is `shows`, the location its scope, the statement in `reading`. Where it is supported (AE, GB, JP, US), its line is `own-service`. A suggestion category that no held location fills is `shows`: it names a topic, with no provider offering it.
- **A released flag that is off by default with no toggle in the admin is `shows`** (after WC-6): WooCommerce's `disable_ui` true with `enabled_by_default` false, or left at its default, false (`FeaturesController.php`, line 229). The platform ships it but doesn't offer it to merchants to turn on. Order Fulfillments and Cart & Checkout Blocks are both defined so.
- **A heading of the platform's public site** (after SA-2 and SA-5). M-13 makes a container with no capability `new` and `core` because it is "the platform's own admin place": the admin, the partners' portal or the app store, where a merchant, partner or developer works. A heading of the platform's public site, such as salla.com's header and footer, is the site's navigation.
  - Where its links are one capability's pages, it takes that capability's line: الموارد, over the help centre, academy, community, blog and updates centre, is CM-ONB-003.
  - Where they are several capabilities' pages (الحلول, القطاعات, عن سلة, أدوات التجار), it names only a grouping. It is `out`, and each link is placed on its own capability.
  - A link to a marketing page for one business sector is `out` too. It names whom the platform markets to, and the sector's page isn't held.
- **The platform's changelog is CM-ONB-003** (after SA-2 and SA-5). This is a reading. salla.com's footer lists its updates centre under الموارد with the help centre, academy and community: the platform's news and learning for its merchants.
  - A changelog category that names only a grouping of posts (تحسينات المنصة) goes there too (M-13).
  - A category or post that names a feature goes on the feature's capability.
- **A store's llms.txt is CM-CNT-007's, and `shows` on CM-AIA-005** (after SA-2).
  - CM-AIA-005 is buyers discovering and buying a store's products through AI chats and agents, on channels the platform or third parties operate.
  - A store's llms.txt is a file the platform writes for AI platforms to read the store. It gives buyers no channel and no purchase. Both platforms that hold it keep it in their SEO pages: Zid in its SEO section, Salla at `marketing/seo/llms-txt`.
  - So it is `core` on CM-CNT-007 where the platform writes it, and `shows` on CM-AIA-005 where its words name AI platforms or buyers' discovery through AI.
  - The review's R4 had let ZD-1's `core` on CM-AIA-005 stand, and ZD-4 and ZD-12 kept to it. The coordinator corrects the three lines.
  - Zid's and Salla's CM-AIA-005 cells are then held with no placement. Shopify's cell holds channels for agents, and BigCommerce's holds endpoints for agents.
- **A line that only one market's page holds takes that market in its `scope`** (after SA-3). salla.com's plans pages differ by market beyond the currency.
  - The Saudi pages show a yearly discount, savings and struck prices, and their comparison table has nine rows.
  - The UAE pages show none of these. Their table has five rows: it lacks Salla's discounted labels, Salla Ads, Mahally and the VAT line with the Zakat authority.
  - So a line only the Saudi pages hold takes السعودية in its scope, beside its plans. One only the UAE pages hold takes الامارات. These are the words of Salla's footer, 'مدعوم في: السعودية الامارات'.
  - SA-2's rule 5 had read the pages as the same text but for the currency.
- **A card line in a page's two languages is one thing** (after SA-4). Where the English and Arabic lines are one element, by their place or by the layer the English one is named for, they take the capability of the words that name the thing more exactly:
  - 'Run discount promotions' is 'إنشاء كوبونات تخفيض', coupons (CM-MKT-002);
  - 'Custom pricing that matches your needs' is 'رسوم دفع مرنة تدعم توسُّع أعمالك', the payment service's fees (CM-PAY-007).

  A thing that only one language names takes its own line, since either language is the platform's statement. An example is SA-4's 'multiple stores' and 'teams', which the Arabic card doesn't name.
- **A category of the platform's services marketplace is CM-PTN-005's** (after SA-25). It is `extension-point` on CM-PTN-005, and `shows` on the capability whose work it names. Examples are Salla's Merchant Services and Shopify's Partner Directory.
  - B-extent's "marketplace category" is a marketplace of apps, themes or extensions, which third parties plug into the platform.
  - An expert hired for theme customization or product preparation does a person's service, with the platform's own tools.
  - The four cells SA-25's categories would have given `extension-point` (CM-CAT-001, CM-CNT-008, CM-MKT-010, CM-SFR-002) keep their placement.
- **An export's column or an importer's field is a field the platform keeps on its records** (after SA-25 and SA-26). It is `core` on the capability whose data it holds.
  - An order's Order Source (`utm_source`) is CM-MKT-008's, as WooCommerce's Order Attribution keeps the same.
  - A product's Cost Price (`cost_price`) is CM-CAT-012's, as WooCommerce's Cost of Goods Sold keeps the same.
  - SA-25's rule E2 had made such a column `shows` on a capability whose thing its title names. Its lines on cells that are already `core` change nothing and stand.
- **A container over the whole admin is `out`** (after SA-21). This is a limit on M-13. Salla's navigation segments 'main' and 'mega' answer with every section of the dashboard. The class holds no capability that is the admin itself, so the container names none. Each section and item is placed on its own. A container over one part of the admin keeps M-13.
- **A container over several sections of the admin, with no title of its own, is `out` too** (after SA-22). A group inside them whose own address is the menu's (the Shipping group at `/more`) keeps M-13 where an entry is about it, as SA-32's plan gate is.
- **A second line on an address is for a page that is itself two things** (after SA-22). A line that would rest only on a gate's feature goes in `reading`, as M-6 refined has it. This settles SA-21's rule for SA-23.
- **A title that names only a grouping, over several capabilities' pages, is `out` like the menus after SA-22** (after SA-28). Examples are a portal navigation group 'Others' and a menu 'More'. M-13's container line is for a title that names a capability or one part of the platform.
- **A feature listed for a partner's account takes the support of the page it gates** (after SA-44). The partners' portal's features gate the partner's own tools. The thing a tool does sets the support, as the routes placed it:
  - the expert marketplace is `extension-point`, third parties offering services;
  - Salla's own billing is `core`.

  So a feature line changes nothing the routes didn't, and the brief's 'a released flag supports `core`' stays for a store's features over the platform's own capabilities.

- **A permission of the partners' portal takes the support of the page it gates, as a feature does** (after SA-30, extending the ruling after SA-44). A partner's tool for a marketplace of third parties stays `extension-point`.

  - Together they are the lead for CM-APP-011, the platform's own apps, which holds nothing on Salla. The App Store's listing is read with the second stage (`selection.tsv`: single app listings).
- **Things a page names only in a list take no line** (after SA-41). This applies to lists of what may be used or changed. The page's own thing takes its line, and the things it lists in passing are not a second thing of the page (the ruling after SA-22). Example: the ready stores' FAQ answer 'customize everything such as plan, theme design, settings, products' is placed on CM-DVT-005 alone. A statement about one of those things is placed on it, such as 'any Salla-developed theme' saying who makes the themes.

Example: the Arabic link أدوات التتبع ('tracking tools') opens Pixels & feeds, so it takes both of that page's lines, CM-MKT-009 and CM-CHN-004, as the English link does. - **A route that only redirects is `shows` on its target's capability** (after SH-8). Its page is its target's, and the target takes its own line. The concern map's `core` on `balance:inactive:redirect-statements` (SH-S-5044) is read again at Shopify's amendment.
- **Only a route's own gates on the store are its scope** (after SH-8). An ancestor's gate belongs to the ancestor, and the coordinator names it in the reading of every line under it, by script (`method/held_router_gates.py`). This is how a menu group's feature is named (SA-42's M6). The agent doesn't look up ancestors.
- **A route's `intent` that the platform documents for apps takes an `extension-point` line** on the route's capability (after SH-8). Shopify's Intents API is the case: an app opens the admin there. An intent no held document describes is named in `reading`.

- **A post's own thing takes its line, and what it names in passing doesn't** (the ruling after SA-41, for changelog posts). Shopify's brand on a product named in passing is no maker statement. The checker's brand note now reads only the line's own terms.
- **In a settings tree, a gate node's plans or features are the scope of the nodes inside it** (after SA-49). An `s:plan-gate` or a gated list is a gate node: its own thing is the gate over its content (SA-48's S4). A route manifest is different: a route's eligibility is the route's own, so an ancestor's is named in `reading` (the ruling after SH-8).
- **A settings `s:redirect` is `shows` on its target's capability** (after SA-49), as a route that only redirects is. This corrects SA-48's S8, which had it `out`.
- **The admin's own interface language is one new thing across the platforms** (after SA-49): a dashboard's or a portal's language menu, or an admin language setting. Its cases are Salla's portal menu and its dashboard's English (Beta), both `new:العربية / English`, and Shopify's Admin languages (SH-34). It is decided in `held/new.tsv`.

Its feature is the scope of every node inside it, and a node's own gate is added to that scope. An `if` on a store's feature or plan is a gate the same way (T2). An `if` on the store's own data, or on a form's value, goes in `reading`.
- **Shopify's changelog speaks as the platform** (after SH-35). A post saying that 'we' make, upgrade or ship an app is a maker statement, so the app's own things are `own-service`, as 'a new app from Shopify' is for Audiences (SH-50). Checkout Blocks is the case: 'we’re upgrading the Checkout Blocks app'. A post that only names an app is no maker statement (the ruling after SH-34).
- **A Shopify-branded app is read by the kind of entry that names it** (after SH-36):
  - A page at the app's own address, with no maker stated, is `shows` on what the app does and `core` on CM-APP-007 (M-10). Editions' cards that link `apps/<handle>` are the case.
  - A post or a card that names the app, with no maker stated, is `core` by what it shows, with the maker a gap (the brief's catalog card).
  - A post in which 'we' make, upgrade or ship the app makes its things `own-service` (the ruling after SH-35).
- **A line takes its own scope where its thing is stated under its own condition** (after SH-69), and the entry's other lines keep theirs. A disclaimer's bank is named for one market (WebBank, the United States). An FAQ names one of its answers for one plan (Plus). This extends the brief's rule that an entry's lines share one scope, as the ruling after SH-34 did for two lines on one capability.
