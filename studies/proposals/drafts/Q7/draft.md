## 2. Proposed: names, structure and behaviour

- **Currency table**. Proposed: each currency Sharrwah supports is a row of data, with its code, the size of its smallest unit and its symbol; a currency is added, renamed or retired by changing rows, not code. Q7-ZD-024 Q7-WC-055
  - From the platforms: Zid's smallest unit and WooCommerce's ISO codes. Why: rule 3, as codes and units sit in every stored amount; and rule 2, as currencies change, like Shopify's Bulgarian markets moving to the euro, or WooCommerce rewriting KIP to LAK. Q7-SH-107 Q7-WC-057
- **Money amount**. Proposed: every stored amount is a whole number of its currency's smallest unit, with that currency's code; a converted amount keeps the original, the result and the rate between them. Q7-ZD-024 Q7-SH-123
  - From the platforms: the unit from Zid, and the pair from Shopify's MoneyBag, which holds the shop's and the customer's amounts. Why: rule 3, as changing how amounts are stored after launch would touch every order and balance. Q7-ZD-024 Q7-SH-123
- **Store currency**. Proposed: each store has exactly one, in which the seller's prices are entered and reports run. The seller picks it when creating the store, from those its place allows, with the place's default preselected. Q7-WC-K0745 Q7-SH-K1935 Q7-ZD-001
  - From the platforms: one currency per store for a seller's prices (Shopify, Zid, WooCommerce, BigCommerce), with a default from the store's country (Zid, WooCommerce, BigCommerce). Why: rule 1, as the default and choices come from **place currency settings**, not a build per place; and rule 3, as each **order currency record** converts to it. Q7-ZD-002 Q7-BC-013 Q7-WC-054 Q7-BC-029
- **Place currency settings**. Proposed: for each place it serves, Sharrwah sets a default currency and the currencies a store there may price in, show, charge in and be paid out in; the last two are also bounded by the **provider currency capability** of the providers connected there. Q7-ZD-001 Q7-WC-054
  - From the platforms: country defaults (Zid, WooCommerce, BigCommerce), Salla's list of currencies a store could add, each tied to a country (observed, store demo, 9 October 2026), and WooCommerce's payout currencies by country. Why: rule 1. Q7-BC-029 Q7-SA-031 Q7-WC-K0739
- **Market**, with its **display currency**. Proposed: a group of buyer countries within one store, with one display currency, other currencies its buyers may pick, and, optionally, its own prices in its own currency. Every store starts with one market for its own place, in the store currency. A buyer is placed by country, then may pick among the market's currencies. Q7-SH-076 Q7-ZD-K0236 Q7-ZD-020
  - From the platforms: Shopify's currency per market and one-country default; Zid's market currency, local default market and prices "وبعملة السوق نفسها"; Salla's default by location and the buyer's own pick. Why: rule 1, as one store is set up for each place it sells to; and rule 3, as the level at which prices and currencies sit is part of the data model. Q7-SH-079 Q7-ZD-019 Q7-SA-K1957 Q7-SA-K1956
  - Not taken: WooCommerce's one list for the whole store, as a market can hold such a list, but the list can't hold prices per place. Q7-WC-013
- **Charged currency** and **shown-only currency**. Proposed: each currency of a market is one or the other. A shown-only currency converts prices for display, and the buyer pays in the store currency, as checkout says. A currency can be charged only if a provider connected for the market declares, in its **provider currency capability**, that it takes it. Q7-BC-031 Q7-BC-005 Q7-BC-014
  - From the platforms: BigCommerce's transactional or display-only setting per currency, and its gateway rule; Shopify's and Salla's charge in one currency when the provider can't take the shown one. Why: rule 2, as a change of provider then changes a market's set-up, not checkout or orders. Q7-SH-K1938 Q7-SA-K1963
  - Not taken: Shopify's tie of local-currency payment to the store's one primary gateway, for all markets, as a store selling to several places may need a provider in each (rule 1). Q7-SH-K1937
- **Order currency record**. Proposed: an order records, when placed, the charged currency and every amount in it, the same amounts in the store currency, the display currency if different, and each rate used, with its source and time; none is recomputed. A refund goes out in the charged currency, any conversion of it at that day's rate, recorded beside the first. Q7-SH-123 Q7-ZD-010 Q7-BC-020
  - From the platforms: the currencies and rates Shopify, Zid, BigCommerce and Salla record on orders; WooCommerce's earlier orders, which keep their currency and totals; refunds in the currency paid (Shopify) at the refund's rate (WooCommerce). Why: rule 3, as an order whose amounts follow a later rate or store currency no longer shows what the buyer paid. Q7-SA-016 Q7-WC-027 Q7-SH-068 Q7-WC-019
  - Not taken: converting recorded sales at the rate of the moment the store currency changes, as Zid's help describes; reports may convert, but an order keeps its amounts. Q7-ZD-003
- **Exchange rate**, with its **rate source**. Proposed: each rate Sharrwah uses, to show prices or convert amounts for reports, comes from the rate source of that currency of the market: automatic, from a plugged-in **rate provider** on a stated schedule, or manual, fixed by the seller; converted prices follow that currency's rounding rule. A payment provider's rate on a payment is its own; Sharrwah records the rate and fee it reports. Q7-SH-078 Q7-WC-K0755 Q7-BC-035
  - From the platforms: Shopify's Dynamic or Manual rate per market, with Price rounding beside it in a market's saved markup (store demo-fake-dev-store, 30 September 2026, observed); WooCommerce's server rates and BigCommerce's merchant's own source through the API; the rate on a payment, outside BigCommerce's scope, and Shopify's rate and fee at capture. Why: rule 2, as the source is likely to change; and rule 1, as a place may need its own. Q7-BC-P0015 Q7-SH-013 Q7-SH-204
- **Payout account**, with its **payout currency**. Proposed: each destination a seller is paid out to has one payout currency. A seller may have several, one the default, and a payment in a currency matching none is converted by the provider to the default's. Which currencies a payout account may have is set per place and provider. Q7-SH-004 Q7-WC-K0737 Q7-SH-P0155
  - From the platforms: Shopify's and WooCommerce's payout currency and default account, and Shopify's Multi-Currency Payouts; payout currencies bounded by region or entity (Shopify), country and its banks (WooCommerce, Salla), or the provider (BigCommerce). Why: rule 3, as the money flow ends at this payout, and partner contracts decide its currencies. Q7-SH-012 Q7-SH-K1268 Q7-WC-K0739 Q7-SA-K1964 Q7-BC-P0016
- **Seller ledger**. Proposed: each amount owed to or by a seller, for sales, fees, refunds and payouts, is an entry in one currency. Balances are per currency, never added across currencies, and move between currencies only by a recorded conversion. The ledger records what is owed, whoever holds the money. Q7-SH-131 Q7-WC-048 Q7-BC-058
  - From the platforms: per-currency balances (Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce), Salla's wallets, each with a currency (store real-merchant, 12 October 2026, observed), and Zid's wallet, its currency set by country. Why: rule 3. Q7-SA-022 Q7-ZD-P0122
  - The guard: the ledger records balances and holds no money. Any arrangement in which Sharrwah holds sellers' money, or a payment company holds it in an account opened through Sharrwah, as Salla's رصيد المدفوعات الإلكترونية and Zid's wallet hold their sellers' proceeds, goes live only by the founder's decision. Q7-SA-K1560 Q7-ZD-K0117

Proposed: the store owner may change the store currency until the first order; after it, only a currency migration run by Sharrwah can, leaving every recorded order as it is and stating whether prices convert at a stated rate or keep their numbers.   - From the platforms: Salla's bar once there are orders; Shopify's owner-only change; prices kept by number (Shopify, BigCommerce) or converted (Zid); Shopify's Bulgaria change, made on its stores' behalf, and WooCommerce's routine rewriting orders' currency code. ## 5. What the design must keep open

- Who holds a seller's money before payout, and in which currency. The records name such balances' currencies only in part (section 1, [4]). Keep the **seller ledger** apart from whatever account holds the money, so the design fits a provider holding it, an arrangement through Sharrwah, or none. Settled by: the founder's decision under the guard, and the providers' contracts. Q7-SA-022 Q7-ZD-P0122 Q7-SH-N01
- Which currencies can be charged and paid out at each place. Keep them as **provider currency capability** data for each place, not code. Settled by: the contracts with the providers Sharrwah works with (section 7). Q7-SH-K1937 Q7-BC-P0016
- Several payout currencies, and an entity per place. Shopify gives each business entity its own payout currency, and several payout currencies only on some plans. Keep each **payout account** tied to its provider account and contracting entity. Settled by: where the company is based, and its contracts (section 7). Q7-SH-K1268 Q7-SH-P0155
- How a **currency migration** treats prices: converted at a stated rate, as Zid's help describes, or kept by number, as Shopify and BigCommerce say. Keep the **order currency record** self-contained, so either fits. Settled by: the first migration Sharrwah needs. Q7-ZD-K0302 Q7-SH-006
- A cross-border seller of record: Shopify's Managed Markets holds the money in a Global-e account whose currency wasn't in what was read. Keep the **order currency record** able to hold a third party's charged currency and rate. Settled by: a company decision on such a partner (section 7). Q7-SH-K1600 Q7-SH-N04
- Whether the rial is one row of the **currency table** or two, and so the level of Yemen's **place currency settings**. Yemen's evidence shows the rial split by area, and a provider that accepts customers by rial zone (section 6). ## 6. Yemen: what its evidence allows, forbids or doesn't show

- **Currency table** and **place currency settings**: one currency and one central bank don't serve the whole country (Stage 1's source: "old and distinguishable new banknotes are treated as two separate currencies"), and a provider's payment API accepts customers by "currency zone". These allow a currency table that holds each rial as its own row, and place Yemen's currency settings at the rial zone, not at Yemen as one place. YK-12 (split @ Sana'a and Aden, as of 2026) YK-452 (by currency zone, old, new or all @ E-Pay (Kuraimi Islamic Microfinance Bank)'s service, as of 2026-10-10)
- **Exchange rate** and **rate source**: the rial has two market rates at once, one in each city. They allow a rate per currency of a market only where the market lies within one zone; one rate for the rial in both cities would be wrong in one. YA-01 (YER 533 @ Sana'a, as of 2026-10-08) YA-01 (YER 1,562 buying, 1,567 selling @ Aden, as of 2026-10-10)
- **Rate provider**: an international data provider's quote differs from both market rates, so the rial's rates can't come from it, as of that date; a manual rate, or a source within the zone, fits. YA-07 (YER 236.197 @ place not stated, as of 2026-10-10)
- **Charged currency**: domestic sales are paid in the rial only at the internationally recognized government's area, as of 2026-04-24. This forbids marking a foreign currency charged for domestic sales there; a foreign currency can only be a **shown-only currency** there. YA-21 (rial only @ the internationally recognized government's area, as of 2026-04-24)
- **Provider currency capability**: the currencies a payment request names differ by provider: the rial alone at one, the rial, Saudi riyal and US dollar at another. This allows a capability declared per provider, as proposed. YA-114 (the rial @ E-Pay (Kuraimi Islamic Microfinance Bank)'s service, as of 2025-05-26) YA-114 (the rial, Saudi riyal and US dollar @ YemenPay's service, as of 2026-08-22)
- **Payout account** and **payout currency**: Stripe and WooPayments aren't available for Yemen, as of 2026-10-10, so Yemen's payout currencies come from the capabilities of providers that are, set in **place currency settings**. YA-62 (Unavailable @ Stripe's service, as of 2026-10-10) YA-62 (Not available, a request to add it open @ WooPayments's service, as of 2026-10-10)
- Yemen's evidence doesn't show the currency in which a Yemeni payment provider pays a merchant out to a bank account. A provider's merchant terms, or a recorded payout, would show it.
- **Seller ledger**: a phone wallet can hold the rial, the Saudi riyal and the US dollar, which allows, and needs, balances per currency. In Sana'a, electronic balances are priced apart from cash, so one rial balance for cash on delivery and electronic amounts would mix two prices there. YA-86 (the rial, Saudi riyal and US dollar @ ONE Cash's service, as of 2026-10-10) YK-16 (priced apart from cash @ Sana'a, as of 2025-12)
- **Order currency record**: a sales-tax registered seller's invoice in a foreign currency gives the rial value at the prevailing rate. This allows the record as proposed, and asks that it hold the rial value and rate even where neither the store nor the charged currency is the rial. YA-166 (rial value at the prevailing rate @ Tax Authority's area (مصلحة الضرائب), as of 2002-05-25)
- Yemen's evidence doesn't show which currency code a provider's API gives each rial zone, which the **currency table** needs. The providers' API documentation, or a test payment in each zone, would show it.
