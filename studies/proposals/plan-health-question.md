# Which plan is healthier: the first, or the re-framed one? (parked)

**Asked by the founder on 19 October 2026, about 19:10 UTC. Framed by the coordinator, and parked to be answered later with the rubric below. Not answered yet.** The founder's words are at the end.

## The question

The work has run under two plans:
- **The first plan,** agreed on 12 October 2026 (gate G0): repair and freeze what was recorded (Stage 0), build Yemen's axes and cases (Stage 1), then take one subject at a time end to end (Stage 2's pilot, S-01: phases A, A2, A3, B, C, D and F, then a decision-register entry at gate G1).
- **The re-framed plan,** agreed on 18 October 2026 after G1 (`PLAN.md` section 11): the founder's intent as eight questions. Each is recorded across the platforms' areas (R1 to R3), drafted as a proposal for Sharrwah's design (R4), checked by risk (R5), and reviewed (R6).

**Asked:**
1. **Health:** judged by the rubric, which plan gets the founder more sound, usable decisions for the tokens and days it costs?
2. **Friction and ceremony:**
   - where did each plan add friction: cost, stops and re-work that didn't improve the result?
   - where did each carry ceremony: steps whose cost changed nothing the founder decides?
   - in particular, which ceremonies of the first plan the second exposes, and which the second still carries.
3. **Lessons:** what should the rest of this work keep, drop or change because of each? What should the first build's design plan take from it?

## The coordinator's pushback (engineering), at the founder's invitation

The founder invited a pushback on the question itself, the judgement being mostly engineering. The founder decides.

1. **The two plans aren't alternatives. They are two turns of one spiral** (Boehm 1988).
   - The first plan's Stage 2 was a pilot: the riskiest subject first, with a kill rule and a gate (G1) built to re-frame the method.
   - It did that job: it found the method's faults on one subject before they were scaled.
   - So "which is healthier" compares a probe with the build it informed. The engineering question is a retrospective: what each turn cost and taught, and whether the current process is fit for what remains (R5, R6) and for the design plan.
2. **On cost, the answer needs no review:**
   - the pilot's agents processed about 1.03 billion tokens for one subject, so the founder's eight items at that rate would cost several times the 2 billion budget;
   - the re-frame records at about 0.16 million tokens per new statement, against the pilot's 0.52.
3. **"Ceremony" can only be judged by tracing each step's output to what the founder saw:** did a finding change a proposal or a decision? That is worth doing for the checks, on a sample. For the rest it costs more than it would show.
4. **Asked at the end, cheaply:**
   - once, at the end of this plan, as the first input to the design plan, where the lessons can still change a process;
   - led by the coordinator from what is measured (costs, findings and what each changed, the founder's messages), with an independent check of the coordinator's account at `high`, about 5 to 10 million tokens;
   - not as a full independent review at `max`, about 20 to 30 million.

**The question, as the coordinator would put it:** "Across both turns, for each step: what did it cost, what did it catch or change that the founder saw, and what should the design plan keep, drop or change?" The rubric above stays as its checklist. Its dimensions 3 (reuse) and 12 (budget) are answered from the measured costs.
