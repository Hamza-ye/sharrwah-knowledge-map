# Yemen: legal, regulatory, licensing and sanctions landscape for e-payments and e-commerce money flows, and the legal positions a non-bank commerce platform (Sharrwah) can occupy (as of 9 October 2026)

*Conventions: "CBY-Aden" = internationally recognised Central Bank of Yemen, head office Aden. "CBY-Sana'a" = Houthi-controlled central bank in Sana'a. **[PRE-2023]** marks facts older than 2023. **[CONTESTED]** marks rules or acts that one authority does not recognise. Most CBY-Aden documents cited below are scanned Arabic PDFs on cby-ye.com that I read page by page. Where the text gives a number, it is quoted from the document itself, not from press coverage. Translations are mine.*

## Key question 1: Which laws and central-bank regulations govern electronic payments, mobile money / e-money and payment service providers, and what do they require (capital, licensing, agents, limits, KYC tiers, interoperability, data localisation)? Who issued them, Aden or Sana'a?

### Takeaway
The statutory base is pre-war and shared by both sides: Central Bank Law No. 14 of 2000, the Payment Systems and Electronic Financial and Banking Operations Law No. 40 of 2006, and AML/CFT Law No. 1 of 2010. The working rules are central-bank instruments, and the two banks issue them separately.

CBY-Aden has a fairly complete written regime:
- **PSP / payment-system-operator instructions (Sept 2022).** A joint-stock company with paid-up capital of at least YER 500m, a CBY licence, a data centre in Yemen, and CBY approval for any outsourcing.
- **Non-bank mobile e-money instructions (Aug 2023), amended by Decision No. 4 of 2025 (July 2025).** A PSP licence comes first. Then: a 100% trust account at a settlement bank, capital of at least 25% of the e-money outstanding, transaction and balance caps, two-tier ID for cash-out, agent rules, and no exclusivity. The 2025 amendment allows USD, SAR and AED wallets.
- **Supporting instruments:** e-KYC instructions (Nov 2023), financial-consumer-protection instructions (Aug 2023), a sanctions-screening circular (Nov 2025) and a crypto ban (Sept 2026).

CBY-Sana'a runs its own regime, which I could not reconstruct as well. Its Decision No. 1 of 2020 opened e-money issuance to non-banks (exchange companies, traders, non-financial firms) with no cap on issuance. CBY-Aden contested that decision as illegal.

### Cited Findings

**Authorities**
- Two central banks have existed since the government moved the CBY head office from Sana'a to Aden in September 2016. CBY-Sana'a kept most of the staff and infrastructure and dominates domestically. CBY-Aden holds international recognition. Neither side has the parliamentary authority to pass new financial legislation [PRE-2023, context still valid]. — [Sana'a Center/DeepRoot, "Challenges and Prospects for Electronic Money and Payment Systems in Yemen", Rethinking Yemen's Economy No. 10, March 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)
- Inside CBY-Aden, licensing decisions are taken by a Licensing Committee set up by Board Decision No. 7 of 2022. Applications are filed with the Governor and studied by the Banking Supervision Sector. — [CBY-Aden e-money instructions, Ref 547/CBY/2023, definitions](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf); [CBY-Aden PSP instructions, Ref 720/CBY/2022, definitions](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf)
- Other bodies involved:
  - The Ministry of Industry and Trade handles commercial registration. A PSP's final licence requires completing registration there.
  - The tax authority administers sales tax (see Q5).
  - The financial intelligence unit (وحدة جمع المعلومات المالية) receives suspicious-transaction reports.
  - The state-run General Authority for Post and Postal Savings (GAPPS) offers transfer and bill-payment services outside the CBY framework, under the telecom ministry [PRE-2023].
  - Sources: [PSP instructions p.9](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf); [e-money instructions §17(h)](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf); [Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)
- BTI 2026 notes that the two CBY branches "operate with competing regulations". — [BTI 2026 Yemen Country Report](https://bti-project.org/en/reports/country-report/YEM) (quoted in search summary; not read in full)

**Primary statutes (shared; issued before the split)**
- **Law No. 40 of 2006 on Payment Systems and Electronic Financial and Banking Operations.** Issued 28 Dec 2006 (8 Dhu al-Hijjah 1427) in Sana'a and signed by President Ali Abdullah Saleh [PRE-2023]. Key provisions:
  - Art. 10: electronic records, contracts and signatures have the same legal effect as written ones.
  - Art. 15: a data message can form an offer or an acceptance.
  - Art. 26: electronic fund transfer is "an accepted means of making payment".
  - Art. 27(2): institutions must provide secure services and keep banking secrecy.
  - Art. 30: the CBY issues instructions on electronic transfers, including approval of e-payment methods.
  - Art. 9(2): banks keep records for at least 10 years.
  - Art. 41: crimes committed electronically carry 3 months to 1 year in prison or a fine of YER 300,000–1,000,000.
  - The law has no penalty aimed specifically at unlicensed payment activity, and it does not expressly reserve e-payment instruments to banks.
  - Source: [Law No. 40 of 2006, National Information Center text](http://www.yemen-nic.info/db/laws_ye/detail.php?ID=18872)
- Both central banks still cite Law 40/2006, Central Bank Law 14/2000 and AML Law 1/2010 as the legal basis for e-payment decisions. Examples: the CBY-Aden June 2024 wallet circular and the July 2025 decision. — [CBY-Aden circular CBY/L/282/2024](https://cby-ye.com/files/667c32dabd223.pdf); [Decision No. 4 of 2025](https://cby-ye.com/files/6875fc55a253e.pdf); [Al-Ain](https://al-ain.com/article/electronic-payment-systems-houthi-money-laundering)
- AML/CFT Law No. 1 of 2010 and its executive regulation (Republican Decree No. 226 of 2010) are the AML base. The 2023 e-money instructions define money laundering and terrorist financing by reference to Arts. 3–4 of Law 1/2010. — [e-money instructions p.3](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf); [CBY-Aden Circular 4/2025](https://cby-ye.com/files/690e0e39ce5d9.pdf)
- Exchange companies are governed by Law No. 19 of 1995 on Money Exchange [PRE-2023]. It requires a CBY licence and prior written CBY approval of articles before registration. — [Law 19/1995 text (YACPA copy)](https://yacpa.org/public/media/496/64d10bfd88673_القانون-رقم--19لسنة-1995م-بشأن-أعمال-الصرافة.pdf). Exchange companies may not take deposits by law, yet in practice many do [PRE-2023]. — [Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)

**Earlier unified framework: CBY Circular No. 11 of 2014 (bank-led mobile money) [PRE-2023]**
- Issued 11 Dec 2014 with support from USAID, the World Bank, CGAP and GIZ, before the split. Its terms:
  - Only banks may issue e-money; they may outsource to MNOs and agents but stay responsible for them.
  - E-money outstanding may not exceed 15% of the bank's paid-up capital plus statutory reserve without CBY approval.
  - 100% backing in a pooled trust account.
  - Limits of YER 30,000 per transaction and YER 50,000 per day; maximum balance YER 300,000.
  - Two-tier ID: national ID with national number above YER 50,000; alternative IDs below that.
  - Source: [Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf), citing [AFI, 7 Mar 2015](https://www.afi-global.org/opinion/central-bank-of-yemen-issues-new-mobile-banking-regulations/)
- CBY-Aden's 2023 instructions still treat banks as offering mobile e-money under Circular 11/2014 ("the bank licensed under Circular No. (11) of 2014 (for banks)"). Non-banks are covered by the 2023 instructions. — [e-money instructions §5(1)](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)
- Licences issued under Circular 11/2014:
  - Al-Kuraimi (2015), Tadhamon (2016), Al-Amal (2017), Yemen Kuwait Bank (2018).
  - CBY-Sana'a licensed CAC Bank (2018).
  - In Dec 2019, Al-Kuraimi's M-Floos held about 80% of e-money accounts. Other wallets then: CAC "Mobile Money", YKB "Floosak", Al-Amal "PYes", Tadhamon "Mahfathati" [PRE-2023].
  - Source: [Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)

**CBY-Aden: PSP / payment-system-operator instructions (Ref 720/CBY/2022, dated 14 Sept 2022; later instruments cite it as "issued 21/9/2022")**
- Drafted with World Bank and USAID technical support. Source for this whole block unless noted: [PSP instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf)
- **Definitions.** "Payment services" covers cash deposit and withdrawal; executing payment transactions linked to issuing or acquiring payment instruments; money transfer; payment initiation ("بدء سداد المدفوعات"); and any other electronic fund-transfer service. A "PSP" is a non-bank financial institution authorised by the CBY to provide these services, issue payment instruments, or manage them. A "payment instrument" includes internet, POS, mobile and stored-value methods.
- **Prohibitions:**
  - No person may act as a PSP or payment-system operator in Yemen without a valid CBY licence.
  - No one may register a company whose purpose includes payment services without prior written CBY approval.
  - No one may use the term "payment services" or a synonym in a name, printed material or trade address without a CBY licence or written approval.
- **Banks** need CBY approval, not a separate licence.
- **Exchange institutions** may not apply unless they convert entirely into a PSP.
- **Legal form and capital:**
  - Must be a joint-stock company.
  - Minimum paid-up capital is YER 500m for a PSP and YER 750m for a payment-system operator; the CBY may raise or lower these.
  - Press reports of 21 Sept 2022 gave the operator figure as YER 700m. The primary text reads "سبعمائة وخمسون مليون" (750m). — [Al-Sharae News, 21 Sept 2022](https://alsharaeanews.com/2022/09/21/63084/)
- **Licensable services include** (§4):
  - management of e-payment transfers;
  - **"management of collection and/or payment of electronic funds"** (closest analogue to an aggregator or collection service);
  - prepaid card issuing;
  - domestic and international personal remittances;
  - issuing payment instruments (except debit instruments);
  - e-money issuance and execution of debit and credit e-payments;
  - other services the CBY approves.
  - Each additional service needs its own approval.
- **Application file:**
  - founders' identities and CVs;
  - holders of 10% or more of capital, with five years of bank statements;
  - financials of corporate founders for three years;
  - source of funds;
  - feasibility study;
  - AML manual;
  - governance, BCP and disaster-recovery plans;
  - outsourcing arrangements;
  - settlement bank;
  - system description;
  - Arabic translations of foreign applicants' documents (so foreign financial institutions may apply);
  - non-refundable fees.
- **Process and timing:**
  - The CBY decides within 60 days of a complete file.
  - A preliminary licence lasts 6 months and can be extended by 6.
  - The final licence requires registration with the Ministry of Industry and Trade, full paid-up capital deposited at a commercial bank the CBY designates, and on-site readiness checks.
  - The final licence lasts 5 years; renewal must be filed 6 months before expiry.
  - Licences are non-transferable.
- **Outsourcing:**
  - Any function may be outsourced to a "third party" only with prior CBY approval.
  - Management, decision-making, internal control and risk functions may not be outsourced.
  - Contracts must cover confidentiality, audit, business continuity and termination rights.
  - Third parties are subject to CBY inspection.
  - Examples of outsourceable services: POS management, ATM management, connection to Visa/Mastercard, connection to other payment systems.
- **Cross-border payments** need written CBY approval.
- **Data:**
  - The main data centre must be in Yemen; hosting it abroad needs a written request, and the CBY may refuse.
  - A separate backup site must be CBY-approved.
  - Records must be kept for at least 10 years.
  - Customer confidentiality survives termination of the relationship.
- **Governance:** a board of at least 3 members, internal audit, an external auditor rotated at least every 2 years, and AML/CFT compliance.
- **Sanctions for breach:** warnings, suspension, fines with Governor approval, temporary closure or revocation. Unlicensed operators are ordered to stop.

**CBY-Aden: non-bank mobile e-money instructions (Ref 547/CBY/2023, signed 8 Aug 2023 / 21 Muharram 1445 by Governor Ahmed Ghaleb)**
Source for this whole block unless noted: [Instructions governing mobile e-money services by non-bank financial institutions](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)
- **Scope:** non-bank financial institutions already licensed as PSPs that want to issue mobile e-money. **A PSP licence must be obtained first.**
- **Definition of e-money:** monetary value stored electronically with the issuer that is:
  1. issued against receipt of funds of equal value;
  2. accepted as a means of payment by the parties dealing with it;
  3. transferable between parties and redeemable at par.
- **Trust account:**
  - Funds sit in a trust account (حساب النقود الإلكترونية المجمع) at a settlement bank. The account is owned by the e-money holders, not by the issuer.
  - It must equal 100% of e-money liabilities at all times.
  - No single settlement bank may hold trust balances above 30% of its total deposits.
  - Lending from the account, or pledging it, is prohibited.
  - Commissions go to a separate account; interest earned is segregated and reported.
  - Reconciliation is daily.
  - If the issuer fails, the settlement bank freezes the account and keeps it outside the estate.
- **Issuer rules:**
  - Issuer capital must be at least 25% of e-money outstanding at all times.
  - E-money has no expiry and pays no interest or incentives.
  - Prepaid airtime may not be used to issue e-money.
  - The issuer may not hold public funds directly.
  - Exclusivity agreements between parties are **not permitted** (§5(12)).
  - Prior CBY approval is needed before contracting or linking with other PSPs or licensed entities.
  - Systems must be interoperability-ready, supporting at least ISO 20022.
- **KYC and accounts:**
  - Activation requires a national-number ID or passport plus basic data.
  - At most 3 accounts per customer, and one mobile number per account.
  - Account opening uses an ID card or passport; a commercial register for legal entities; a local mobile number.
  - Records are kept 5 years.
- **Transfers to non-subscribers (two-tier ID):** above YER 150,000 needs a national-number ID (or passport for foreigners). Up to YER 150,000 accepts an ID without national number, family card, military card, social-security card, voter card or passport.
- **Original 2023 caps for individuals:**

  | Cap | YER |
  |---|---|
  | Per transaction | 400,000 |
  | Per day | 700,000 |
  | Per month | 2,000,000 |
  | Per year | 12,000,000 |
  | Maximum balance | 2,000,000 |

  Caps for legal-entity accounts are set by the issuer with prior written CBY approval.
- **Permitted services:**
  - opening accounts;
  - buying and selling e-money;
  - wallet-to-wallet and wallet-to-bank transfers;
  - transfers to non-subscribers;
  - bill payment;
  - **purchase of goods and services from merchants**;
  - receiving international remittances only via a commercial bank or licensed remittance company;
  - others the CBY approves.
  - Issuers may not offer currency exchange.
- **Agents:**
  - Master agents are financially strong companies; they may appoint sub-agents with issuer approval.
  - Ordinary agents are small businesses or individuals.
  - Agents may open accounts, buy and sell e-money (cash-in and cash-out) and handle complaints; other activities need CBY approval.
  - Ordinary agents may not sub-agent and may not charge extra fees.
  - An agent must be a Yemeni national (ID or commercial register), have a clean record, not appear on the bad-cheque blacklist in the past 3 years, have fixed premises, and hold an account at the settlement bank.
  - The issuer reports its agent list monthly and stays fully liable for its agents.
  - Agent-network management may be outsourced.
- **Dormancy:** an account may be frozen after 1 year of inactivity, closed after 3 years dormant, and treated as abandoned property after 1 more year (CBY notified).
- **Licensing steps:**
  - Application to the Governor listing shareholders of 5% or more, the location of the data platform and main servers, and the chosen settlement banks.
  - Review within 2 months.
  - Preliminary licence for 6 months, extendable by 6.
  - Final licence after verifying premises and the presence of main servers and platform, a penetration and vulnerability test certificate, the trust account, and procedures.
- **Reporting:**
  - audited accounts within 3 months of year-end;
  - quarterly income statements;
  - monthly statistics, including **person-to-merchant** payments, off-us interoperable transactions and trust-account balances;
  - monthly complaint counts.
- **Penalties:** warnings, fines, suspension or revocation. Unlicensed provision is stopped, with full liability.

**CBY-Aden: Decision No. 4 of 2025 (Ref 349/CBY/2025, 9 July 2025) amending the e-money instructions**
Source: [Decision No. 4 of 2025](https://cby-ye.com/files/6875fc55a253e.pdf)
- E-money issuers may now hold and transact in USD, SAR and AED as well as YER. **Conversion from YER into foreign currency is still prohibited.**
- **New individual caps:**

  | Cap | YER wallets | USD wallets (or SAR/AED equivalent) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Per transaction | 1,000,000 | 400 |
  | Per day | 2,000,000 | 500 |
  | Per month | 5,000,000 | 1,000 |
  | Per year | 50,000,000 | 10,000 |
  | Maximum balance | 8,000,000 | 1,500 |

- Legal-entity caps are still set by the issuer with prior written CBY approval.

**CBY-Aden: other instruments relevant to a platform**
- **e-KYC instructions (Ref 840/CBY/2023, 9 Nov 2023).** Source: [e-KYC instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/65806fe2b3757.pdf)
  - Apply to banks and to licensed mobile e-money PSPs. They do not apply to exchange companies.
  - Cover natural persons only.
  - Prior written CBY approval is needed to use e-KYC.
  - Tiers are risk-based: simplified e-KYC for low risk (sanctions-list screening, review every 4 years); standard e-KYC for medium risk (review every 2 years); enhanced due diligence for high risk (senior-management approval, source of wealth, annual review).
  - Electronic records and signatures created in e-onboarding are valid under Law 40/2006.
- **Financial-consumer-protection instructions No. 589, dated 20 Aug 2023.** Title and date only; I did not read the 37-page text. — [CBY-Aden supervision page](https://cby-ye.com/pages/14); [PDF](https://cby-ye.com/files/64e6f1e87e2a1.pdf)
- **Governor's Decision No. 23 of 2024 (26 June 2024).** Source: [CBY-Aden news 706](https://cby-ye.com/news/706)
  - New domestic cash transfers by exchange companies and transfer agents must go exclusively through the CBY-supervised unified network (UNMONEY).
  - Bank-owned networks could run until 30 July 2024; all other cash-transfer channels were prohibited.
  - **Licensed e-wallets and PSPs are exempt within their regulatory ceilings.**
  - Penalties include licence withdrawal, suspension and fines.
- **Circular CBY/L/282/2024 (26 June 2024)** bars banks and exchange companies from any direct or indirect dealing with 12 e-wallets and payment services it calls unlicensed, which operate from Sana'a, and with any other unlicensed wallet. Wallets named: Cash, Al-Dawli Money, Jawali, Floosak, Saba Cash, Mobile Money, Yemen Wallet, E-Rial wallet, Rial Mobile, Jaib, WeCash e-payment services, Al-Mutakamila. [CONTESTED: see Q2 on its later status.] — [Circular PDF](https://cby-ye.com/files/667c32dabd223.pdf); [CBY-Aden English notice, 26 Jun 2024](https://english.cby-ye.com/news/181)
- **Periodic Circular No. 4 of 2025 (2 Nov 2025): enhanced due diligence and international sanctions-list screening.** Applies to banks and exchange companies; details under Q3. — [Circular 4/2025](https://cby-ye.com/files/690e0e39ce5d9.pdf)
- **Periodic Circular No. 1 of 2026 (Ref CBY/L/258/2026, 15 Sept 2026)** prohibits dealing in virtual assets or cryptocurrencies and with VASPs and exchanges (Binance, Coinbase and others are named). It applies to banks, exchange companies, **payment service providers, payment-system operators and e-wallets** under CBY supervision, including their agents. — [Circular 1/2026](https://cby-ye.com/files/6aaa38b226baa.pdf)
- The CBY-Aden payments page also hosts ATM instructions and IBAN rules. — [CBY-Aden payments page](https://www.cby-ye.com/pages/11)

**CBY-Sana'a instruments [PRE-2023, CONTESTED]**
- **Early 2020:** CBY-Sana'a created a Payments System Department. — [Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)
- **Governor's Decision No. 1 of 2020** (dated 8 Mar 2020 per the Sana'a Center footnote; reported 19 Mar 2020) sets regulatory rules for financial institutions offering mobile e-payment services.
  - It has 14 articles; Art. 13 keeps Circular 11/2014 in force for banks.
  - It lets non-banks (money exchange companies, traders, non-financial institutions) obtain a CBY-Sana'a licence to issue e-money and manage agent networks.
  - It sets **no maximum on issuance**, overriding the 15% cap of Circular 11/2014.
  - It introduces prepaid cards and other instruments.
  - Sources: [Yemen Banks Association report](https://yemen-yba.com/7623/%D8%B5%D8%AF%D9%88%D8%B1-%D9%82%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%85%D8%AD%D8%A7%D9%81%D8%B8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D9%86%D9%83-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%B1%D9%83%D8%B2%D9%8A-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%B5%D9%86%D8%B9%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A8%D8%B4%D8%A3%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%B8%D9%8A%D9%85%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D9%84%D8%AA%D9%82%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%A4%D8%B3%D8%B3%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%8A%D8%A9-%D8%AE%D8%AF%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%81%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%83%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%88%D9%86%D9%8A-%D8%B9%D8%A8%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%87%D8%A7%D8%AA%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%85%D9%88%D9%84); [Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)
- CBY-Aden called this legal framework illegitimate in Jan 2020 and in a 21 Sept 2020 circular. — [Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)
- **Q1 2021:** CBY-Sana'a authorised WeNet (the brand of Yemen Financial Services Company, the bank-owned switch) to settle between banks and e-wallets. It licensed more e-rial providers, including IBY, SBYB, ONE and Tamkeen. — [Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)
- **2026:** the Houthi authorities run their own Fast Payment System through a privately owned operator. It works only in Houthi-controlled areas. — [Sana'a Center, Yemen Review Apr–Jun 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2026/28066)
- **Wallets in the Sana'a ecosystem:**
  - Jawali (WeCash) says it holds a central-bank licence issued mid-2022 without saying which bank. It reports about 1m subscribers.
  - A 2024 report by 26sep.net, a Sana'a outlet, counted 15 e-wallets in total.

- **2023:** the Houthi "anti-usury" law ended interest payments. IBY stopped paying depositors interest in March 2024. — [Sana'a Center, Yemen Review Apr–Jun 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2025/25056)

### Inferences
- In CBY-Aden areas, a non-bank cannot provide any "payment service" (including collection of electronic funds or payment initiation) without a CBY PSP licence. It also cannot call itself a payment service or register a company with that purpose.
- E-money (stored value redeemable and accepted by third parties) needs a second, e-money licence on top of the PSP licence.
- A platform that only provides software and never touches funds sits outside these definitions. It is still caught indirectly through the CBY's prior approval of the licensed partner's outsourcing and integration contracts, and through the onshore data-centre rule.
- In Aden areas, merchant payments ("purchase of goods and services from merchants") are an explicit, reportable e-money use case. Wallet-based checkout through a licensed wallet is squarely inside the rules.
- The 2025 caps (YER 1m per transaction, 2m per day; legal-entity caps negotiated with CBY approval) bound per-order ticket sizes for individual buyers.
- No-exclusivity (§5(12)) means a wallet partner cannot grant, or demand, exclusivity.
- The Sana'a regime is more permissive on paper: non-banks, exchange companies and traders can be issuers, and there is no issuance cap. Its content is poorly documented in public, and CBY-Aden does not recognise it.

## Key question 2: How has the split between the Aden and Sana'a central banks (including the 2024 dispute over bank headquarters and the unified money transfer network) changed which payment and transfer providers may operate where, and what does a platform serving both areas face?

### Takeaway
Since 2024, each central bank has used licensing as a weapon:
- CBY-Aden forced domestic cash transfers onto its UNMONEY network, declared 12 Sana'a-based wallets "unlicensed", and suspended Sana'a-headquartered banks.
- CBY-Sana'a banned UNMONEY, banned government-area banks, and later cut off Al-Kuraimi after it moved to Aden.
- A UN-brokered deal of 23 July 2024 froze the bank-sanction war.
- The March 2025 FTO designation then drove eight major banks to move headquarters to Aden, which triggered fresh Sana'a retaliation.

A platform serving both areas faces two non-recognising licensing authorities, two currencies at very different rates, wallets that may be "licensed" on one side and "unlicensed" on the other, and partners that can be cut off by either side at short notice.

### Inferences
- **Which provider is licensed where:**
  - Wallets and banks still headquartered in Sana'a (for example IBY, and the wallets named in the June 2024 circular) are, for CBY-Aden purposes, unlicensed or sanctions-exposed.
  - Banks that moved to Aden (Al-Kuraimi, Tadhamon, Shamil and others) are now CBY-Aden-supervised but face blocking or retaliation in Sana'a, as Al-Kuraimi did in June 2025.
  - No partner can be assumed lawful and operational on both sides simultaneously.
- **Domestic cash transfers:** in Aden-controlled areas, exchange-company domestic cash transfers must run over UNMONEY (Decision 23/2024), while licensed e-wallets and PSPs are exempt within their caps. A platform settling sellers in Aden areas should therefore use licensed wallets or banks rather than exchange-house hawala networks. In Sana'a areas UNMONEY is barred.
- **Practical requirements for serving both areas:**
  - separate rial pricing (old vs new notes, about 3x apart);
  - separate partner wallets and banks per zone;
  - separate merchant records per zone;
  - for any regulated role, separate approvals from each authority, which do not recognise each other;
  - written evidence of which authority licenses each partner.
- **Persistent risk:** the July 2024 truce stopped the escalation but did not settle licensing jurisdiction. The FTO-driven relocations (2025) and the retaliation against Al-Kuraimi show the risk persists into 2025–26.

- I could not determine whether a single wallet operates lawfully in both zones in 2026 (for example after relocation).

## Key question 3: What do US/UN/EU sanctions and designations (including the 2025 FTO designation of Ansarallah and OFAC actions against Yemeni banks or exchange companies) mean for a platform or its partners processing payments in Yemen, including diaspora and cross-border flows and foreign processors' willingness to serve Yemen?

### Takeaway
- Ansarallah has been an SDGT since 16 Feb 2024 and an FTO since 4 Mar 2025. Knowingly providing material support is a US crime, and financial institutions must freeze and report.
- OFAC has designated two Yemeni banks: Yemen Kuwait Bank (17 Jan 2025) and the International Bank of Yemen (17 Apr 2025). It has also designated Houthi-linked exchange houses and financiers, including the CBY-Sana'a governor (reported Dec 2024).
- The UN's 2140 asset freeze, which lists the Houthis as a group, runs to 14 Nov 2026. The EU implements it.
- Yemen has been on the FATF grey list since 2010.
- Practical consequences:
  - Partners must screen against UN, OFAC, EU and HMT lists; CBY-Aden made this mandatory in Nov 2025.
  - Non-US platforms face secondary-sanctions risk if they facilitate significant transactions for designated persons.
  - Global processors (Stripe, for example) do not serve Yemen. Western Union and MoneyGram cut off the sanctioned bank.
  - Personal remittances to non-blocked individuals stay authorised under OFAC GL 24A. Commercial flows through Houthi-controlled entities do not have that protection.

### Inferences
- **FTO effects on partners and platform:**
  - Any partner bank, wallet or exchange company headquartered in, or controlled from, Houthi areas risks being treated as acting for an SDGT/FTO, as IBY was.
  - A platform (especially one with US persons, USD flows, US cloud and app-store vendors, or US-linked investors) needs sanctions screening of sellers, partners and beneficial owners.
  - Revenue, fee and tax payments to Houthi authorities by the platform itself (Sana'a commercial licensing, taxes, levies) may raise material-support questions for US-nexus actors. This is a legal question for counsel and was not verified.
- **Diaspora and cross-border flows:**
  - GL 24A covers noncommercial personal remittances only.
  - Diaspora *purchases* from sellers are commercial flows with no general licence. They need the counterparties to be non-blocked, and they need correspondent banks willing to route to Yemen.
  - Under the PSP instructions, cross-border payment services need written CBY-Aden approval.
- **Banking partners:** the YKB and IBY designations, the Floosak/YKB link and the exchange-house designations argue for partnering with CBY-Aden-licensed, Aden-headquartered banks and wallets that run the screening Circular 4/2025 requires. The 50% ownership rule may reach wallets owned by designated banks.
- **Foreign processors:** the grey-list status, the FTO, the absence of Stripe and the Western Union/MoneyGram exits all indicate that foreign card acquirers and processors will generally not onboard Yemen-domiciled merchants. Card acceptance for diaspora buyers would likely need a foreign merchant entity with Yemen exposure accepted by its acquirer. That conclusion is inference; I found no processor policy statement.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the Treasury primary text for the Dec 2024 action (date and list) or for the 16 Jan 2026 action, so I could not confirm which Yemeni exchange companies were designated.
- I found no evidence on whether any Yemeni e-wallet itself is designated.
- I found no evidence of Visa or Mastercard scheme restrictions for Yemen.
- I found no 2025–26 EU autonomous measures on the Houthis.
- I found nothing on UK-specific measures beyond the HMT list reference.

## Key question 4: Can a non-bank platform legally hold sellers' funds, net fees, or offer a wallet or store credit in Yemen? Is there a payment-facilitator / aggregator concept, an agent regime, or a closed-loop exemption? What would a licence application involve? (Map of platform positions)

### Takeaway
- **CBY-Aden areas.** A non-bank that collects, holds or pays out other people's money electronically is providing a licensable "payment service": the PSP instructions expressly cover "management of collection and/or payment of electronic funds" and payment initiation. Issuing stored value redeemable across sellers is e-money, which needs a second licence.
- **No aggregator or payment-facilitator category.** There is no "payment facilitator" or sub-merchant category and no closed-loop or limited-network exemption in the texts I read.
- **Agents.** The agent regime covers agents *of e-money issuers* (cash-in/cash-out and onboarding) under the issuer's full liability, not agents of merchants.
- **Wallet fee deduction.** Wallets may deduct agreed fees from payment amounts. That supports a model where the licensed wallet nets the platform's fee rather than the platform holding funds.
- **CBY-Sana'a areas.** Decision 1/2020 lets non-banks, including traders, be licensed issuers. In practice that is a CBY-Sana'a licence, which CBY-Aden does not recognise and which carries US sanctions risk.

### Cited Findings
- **Licence needed to collect or pay electronic funds for others (Aden):**
  - "Payment services" includes money transfer, payment initiation and "other services operating to transfer funds electronically".
  - The services a licensed PSP may provide include "management of collection and/or payment of electronic funds".
  - Operating without a licence is prohibited, as is registering a company with that purpose or using "payment services" terminology.
  - Exchange companies cannot apply unless they convert fully.
  - Source: [PSP instructions §§1, 3, 4](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf)
- **Holding stored value (Aden):**
  - E-money is stored value issued at par, accepted as payment by parties and redeemable. Only PSP-licensed non-banks holding the e-money licence, or banks under Circular 11/2014, may issue it.
  - Customer funds must sit in a 100% trust account at a settlement bank owned by holders. The issuer may not hold public funds directly.
  - Capital must be at least 25% of e-money outstanding, with a YER 500m PSP minimum.
  - No interest may be paid. Redemption is at par on demand.
  - Source: [e-money instructions §§1, 5–6, 10–11](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf); [PSP instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf)
- **Fee netting:** "the user and the e-money issuer may agree to deduct fees and commissions from the payment order amount". Fees must be disclosed, with 2 weeks' notice to users and 14 days' prior notice to the CBY of changes. — [e-money instructions, Fees §(ي)](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)
- **Agents:** e-money issuers may appoint master and ordinary agents for account opening, cash-in/out and complaints only; anything else needs CBY approval. Agents must be Yemeni with fixed premises and a settlement-bank account, and the issuer remains fully liable. — [e-money instructions §§16–17](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)
- **Third parties and outsourcing:**
  - A PSP may outsource technical functions to a "qualified specialised third party" only with prior CBY approval. Contracts must include confidentiality, audit, BCP and termination rights. Third parties are subject to CBY inspection.
  - E-money issuers need prior CBY approval before contracting or linking with PSPs or other licensed parties.
  - Exclusivity is banned.
  - Sources: [PSP instructions §5](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf); [e-money instructions §5(11)–(12)](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)
- **Merchant payments:** "use of e-money to purchase goods and services from merchants" is an expressly permitted wallet service, and issuers report person-to-merchant payments monthly. Legal-entity (merchant) account caps are set by the issuer with prior written CBY approval. — [e-money instructions §§5(18), 15, 18](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf); [Decision 4/2025](https://cby-ye.com/files/6875fc55a253e.pdf)
- **Settlement-bank trust structure:** the settlement-bank contract must state that the trust account belongs to the issuer's customers, provide daily reconciliation, and ring-fence the account on insolvency. — [e-money instructions §17(b)](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)
- **Sana'a [PRE-2023, CONTESTED]:** the March 2020 decree lets money exchange companies, traders and non-financial institutions obtain CBY-Sana'a licences to issue e-money, contract third parties to manage agent networks and receive cash against e-rial balances, with no issuance cap. — [Sana'a Center 2022](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Rethinking_Yemens_Economy_No10_En.pdf)
- **Licence application (Aden).** Two stages: first a PSP licence (requirements under Q1), then an e-money licence (application to the Governor, 2-month review, preliminary licence for 6 months extendable by 6, final licence after server, pen-test and trust-account checks). — [PSP instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf); [e-money instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)
  - The PSP licence requires:
    - a joint-stock company with YER 500m paid-up capital deposited at a CBY-designated bank;
    - a fit-and-proper file for owners of 10% or more;
    - a feasibility study;
    - AML, governance, BCP and IT-security documentation;
    - a data centre in Yemen;
    - a settlement bank;
    - registration with the Ministry of Industry and Trade;
    - fees;
    - a CBY decision within 60 days;
    - a preliminary licence of 6 months, extendable by 6;
    - a final licence of 5 years.

### Inferences
#### Position-by-position map (CBY-Aden and CBY-Sana'a). Evidence as cited above; conclusions are inference unless marked otherwise.

**1. Record-only (sellers collect into their own wallet or bank accounts; the platform records outcomes)**
- **Aden:**
  - No CBY licence appears to be needed: the platform provides no "payment service" as defined, provided it does not initiate payments, hold funds or present itself as a payment service.
  - Under the PSP instructions it must not market itself with "payment services" wording.
  - Needs ordinary company and commercial registration and tax registration on its own subscription revenue (see Q5).
- **Sana'a:** the same logic applies. Commercial registration with Sana'a's Ministry of Economy, Industry and Trade, which in April 2026 revoked 4,225 commercial-agency licences, shows aggressive licensing practice. Whether a Sana'a e-payment rule reaches software-only platforms is unknown.
- **Sanctions:** avoid recording or settling via designated banks or wallets (IBY; YKB and possibly Floosak). Screen sellers.

**2. Technical service provider integrating providers on the seller's own merchant account**
- **Aden:**
  - The platform itself needs no licence, but it is a "third party" to a licensed PSP or e-money issuer.
  - The partner must get prior CBY approval for the outsourcing or integration.
  - The contract must give the CBY audit and inspection access, confidentiality, BCP and exit terms.
  - The partner's main data centre must be in Yemen, so platform components that process payment data may draw scrutiny if hosted abroad.
  - No exclusivity.
  - Payment-initiation features (pushing a debit request from the buyer's wallet) come close to the licensable "payment initiation" service. They should be run as the partner's service.
- **Sana'a:** partner-dependent. The partner holds a CBY-Sana'a licence that CBY-Aden does not recognise. Integrating the same software with wallets on both sides may be read by each side as dealing with the other's "unlicensed" entities (the June 2024 circular bars Aden-supervised *banks and exchangers*, not tech firms, from dealing with those wallets).

**3. Aggregator / sub-merchant under a licensed bank's or wallet's master account (platform is merchant of record and pays out sellers)**
- **Aden:**
  - There is no payment-facilitator category.
  - Receiving buyers' funds into the platform's own account or wallet and paying sellers is "management of collection and/or payment of electronic funds", a licensed PSP service (JSC, YER 500m).
  - The lawful alternative is for the licensed wallet or bank to provide the collection service, using its own legal-entity merchant accounts for each seller with CBY-approved caps and settling directly to sellers, while the platform acts as its CBY-approved technical third party.
  - Nothing found authorises a bank or wallet to let a non-licensed sub-merchant aggregator commingle third-party funds.
- **Sana'a:** Decision 1/2020 might allow a trader or non-bank to be licensed by CBY-Sana'a for e-money and payment services. That would be a CBY-Sana'a licence, contested by Aden and exposed to US sanctions risk.

**4. Commercial or collection agent**
- **Aden:**
  - The only agent regime found is *issuer agents* (cash-in/out, onboarding), appointed by and under the full liability of a licensed e-money issuer. They must be Yemeni, have fixed premises and a settlement-bank account, and may not charge extra fees.
  - A platform could become a wallet's master or ordinary agent, for example at pickup points.
  - Collecting buyer payments *on behalf of sellers* is not an agent activity listed in the instructions and needs CBY approval or a PSP licence.
  - Cash-on-delivery couriers that move collected cash to sellers in other cities may look like domestic cash transfers, which in Aden areas must go through UNMONEY when done by exchangers (Decision 23/2024). This is unclear for non-exchangers.
- **Sana'a:** the Decision 1/2020 framework lets licensed institutions contract third parties to manage agent networks. Its detailed agent rules were not found.

**5. Escrow or trust account at a bank**
- **Aden:**
  - I found no escrow statute or CBY escrow instrument.
  - A platform-owned bank account holding buyers' money pending delivery for third-party sellers is functionally "collection and payment of funds for others", which points to the PSP licence.
  - The safest form is a bank-provided escrow or trust product in which the bank holds and releases funds on contractual instructions from the platform. That is the bank's regulated service; its availability is unknown.
  - The trust-account mechanics in the e-money rules (holder-owned, 100% backed, ring-fenced, daily reconciled) show what the CBY expects for safeguarding.
- **Sana'a:** unknown.

**6. Holder of seller balances / issuer of stored value or e-money (open or closed loop)**
- **Aden:**
  - Holding sellers' balances or issuing buyer credit that is redeemable or usable across multiple sellers fits the e-money definition. It needs a PSP licence plus an e-money licence, a 100% trust account and capital of at least 25% of float.
  - There is no explicit closed-loop exemption. A single-merchant, non-redeemable store credit, for example a seller's own gift card usable only with that seller, arguably falls outside "accepted as a means of payment by the parties", but this has not been tested.
  - Platform-wide credit usable at many sellers is closer to e-money.
  - Interest or incentives on balances are prohibited for licensed issuers.
- **Sana'a:** a non-bank "trader" could seek a CBY-Sana'a e-money licence (no cap), but it would be contested by Aden and exposed to sanctions risk.

**Fee netting**
- The platform deducting its commission from funds it holds presupposes holding funds, as in positions 3, 5 and 6.
- The rules expressly let the *licensed wallet* deduct agreed fees from the payment amount. A split where the wallet remits the platform's commission is therefore the compliant path in Aden areas.
- Billing sellers separately for subscriptions and commissions avoids the issue entirely.

### Gaps
- I found no CBY guidance or enforcement on marketplaces, aggregators, payment facilitators or escrow. All mapping above is inference from definitions.
- I did not see the full CBY-Aden PSP instructions annexes (fees, forms, risk-management standards, pp. 23–33).
- I did not find whether any Yemeni bank offers escrow or third-party trust accounts to non-financial companies.
- I could not obtain Sana'a's detailed rules or any Sana'a licensing of tech platforms.
- I have no data on licence-application fees, CBY processing in practice, or whether foreign-owned joint-stock companies have actually been licensed.

## Key question 5: What tax obligations (general sales tax rates, digital-services rules, invoicing) and consumer-protection or e-commerce rules apply to platform-mediated sales? (Including data rules.)

### Takeaway
- **Sales tax.** The General Sales Tax (GST) Law No. 19 of 2001, amended by Law No. 42 of 2005, applies a 5% general rate to goods and services, with 10% on telecoms.
  - Registration is required from YER 50m annual turnover; importers register regardless of size.
  - Returns are monthly, due by the 21st, and full tax invoices are required.
  - Non-resident suppliers must appoint a local agent.
  - There are no specific digital-services or e-commerce tax rules.
- **Consumer protection and e-commerce.** The general Consumer Protection Law No. 46 of 2008 applies. There is no e-commerce law. Electronic contracting and payment validity rest on Law 40/2006. The CBY-Aden e-money and consumer-protection instructions govern payment-side consumer protection.
- **Data.** I found no comprehensive data-protection law. The only data rules found are sectoral: banking secrecy, an onshore data centre for PSPs, and record retention.
- **Split.** Tax administration is split: the Houthis levy additional taxes and charges, and customs valuation differs by zone.

### Cited Findings
- **GST statute:**
  - Law No. 19 of 2001 imposes GST on local sales and imports of goods and services. — [WTO accession copy of Law 19/2001](https://www.wto.org/english/thewto_e/acc_e/yem_e/WTACCYEM18A1_LEG_6.pdf); [Yemen NIC Law 19/2001](https://yemen-nic.info/db/laws_ye/detail.php?ID=11733)
  - Law No. 42 of 2005 (18 July 2005) amended it, setting the general rate at 5% [PRE-2023]. — [Yemen NIC Law 42/2005](http://www.yemen-nic.info/db/laws_ye/detail.php?ID=19343); [WTO copy](https://www.wto.org/english/thewto_e/acc_e/yem_e/WTACCYEM18A1_LEG_6.pdf)
  - Law No. 7 of 2020 (3 May 2020; government side) amended the GST law on cancelling tax assessments for certain periods. — [Yemen Tax Authority](https://tax.gov.ye/7-2020/)
- **Grant Thornton indirect-tax guide (1 Jan 2022) [PRE-2023]:**
  - Rates: standard 5%; telecoms and mobile 10%; exports zero-rated; some items exempt.
  - Registration: mandatory at YER 50m annual taxable turnover (taxable plus exempt sales); importers, exporters and distribution agents register regardless of size.
  - Non-resident suppliers without an establishment must appoint a local representative; otherwise the resident customer pays.
  - Unregistered recipients of imported services remit within 30 days.
  - "No specific legislation" exists for non-resident digital or electronic services.
  - Tax invoice (paper or electronic, serially numbered, in duplicate) must show seller and buyer names, addresses and registration or ID numbers, description, value, rate, tax and total.
  - Returns are monthly, due within 21 days.
  - Penalties: 2% per month (capped at 20%) for late filing or payment, plus 1% per month on assessed tax; doubled for repeat offences within a year.
  - No e-invoicing or real-time reporting.
  - Source: [Grant Thornton, Indirect tax – Yemen](https://www.grantthornton.global/en/insights/indirect-tax-guide/indirect-tax---Yemen/)
- **Sana'a side:** the Sana'a government approved draft amendments to the income-tax, GST and customs laws and sent them to its parliament. — [Yemen Eco](https://yemeneco.org/archives/57418) (undated in the search result; probably about 2023; content of the rates not found)
  - In 2026 the Houthis intensified taxation (state taxes, "war-effort" taxes, mandatory donations). — [Sana'a Center Apr–Jun 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2026/28066)
  - A Houthi security official oversees fee and tax collection from exchange and commercial companies (OFAC, 11 Sept 2025). — [US Treasury sb0243](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0243)
- **Government-side customs:**
  - Presidential Decree No. 11 (late Oct 2025) moved customs to the market rate (about YR1,630 per US$, from YR700) and required central revenue deposits at CBY-Aden. — [Sana'a Center Oct–Dec 2025](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/oct-dec-2025/26135)
  - In mid-May 2026 the customs dollar for non-essential goods was liberalised from YR750 to YR1,550. — [Sana'a Center Apr–Jun 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/april-june-2026/28066)
  - The 2026 state budget (March 2026) projects revenue of YR3.96tn, 41.5% from taxes. — [Sana'a Center Jan–Mar 2026](https://sanaacenter.org/the-yemen-review/jan-mar-2026/26962)
- **Consumer protection:** Law No. 46 of 2008 on Consumer Protection was issued on 7 Sept 2008 [PRE-2023]. It sets the general framework under the Ministry of Industry and Trade and a Supreme Committee for consumer protection. I found no e-commerce-specific provisions in what I saw. — [ESCWA Arab Legislative Portal](https://alp.unescwa.org/plans/1811); [WIPO Lex YE005](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/details.jsp?id=9551); [FAOLEX copy](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/yem221463.pdf)
- **Payment-side consumer protection (CBY-Aden e-money instructions, Aug 2023):**
  - written contract in plain language;
  - PIN;
  - instant SMS notification of each transaction;
  - payee-name confirmation before execution;
  - complaint handling within 10 working days, with escalation to the CBY;
  - fee disclosure, and fees shown before each transaction;
  - compensation plan.
  - Source: [e-money instructions §17(ط)–(ك)](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)
  - CBY-Aden financial-consumer-protection instructions No. 589 (20 Aug 2023) also exist; text not reviewed. — [CBY-Aden](https://cby-ye.com/pages/14)
- **Electronic contracting:** Law 40/2006 gives electronic contracts and signatures legal effect (Arts. 10, 13, 15) and makes electronic fund transfer an accepted means of payment (Art. 26). Art. 28 makes the bank liable for unauthorised debits after the customer notifies loss or breach. — [Law 40/2006](http://www.yemen-nic.info/db/laws_ye/detail.php?ID=18872)
- **Data:**
  - Law 40/2006 requires banking secrecy (Art. 27(2)).
  - PSPs must keep their main data centre in Yemen unless the CBY approves otherwise, maintain strict confidentiality that survives termination, and keep records for at least 10 years.
  - E-money issuers keep records for at least 5 years and must show their main servers at final licensing.
  - Sources: [Law 40/2006](http://www.yemen-nic.info/db/laws_ye/detail.php?ID=18872); [PSP instructions §§6, 12](https://cby-ye.com/files/632b0c240dc11.pdf); [e-money instructions](https://cby-ye.com/files/64dde85e0a84f.pdf)
  - UNCTAD's 2022 cyberlaw review listed Yemen among countries with a *draft* cybercrime law awaiting approval [PRE-2023]. — [UNCTAD, 2022](https://unctad.org/news/least-developed-countries-still-lag-behind-cyberlaw-reforms)

### Inferences
- **Sellers:** those above YER 50m turnover must register for GST, charge 5% (10% on telecom products) and issue compliant invoices. Most small sellers fall below the threshold.
- **The platform:** its own commission or subscription revenue is a taxable service at 5% once it passes the threshold. A foreign-domiciled platform with no Yemeni establishment would, under the general GST rules, need a local agent, or its Yemeni customers bear the tax. No digital-services regime has been found, so the position for foreign SaaS is legally unsettled.
- **Two tax authorities:** in practice sellers and platforms in Houthi areas face Sana'a's tax authorities and additional levies, and payments to them raise the sanctions questions noted in Q3.
- **E-commerce contracts:** consumer-protection exposure is under the general 2008 law and contract law. The platform's terms (seller as merchant of record, refunds, disclosures) are not dictated by a specific e-commerce statute. Payment-side disclosures are dictated by CBY rules for any licensed wallet partner.
- **Data:** the absence of a general data-protection law lowers formal privacy compliance burdens. CBY data-localisation expectations still apply wherever the platform touches a licensed partner's payment data.

### Gaps
- I did not verify current (2026) GST rates or thresholds on either side from a primary 2024–26 source; the most recent professional summary is from January 2022.
- I did not find whether Sana'a changed GST rates or introduced e-commerce or digital-services levies, or what zakat obligations attach to online sellers.
- I could not verify the YER 50m threshold against the text of the 2005 law or the executive regulation.
- I found no e-commerce law, e-commerce licensing activity code, or online-seller registration requirement on either side.
- The full text of Consumer Protection Law 46/2008 was not reviewed for distance-selling or returns provisions.
- I found no data-protection law or draft.
