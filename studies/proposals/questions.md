# The questions agents are given (draft, 18 October 2026)

**Status: draft, revised after the check of the drafts (`studies/proposals/check-of-the-draft.md`, findings 17 to 25). It is checked again before the founder's go.**

The founder's section B (`founder-messages/2026-10-18-g1-answer.md`) states what the founder wants Sharrwah to make possible. Its items are the questions the work answers. Agents get each one only as worded below, in neutral words. The founder's working words never become names or categories that agents record under.

**Rules for the wording:**
- **It asks what the platform says and does,** in its own words and terms. It doesn't say what Sharrwah wants, or what the answer should look like.
- **It fixes no level and no setter.** It never says where something is set (a store, a market, an account, a plan) or who sets it. The agent records what the platform shows, at the level and with the setter the platform names.
- **It uses no category of ours.** "Market", "availability", "conditions", "manual payment" and "parts" are the founder's working words. The questions use plain descriptions of what to look for instead, and name no platform's term either.
- **It names what to read, not what to find.** The brief gives each run its reading list. The question says what bears on it, so the run can tell what to record.

**How a run uses its question:**
- it records the statements in the platform's material that bear on the question and aren't already among the statements a script copied for it, in the platform's own words, scoped as the source scopes it. How far it reads, in what order, and when it stops are set by its brief;
- what it doesn't find in what it read, it records as "not in what was read", listing what it read. It records something as absent only from a complete list the platform publishes;
- it doesn't answer the question in its own words. The answer is the coordinator's proposal, later.

## Q1. Places a store sells in or operates from

The founder's item: B1, "one platform, set up per market".

> When a seller sells to, or operates from, more than one place (a country, a region, or however the platform names a place), what can differ between those places, and what stays the same? For each thing that can differ: what does the platform call it, and where is it set and by whom, as the platform shows it?
>
> What does the platform set up differently for sellers based in different places?
>
> What does the platform say adding a place involves, whether a seller adds one to sell in or the platform adds one where it operates? And adding a currency, or a payment provider?

## Q2. What a place's rules and the platform's own choices change for a seller there

The founder's item: B2, "rules land as availability, not as design".

> What does the platform offer, restrict, withhold, require or do differently because of a place's laws, sanctions or licensing, or because of its own choice of partners and providers? Record each, wherever it shows: in what the platform offers, in its terms, in which of its companies contracts with the seller, in how the product behaves, in where data is held, or anywhere else. Record the reasons the platform gives.

## Q3. What a seller must do or meet before doing particular things

The founder's item: B3, "conditions on what a seller can do".

> What must a seller do, provide or meet before they can do particular things? Examples are opening the store to buyers, taking payments, receiving payouts, or selling particular products. Record any such requirement on the seller or the seller's business, but not the permissions of the seller's staff or of apps.
>
> For each: what it requires, who sets it, as the platform shows it, when it is checked, and what the seller can and can't do while it isn't met.

## Q4. Orders whose payment doesn't pass through the platform

The founder's item: B4, "selling without Sharrwah touching the money".

> Can a seller take orders whose payment is arranged directly between seller and buyer, with no money passing through the platform or a payment service it provides? What does the platform call this? For such orders, what does the platform do and not do: recording the payment, the order's status, refunds, fees and reports? What limits apply?
>
> Record also any way of paying outside the checkout, such as paying on delivery, where the platform or a service it provides collects the money for the seller.

## Q5. Payment methods: which a seller can offer, who decides, and where the money goes

The founder's item: B5, "Sharrwah chooses which payment methods are offered".

> Who decides which payment methods a seller can offer buyers, and on what basis, as the platform shows it? Record each party the platform names as deciding, and what each decides.
>
> Through which kinds of providers, and through which kinds of add-ons to the platform (whatever it calls them), do payment methods come? What does the platform say happens when a provider or a method stops being available?
>
> Where does the money from each method go on its way to the seller, including when it goes straight to the seller?

**What this carries.** The last paragraph carries the pilot's deferred Decisions 1 to 13 into the work.
- **Decision 1,** who holds the money, is what the paragraph asks.
- **Decisions 2 to 12** mostly follow from Decision 1 in the pilot's entry: who settles, pays out and collects; agency; deductions; the merchant's accounts; payout timing; funds when the service ends; refunds; and a security interest.
- **Decision 13,** when the merchant is paid under installments, doesn't follow from Decision 1.
- **Decisions 2 to 13** are framed from the pilot's records. Q5's runs read anew for them only where Q5's proposal needs what those records lack.
- **Decision 14,** on currency, goes with Q7.

## Q6. Who can register as a seller, and from where

The founder's item: B6, "a seller anywhere can register".

> Who can register as a seller, from which places, and with what requirements? What can a seller do when they are based in, or sell from, a place where the platform, or part of what it offers, isn't available, and how does the platform say so?

## Q7. Currencies

The founder's item: B7, "currency".

> In what currency or currencies are a seller's prices held, shown to buyers, paid by buyers, and paid out to the seller? At what level does the platform set each, and what decides it? What can be set or changed later, by whom, and with what effect on what is already recorded? If a seller's money is held anywhere before it is paid out, in what currency?

**What this carries.** The founder expects one store currency to be the platforms' norm, and asks the work to confirm or correct that. The question doesn't say so, so as not to lead the agent.

The pilot's deferred Decision 14, on the currency a seller's money is held and paid out in, comes back with this question's proposal. Where the proposal frames it, it notes the founder's line in B7: the rial's two editions are not part of the core.

## Q8. How the platform organizes what it offers, and how that grows

The founder's item: B8, "built gradually, in parts".

> How does the platform organize what it offers, in its own names, and how does that grow? Record the units it names, whatever it calls them. For each: what it depends on and what it shares with others, how new ones are added or existing ones extended, and how the platform introduces them, for example by plan, by place or by release stage.

Much of this comes from the areas list (step R1) and what is recorded. A run records only what those don't show. If the founder takes the first cut in `sizing.md`, Q8 has no recording runs, and its proposal is drafted from the areas list and what is recorded.

## What isn't a question

- **The guard** on arrangements that hold sellers' money is a decision of the founder's (`founder-decisions.tsv`, G1). The money proposals say what the design provides for. Whether any such arrangement goes live is the founder's to decide.
- **Company decisions** are where the company is based, which providers and partners Sharrwah works with, any licence, and who may register. They aren't questions agents are given. The work collects the evidence that bears on each, and the coordinator brings each to the founder in plain words, with a recommendation and whether it needs a lawyer.
- **Areas no question covers,** such as tax, show on the areas list. The founder marks them, and each gets a line saying why the work leaves it out.
