# S-01, phase C: notes

Read for this phase: `briefs/C-analysis.md`; `variation-points.tsv` and `variants.tsv`; each platform's `statements.tsv`, `terms.tsv` and `absences.tsv`, all without any `note` column. No `notes*.md` file, no `note` column and no other file of the study was read. The gap rows' contents (`gaps.tsv`) are not on the brief's list, so a `gap` variant is read only as "this part is not answered".

## Step 0: what the subject is about

> Who holds the merchant's funds between the buyer's payment and the merchant's payout?

**The product.** The question asks which party, inside a platform's own flow of funds, holds the money between the buyer's payment and the payout: a balance, a wallet, a processor's or acquirer's account, a BNPL provider, a carrier. Each reference answers it in its own payment documentation, terms and admin. It does not ask what a market requires: laws and regulators appear in some answers, and step 3 tests them as causes. It does not ask about the company either (Sharrwah's people, money, licences or choices). So nothing in step 0 goes to the founder as a company question.

## How the brief's rules were applied

These readings are mine where the brief leaves room. Each one is also listed under "Problems with the method" below.

- **Answer.** A variant's answer is the who or what its excerpt puts in the variation point's role: a party, an account, a balance, an agreement, an amount or a schedule. A platform's set of answers is the answers of its variants that are not `gap`. An `absent` variant is an answer: it rests on a complete list.
- **Same.** I count two answers as the same only when they use the same words, or one is the platform's own translation of the other in `terms.tsv`, or they name the same party or identifier (an amount or a currency code counts as an identifier). "The merchant" (you, your bank account) is the same party everywhere. Salla and Zid are different parties, and so are Stripe and PayPal.
- **Gaps.** When a platform has a `gap` variant, its set is incomplete. A difference counts as shown only when one platform has an answer that the other's set lacks *and* that other platform has no gap. Agreement is never shown while either side has a gap.
- **Like with like.** Declarations (`stated`, `official page`, `shipped`) are compared with declarations, and `observed` with `observed`. A variant whose `kind` holds both counts in both classes.
- **Scopes.** I count two scopes as overlapping when they name the same country, or when at least one names no country. A scope naming only a product, provider, plan or order type (for example "تمارا", "WooPayments", "باقة بلس") names no country, so I treat it as general for this test.
- **Test 2 ("names a law or a regulator's rule").** I counted a statement only when it names a particular law (Retail Payment Activities Act; Personal Property Securities Act 2009 (Cth)) or a particular regulator together with its rule, restriction, authority or authorisation (the Saudi Central Bank and the Ministry of Commerce and Investment; the Bank of Canada; the Central Bank of Ireland; the U.K. Financial Conduct Authority). The statement must also present it as the reason for the variant or its condition. Generic wording did not count: "Law", "applicable law", "to the extent permitted by law", "legally required", "Legal Process", "financial regulations", "a regulatory authority", "the laws of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia", "U.S. Law", or a country's unnamed "requirements" (S01-SH-065).
- **Test 3.** Of the eleven statements with a `marker`, only S01-BC-059 (`beta`) is cited by a variant, V-567 in VP-13.
- **Coexistence letters.** The brief allows one letter per platform, but many platforms bind their variants in two ways at once: some per market, store or plan, and several of them together in one store. I gave the letter for the binding that governs most of the platform's variants on that VP, citing the statements that show it, and wrote the other binding in the `note` column. `B` needs a statement that ties the variants to the market, store settings or plan. `A` needs a statement that shows two or more of them in force in one store. I read `C` ("one per product") as one per catalog product, and no variant was bound that way. `D` needs a statement saying the platform offers only one, or an `absent` variant, and none qualified. I gave `unknown` in three cases: the platform's only answer is a single variant or restatements of one answer; it has only a gap; or the statements do not show how its variants bind (for example, Salla's تابي and تمارا on VP-22).
- **Expensive to change.** I filled `expensive_because` on every row, including rows whose cause selects no expensive/cheap rule (market causes, no difference), so that every VP has the same columns.

## Causes recorded as `unknown`, with why

- **S01-VP-07 (who or what fills "Reserve").** Salla's only variant is a gap (V-134), and so is BigCommerce's (V-135). So neither Salla against Zid nor Shopify against BigCommerce can be compared. Test 4 cannot show that the four hosted platforms agree, and test 5 cannot use Salla. No statement cited for the VP names a law or regulator, and none carries a marker.
- **S01-VP-10 (who or what fills "security interest").** Zid's only variant is a gap (V-171), and so is BigCommerce's (V-172). Salla has a gap (V-170) beside its one answer (V-550). So neither pair can be compared, and tests 4 and 5 cannot be shown. No named law and no marker.
- **S01-VP-21 (who or what fills "refunded to your customers").** Salla's answer (V-504) and Zid's known answers (V-505 to V-508, V-510) name the same party, the buyer, returned to as "the source". Zid also has a gap (V-509), so the two can be shown neither to agree nor to differ. BigCommerce's only variant is a gap (V-511), so Shopify against BigCommerce cannot be compared either. The legal wording cited (S01-SH-106 "applicable law and Network Rules", S01-WC-010 "regulatory authority", S01-ZD-014 "الجهات المعنية") names no particular law or regulator.

## Problems with the method

1. **Same-ness has no notion of role.** Salla and Zid often each name themselves in the same role (their own balance, their own payouts). Under the brief's rule these are different parties, so the two differ, and 14 of 23 VPs end at "vendor choice". If sameness were judged by role (for example, "the platform's own balance"), several of these would read differently. I applied the rule as written.
2. **"Names a law or a regulator's rule" is not defined.** Under my reading, test 2 fires for VP-08, VP-12, VP-17 and VP-18 only. A looser reading that also counted generic legal wording could move VP-01 (S01-WC-116 "as required by Law"), VP-09 and VP-19 (S01-SH-024, S01-SH-134), VP-13 (S01-ZD-004 "قوانين المملكة العربية السعودية"), VP-14 (S01-SH-065 "requirements" in Japan), and VP-20 and VP-21 (S01-SA-082 under the article on KSA laws; S01-WC-010 "regulatory authority").
3. **One marked statement decides VP-13.** The cause there is `maturity` because V-567 cites S01-BC-059 (`beta`). The difference itself is about which agreement governs the merchant's payments, not about a release stage.
4. **Test 1 and "doesn't" answers.** On VP-05, every excerpt names the merchant as the one paid directly, except V-105. V-105 names WooPayments, but only to say it has no part in BACS. I read it as naming no other holder, so VP-05 is `no difference`. Read strictly, V-105 would break test 1, and the VP would go on to the later tests.
5. **Strict set equality hides shared answers.** On VP-16, Salla and Zid name the same minimums for Saudi Arabia (100 riyals) and Kuwait (1 dinar). Their sets still differ through Zid's مزيد minimum and Salla's move of small amounts to the wallet. The rule therefore reads "vendor choice".
6. **One letter per platform** cannot carry a platform that binds some variants per market and runs several in one store. The second binding is only in `note`, and the `follows` rows are built from letters only.
7. **`general_variant` holds one id per VP.** On VP-14, three platforms each have a general, configurable variant (Salla V-292, Shopify V-286, WooCommerce V-276).
8. **"Product" in letter C** can mean a catalog item or a payment product (WooPayments, Salla Payments). I took the first.
9. **Scopes without a country** (a provider, a plan, an order type) are not covered by "the same country, or general". I treated them as general.
10. **Gap contents are outside the reading list.** I could not check whether a gap could hold another platform's answer, so I assumed it could.
11. **Market causes have no expensive/cheap row.** For VP-08, VP-12, VP-17 and VP-18, `follows` comes from letters alone, and no row asks for a decision.
