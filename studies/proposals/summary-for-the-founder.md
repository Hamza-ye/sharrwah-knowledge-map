## The steps, and what each gives

1. **The areas list.**
   - For each platform, one short run lists its broad areas in its own names, such as "Payments" or "Taxes and duties". A script checks that every name really is the platform's.
   - I merge the five lists into a few tens of areas, each with a plain line.
   - With the list you get:
     - what marking an area "won't do" removes from the work;
     - what evidence your eight items keep anyway;
     - what the work leaves out, and why;
     - for each item, which platforms I read in depth, and which only for anything different.
   - An independent agent checks the list. Then **you mark** what Sharrwah won't do and what it must. Anything unmarked stays in.
2. **The sample.** While you mark, one short run records one of your items on one platform, picked by a fixed rule as a typical run. It measures the cost per new statement. If that isn't under your bar, the work stops and comes to you.
3. **Recording.** About 40 short runs, each one item on one platform.
   - A script first copies what's already recorded, unchanged.
   - Each run reads only what's still missing, and ends with a short account of what its platform shows.
4. **Drafting the proposals.** One run per item drafts from those accounts, and I finish each. Each proposal gives:
   - how the platforms handle it, in their own terms;
   - Sharrwah's proposed names, structure and behaviour;
   - what can plug in, what is set per what, and who sets it;
   - the parts it can be built in, in order;
   - what the design must keep open;
   - what Yemen's evidence allows, forbids or doesn't show.

   The company decisions are collected along the way.
5. **Checks.**
   - Each fact a proposal rests on is re-checked against its source.
   - Anything "not found" that a proposal rests on gets a full search, and a second agent's audit.
   - Each Yemen fact used is re-checked against its quotes.
   - A script checks every quote.
6. **Review, then you.**
   - An agent that did none of the work reviews the proposals and the company decisions.
   - One short run checks that the proposals don't conflict with each other, for example on currency, payment methods and payouts.
   - Then they reach you in one message, each with its own answer line. Each company decision comes with my recommendation and whether it needs a lawyer.

## The rulings that still shape the result

You may overturn any of them.
- **"Absent" needs a complete list** that the platform publishes. Otherwise it's "not in what was read", with what was read and the search behind it.
- **Quotes are exact,** from a fixed version of each source, and a script checks every one.
- **Kinds of source stay apart:** a real store, the platform's code, its help pages and its legal pages. Like is compared with like.
- **A statement answers a question only if its own words name who or what does the thing,** not because it shares a word with the question.
- **The seller, the buyer and the platform itself** count as the same party on every platform.
- **A statement that a party has no part** names no one, but shows that party isn't involved.
- **The money's path ends at the payout to the seller's own bank account.** A wallet counts only where its words say it holds sales money.
- **For Yemen:**
  - the seller sells in the Yemeni market;
  - each fact is read with its date;
  - only a rule stated for all of Yemen overrides rules for places within it;
  - a rule only proposed is never taken as today's.
- **Agreements a platform's terms take in,** such as Stripe's under Shopify Payments, count as that platform's terms.
- **What we already hold is read before anything is asked of you.** A page read new is saved at a fixed version before it is quoted.
- **Agents' effort is set by step:** the highest for checks, reviews and drafting; high for the area runs and re-checks; medium for recording, re-run higher where a check shows gaps.
