## What the terms pass found

Six words in M1's records are narrower or vaguer than the platforms show:
- **Payment method.** The platforms have three kinds: paid through a provider; paid to the seller directly and recorded by hand (bank transfer, cash on delivery, money order); and value the seller already owes, spent at checkout (gift cards, store credit, a customer's wallet). D-001's "each payment method's provider holds the money" names only the first.
- **Cash on delivery.** On Salla, carriers outside Salla's own labels settle with the seller directly, which is option C. Cash collected through Salla's own labels lands in Salla's balance, which is option A. So cash collected through Sharrwah's own labels into a Sharrwah balance would be A, which your line keeps off at launch.
- **Fees.** There are three kinds: the platform's own charge on sales (Shopify charges one when another provider is used), the payment service's processing fees, and a courier's fee for cash on delivery. D-002 gives "fees" to payments without saying which.
- **What the seller is shown.** D-004 decides that each provider's payout time is shown per method. Shopify shows no payout information for providers other than its own. Showing it is a product choice for S-07, not a money decision.
- **The seller's rules on methods.** Salla's can also reject a payment or put the order under review. D-002 covers hiding, ordering and renaming only.
- **Store credit.** D-003 puts it under the guard on money. The platforms show it as value the seller owes and credits, with no money held. A wallet buyers pay money into would be a decision for you, at S-05.

And one fact. D-003 rests a driver on the seam note's claim that every Shopify balance-transaction type has "a reversal twin". Shopify's schema shows 38 reversals among 114 types (`admin-graphql-schema/2026-10/items.jsonl:3240`). The decision stands on its other evidence. The sentence is corrected before D-003 stands, whichever option you choose.
