Obligation to Payout

How Shopify models payments, read at the level of the question below rather than as a list of payment methods. It gives the Knowledge Map a revised set of PAY questions, each tied to the evidence observed-sh already holds, and tests the model against Yemen's arrangements.

The organising question

What obligations can commerce create, what arrangements can satisfy them, how are authorization and financial outcomes established, and how are those outcomes reconciled with the order, the parties, and the eventual movement of money?

The model in eight lines

  1. Roles, not companies. An order can name three different sellers, and one organization can be platform, credential holder and processor at once.
  2. Agreements create obligations. Every order, edit, refund and return appends an agreement; what is still owed is one signed number, and its timing lives in separate terms and schedules.
  3. Arrangements are points in a space. Under eight overlapping vendor classifications sit a few real dimensions: who acquires the proof, who decides, how fast, whether funds can be reserved, how it reverses, who holds the money, who carries the loss.
  4. Each try is an attempt with an identity. One idempotency key that never expires, a small closed set of states, deadlines on every waiting state, and a re-check before the irreversible step.
  5. Outcomes come from more than processors. Providers, the platform's own stored-value ledgers and people (staff marking paid, a cashier counting a drawer) all establish outcomes, each recorded differently.
  6. The order keeps four records apart. What was agreed, what was attempted, what money moved and what falls due; the payment status is derived, never stored on its own.
  7. Settlement is a ledger of its own. Whoever holds the money keeps signed entries of gross, fee and net, with a reversal type for each correction, and nets fees, reserves and debts before paying out.
  8. The shape is fixed; the values vary. Providers plug into one contract, merchants shape the offer through one Function, and each market is the same template with different numbers.

Frame the question by setup option, not by provider

A setup option is one configuration of roles over a rail in one authority zone: who captures the buyer's proof, who confirms the payment, who holds the money and for how long, how it reaches the seller, and where Sharrwah stands. One provider can host several. Al-Kuraimi alone supports four: transfer plus screenshot, its payment API on the seller's own account, Sharrwah collecting into its own account, or bank-held accounts per seller. Each carries a different licence consequence, and a wallet licensed in Sana'a counts as unlicensed in Aden. Providers come and go; the options stay.

For each authority zone Z of a market at date T: which setup options can discharge a buyer's obligation to a seller? A setup option is a rail together with who acquires the buyer's proof, who establishes the outcome, who holds the money and for how long, how it reaches the seller, and which position Sharrwah occupies. For each option, what does it allow (reserving, reversing, currencies, limits, timing) and what does it require (licence, partner, seller onboarding, sanctions screening)? Which options can Sharrwah support from its current position, which next, and what evidence or event moves it up a position, or forces it back down?

In working form the question has four steps: list every combination of rail, role assignment and Sharrwah position in each zone; describe each combination on the fixed dimensions; mark which combinations are lawful and available from Sharrwah's current position; and name the trigger and precondition for every move between positions.

What the model can settle before the answers

The answers decide which positions are switched on, in which zone and on which rail. They do not decide the model's shape, as long as position, outcome source, custodian, zone, currency edition and initiation style are data rather than assumptions. Two answers can still change architecture rather than configuration: PAY-44 (which entity contracts and what US nexus it carries) and PAY-55 (whether the onshore data rule reaches Sharrwah's payment components). Those two belong first in the queue.
